Use this page to calibrate a rig with several cameras, several IMUs, or both.
Multi-sensor rigs are the case MVIS is built for.

- **One graph:** every camera and every IMU enters one factor graph (one joint
  problem solved together).
- **No separate stereo step** and no pairwise composition.
- **Correlations kept:** links between the sensors are kept rather than discarded.

## Several cameras

Add one entry per camera and choose the reference:

```yaml title="config.yaml"
cameras:
  - camera_id: 0
    source_type: rosbag_topic
    source_path: /data/rig.bag
    topic: /cam0/image_raw
    distortion_model: kb4
  - camera_id: 1
    source_type: rosbag_topic
    source_path: /data/rig.bag
    topic: /cam1/image_raw
    distortion_model: kb4
  - camera_id: 2
    source_type: rosbag_topic
    source_path: /data/rig.bag
    topic: /cam2/image_raw
    distortion_model: radtan       # models may be mixed
    shutter_type: rolling

reference_camera: 0
optimize_time_offset: true         # for unsynchronised cameras
```

- **Extrinsics** (the pose of each camera on the rig) are expressed against
  `reference_camera`.
- **Camera-to-camera transforms** follow by composition through the graph.
  This is what keeps a five-camera rig consistent.

**Cross-FOV rigs** put a fisheye and a narrow lens on the same rig. Here
time-offset estimation usually fails. For this case MVIS runs two independent
estimators:

- **PnP-based:** from the board pose seen in each camera
- **M5 homography cross-correlation**

## Several IMUs

The first entry in `imu_topics` is the **base IMU**:

- **Reference clock** for the whole rig
- **The only IMU that carries the trajectory**
- Every other IMU is **auxiliary**, related to it by a rigid transform and a
  time offset.

```yaml title="cam_imu.yaml"
input:
  imu_topics: ["/imu0", "/imu1", "/t265/imu"]
  imus:
    - {}                       # base — needs the accel
    - {}
    - {gyro_only: true}        # aux with an untrustworthy accel
```

Choose the best sensor as base: highest rate, lowest noise. Its errors
propagate to the whole rig.

`gyro_only` is set per IMU and is not permitted on the base. What it does and
when to prefer it over `estimate_t_ga`: [IMU model](/concepts/imu-model/).

## Why redundancy helps

Several IMUs observe the *same* motion. That redundancy constrains parameters a
single IMU leaves weak:

- **Time offsets**
- **Readout** (the rolling-shutter readout time)
- **IMU intrinsics** (scale, misalignment and similar sensor errors)

This is the reason to carry the extra sensors at all. It also makes the
[round-trip check](/guides/roundtrip/) meaningful on these rigs. A wrong
calibration usually cannot satisfy every IMU at once.

## Sensor numbering when comparing with Kalibr

MVIS numbers sensors by role; Kalibr numbers them by input order. Getting this
wrong silently compares the wrong pair. The tables below map the released rigs.

**4 IMUs + 3 cameras**

| Sensor | BlackFly | T265 L | T265 R | GX3-25 | GX3-35 | Xsens MTI-100 | T265 IMU |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kalibr | cam0 | cam1 | cam2 | imu0 | imu1 | imu2 | imu3 |
| MVIS | Cam2 | Cam0 | Cam1 | Ib | Is0 | Is1 | Is2 |

**4 IMUs + 4 cameras**

| Sensor | T265 L | T265 R | ELP L | ELP R | GX3-25 | GX3-35 | Xsens | T265 IMU |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kalibr (T265) | cam0 | cam1 | — | — | imu0 | imu1 | imu2 | imu3 |
| Kalibr (ELP) | — | — | cam0 | cam1 | imu0 | imu1 | imu2 | imu3 |
| MVIS | Cam0 | Cam1 | Cam2 | Cam3 | Ib | Is0 | Is1 | Is2 |

MVIS reads and writes Kalibr `camchain` and `camchain-imucam` files
directly. See [Result formats](/reference/result-formats/).
