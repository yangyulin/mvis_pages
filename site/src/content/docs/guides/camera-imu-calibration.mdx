Use this page to calibrate a camera together with an IMU (inertial measurement
unit: gyroscope plus accelerometer). You need a bag recorded with a calibration
board in view.

In MVIS this is a **two-step** workflow:

1. Calibrate the cameras first.
2. Feed that result into the cam-IMU solve.

We pin the camera intrinsics (focal length, centre, distortion) that the board
sequence already constrained well. That keeps them from being traded against
the lever arm (the offset from the IMU to the camera).

## 1. Calibrate the cameras

Run a camera-only calibration on a board sequence
([Camera calibration](/guides/camera-calibration/)):

```bash
mvis calibrate -c mvis_cam0_ds.yaml
```

Its `output_file` becomes the cam-IMU config's `camera_input.path`.

## 2. Write the cam-IMU config

```yaml title="cam_imu.yaml"
input:
  format: ros1                  # ros1 | ros2 | euroc
  path: /data/dataset-calib-imu1.bag
  imu_topics: ["/imu0"]         # first entry is the base IMU
  window_mode: manual
  t0: 3.0
  window: 45.0
  cache_dir: /tmp/mvis_detcache

board: {board_type: apriltag, rows: 6, cols: 6, tag_size: 0.088, tag_spacing: 0.3, tag_family: t36h11}

camera_input:
  path: /tmp/calib_ds_cam0.yaml   # from step 1
  free_intrinsics: false
  estimate_readout: false

solve:
  state_hz: 30.0
  max_iterations: 40
  imu_intrinsics: dw_da
  imu_intrinsics_model: kalibr    # kalibr | rpng | calyx
  corner_subsample: top_left

output:
  path: /tmp/cam_imu.json
```

## 3. Run

```bash
mvis cam-imu -c cam_imu.yaml
```

The solve estimates:

- **Extrinsics** `T_CtoI`: the camera → IMU transform
- **Time offset** `td`: the clock delay between camera and IMU
- **Rolling-shutter readout**: the time a rolling-shutter camera takes to read one frame
- **IMU intrinsics**: such as scale and misalignment of the gyroscope and accelerometer
- **Biases** of the gyroscope and accelerometer

It works for single or multi cam/IMU rigs, and for all six distortion models.

## `state_hz` is the knob that matters

Pose, velocity, and bias state nodes are placed at `state_hz`. It **defaults to
30 Hz**. A study across MIC, TUM-VI raw, and T265 found:

| Case | Effect of `state_hz` |
|---|---|
| **Dynamic-motion rigs** | Reprojection error, lever arm, and `td` all **alias at low rates** (fast motion is undersampled) and plateau around 30 Hz |
| **Gentle calibration sequences** | Flat: the rate does not matter |
| **Stereo baseline, IMU intrinsics/biases** | **Rate-invariant** either way |

So:

- **Leave it at 30** unless you are on a gentle sequence and want the speed.
- **Suspect it first** if `td` or the lever arm look wrong on a dynamic rig.

## Batch: a whole dataset family from one config

To calibrate many sequences at once, list them under `input.bags` instead of a
single `path`:

```yaml title="cam_imu_batch.yaml"
input:
  format: ros1
  bags:
    - /data/seq01.bag
    - /data/seq02.bag
    - /data/seq03.bag
```

- **Output:** each is calibrated into a discoverable `bag<NN>/cam_imu.json`.
- **Formats:** ROS1 bags and EuRoC `mav0` directories both work. Each inherits the config's format.
- **Use:** this is how the repeatability study is run.

The spread of extrinsics and intrinsics *across* sequences is a far better
accuracy signal than any single run's residual.

## Awkward IMUs

Two options exist for accelerometers you cannot trust.

**Gyro-only**: calibrate an auxiliary IMU from its gyro alone.

- **Solved:** extrinsic rotation, `td`, `Dw`, gyro bias (`Dw` is the gyro scale and misalignment)
- **Pinned:** lever, `Da`, `Tg` (`Da` is the accel scale and misalignment; `Tg` is how linear acceleration leaks into the gyro)

```yaml title="cam_imu.yaml"
input:
  imus:
    - {}                     # base IMU — needs the accel
    - {gyro_only: true}      # aux
```

It is not allowed on the base IMU. The base IMU needs the accelerometer for
gravity and metric scale.

**Estimate `t_ga`**: solve the base IMU's *internal* gyro-vs-accel timing
offset (the delay between its gyroscope and its accelerometer):

```yaml title="cam_imu.yaml"
solve:
  estimate_t_ga: true
```

Both target the same failure. A driver that interpolates the accelerometer to
the gyro rate injects a timing offset, and that offset corrupts `td`.

The T265 is the canonical case:

- **IMU:** Bosch BMI055, 62.5 Hz accel interpolated to 200 Hz
- **Measured:** `t_ga ≈ −6.2 ms`, stable to ~0.2 ms across bags

Details in [IMU model](/concepts/imu-model/).

## 4. Validate

Do not stop at the residual:

```bash
mvis roundtrip -c cam_imu.yaml
```

[Round-trip validation](/guides/roundtrip/) applies the calibration to its own
bag and recalibrates. It is the check that catches a good-looking, wrong answer.
