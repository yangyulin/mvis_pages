Use this page to solve cameras and IMUs in one graph. You need the recording
from [step 2](/guides/calibration-motion/) and the camera YAML from
[step 3](/guides/camera-calibration/). You get `cam_imu.json` and a calibration
YAML.

The running example is recording 01 of our VI-Rig with 4 IMUs and 4 cameras
([dataset page](/datasets/virig-4imus-4cams/)):

- **Cameras:** a T265 fisheye pair (global shutter) and an ELP stereo pair (rolling shutter).
- **IMUs:** Xsens MTi-100, MicroStrain GX3-25 and GX3-35, and the T265 IMU.

The solve estimates, all at once:

- **Extrinsics:** where each camera and IMU sits relative to the base IMU (lever arm and rotation).
- **Time offset `td`:** the clock delay of each camera and each extra IMU to the base IMU.
- **IMU intrinsics:** scale and misalignment of each gyroscope (`Dw`) and accelerometer (`Da`).
- **`t_ga`:** the delay between an IMU's own gyroscope and accelerometer samples.
- **Camera intrinsics:** refined from the step 3 result, not held fixed.
- **Biases** of every gyroscope and accelerometer.

## 1. Point at the camera prior

Step 3 ([Camera-only calibration](/guides/camera-calibration/)) writes a camera
YAML. Name it in the cam-IMU config:

```yaml title="virig_4imus_4cams_cam_imu.yaml"
camera_input:
  path: camera_prior.yaml
```

- **What it gives:** the starting intrinsics of each camera (the prior).
- **One file, in `cam_topics` order:** join the T265 and ELP results of step 3.
  MVIS sorts the cameras by `id` and matches them to `cam_topics` by order. So
  here the T265 fisheyes are `id` 0 and 1, the ELP left and right 2 and 3.
- **Our example's prior:** [camera_prior.yaml](/media/guides/virig-4imus-4cams/camera_prior.yaml).
  It joins earlier camera-only results and uses `equidistant` for the T265,
  which gives the same result as `kb4` on this lens
  ([step 3](/guides/camera-calibration/#3-choose-a-fisheye-model-for-the-t265)).
  So the report lists the T265 cameras as `equidistant`.
- **What happens next:** the cam-IMU solve refines the intrinsics (`estimate.camera.intrinsics: true`). It solves the extrinsics and time offsets itself; the prior's extrinsics are not used.

## 2. Write the config

This is the config of the [VI-Rig 4 IMUs + 4 cameras example](/benchmark/examples/virig-4imus-4cams/),
recording 01. The highlighted lines matter for this step.

```yaml title="virig_4imus_4cams_cam_imu.yaml" {2-3,9-15,23,25,35-42,44,46,48-49,51-54}
input:
  format: ros1
  path: 4imus_4cams_01.bag
  cam_topics:
  - /t265/fisheye1/image_raw
  - /t265/fisheye2/image_raw
  - /elp/split_sync_image_node/left/image_raw
  - /elp/split_sync_image_node/right/image_raw
  imu_topics:
  - /imu/data
  - /gx3_25/data
  - /gx3_35/imu/data
  - /t265/imu
  window_mode: motion
  segment_length: 80.0
board:
  board_type: apriltag
  rows: 6
  cols: 6
  tag_size: 0.088
  tag_spacing: 0.3
  tag_family: t36h11
  heights_file: board_shape_4imus_4cams.yaml
camera_input:
  path: camera_prior.yaml
  cameras:
  - {}
  - factor_hz: 4.0
  - estimate_readout: true
    factor_hz: 4.0
  - estimate_readout: true
    factor_hz: 4.0
solve:
  max_iterations: 40
  corner_subsample: two_anti
  estimate_t_ga: true
  sigma_t_ga: 0.01
  vision_pose: imu
  sigma_g: 0.00065
  sigma_a: 0.0026
  sigma_bg: 0.001
  sigma_ba: 0.002
estimate:
  board_points: true
  camera:
    intrinsics: true
    tr: false
    td: true
    extrinsics: true
  imu:
    intrinsics: dw_da
    model: calyx
    extrinsics: true
    td: true
output:
  path: cam_imu.json
```

What the highlighted lines do:

- **`input`:** a ROS1 bag. The first `imu_topics` entry (`/imu/data`, the Xsens) is the **base IMU**. Every other sensor is solved relative to it.
- **`window_mode: motion`:** MVIS uses the `segment_length` seconds (here 80 s) with the most IMU motion.
- **`board.heights_file` + `estimate.board_points: true`:** solve the board shape too, starting from a measured shape. [`board_shape_4imus_4cams.yaml`](https://github.com/openmvis/openmvis/blob/main/examples/regression/mvis_elp/board_shape_4imus_4cams.yaml) ships with MVIS in `examples/regression/mvis_elp/`. Our board is not flat; a flat solve shifts the gyro scale and the lever arm ([why](/why/board-shape/)).
- **`solve.corner_subsample: two_anti`:** use two opposite corners of each tag (top-left and bottom-right). Our config sets it for speed. The cam-IMU default is `all` (every corner).
- **`solve.estimate_t_ga: true`, `sigma_t_ga: 0.01`:** solve `t_ga` for each IMU. Keep this on.
- **`solve.vision_pose: imu`:** moves the pose to each corner's capture time with the raw base-IMU samples. It is the default.
- **`sigma_g`, `sigma_a`, `sigma_bg`, `sigma_ba`:** gyro and accel noise, and their bias random walk.
- **`estimate.camera`:** intrinsics free, `td` and extrinsics solved.
- **`estimate.imu`:** IMU intrinsics `dw_da` with the `calyx` model (see section 3), plus extrinsics and `td` of the extra IMUs.

The other lines belong to later steps:

- **`camera_input.cameras`:** one entry per camera, in `cam_topics` order. The three cameras after the first enter the graph at 4 frames per second (`factor_hz`), which keeps the solve small. The first camera keeps the cam-IMU default of 10 Hz.
- **ELP readout lines** (`estimate_readout`, `tr`): `estimate.camera.tr: false` is the rig-wide default and holds the readout fixed. The per-camera `estimate_readout: true` on the two ELP entries overrides it, so the run solves the ELP readout and keeps the T265 (global shutter) at zero. Details in step 5, [Rolling-shutter calibration](/guides/rolling-shutter/).
- **The three extra IMUs:** step 6, [Multi-IMU spatial-temporal calibration](/guides/multi-sensor-rigs/).

For your own rig, change the topics, the board and the prior path. Keep the
rest as a starting point.

## 3. Turn on IMU intrinsics

`estimate.imu.intrinsics` sets how much of each IMU model we solve:

| Value | What is solved |
|---|---|
| `pinned` | nothing: `Dw` and `Da` stay at their start values |
| `dw_da` | scale and misalignment of gyro (`Dw`) and accel (`Da`). Our setting, and the default |
| `dw_da_r` | as `dw_da`, plus the gyro–accel rotation. Same as `dw_da` for `calyx`, which keeps that rotation inside `Dw` |
| `full` | as `dw_da_r`, plus `Tg` (how linear acceleration leaks into the gyro) |

- **A bool also works:** `true` means `full`, `false` means `pinned`.
- **`estimate.imu.model`** picks the model family: `calyx` (default), `kalibr` or `rpng`.
- **Priors:** freed terms get a weak prior. `solve.sigma_dw_da` defaults to 0.1, `solve.sigma_tg` to 0.05.

```yaml title="virig_4imus_4cams_cam_imu.yaml"
estimate:
  imu:
    intrinsics: dw_da   # pinned | dw_da | dw_da_r | full
    model: calyx        # calyx | kalibr | rpng
```

What the terms mean: [IMU model](/concepts/imu-model/). Why it pays off:
[Why calibrate the IMU](/why/imu/).

## 4. Run

```bash title="shell"
mvis cam-imu -c virig_4imus_4cams_cam_imu.yaml
```

- **Other bag:** `--bag <file>` replaces the config's source with that ROS1 bag.
- **Time:** 22 min in our run (MVIS 0.1.0, 2026-11-12), on 16 CPU cores with
  one thread for the linear-algebra libraries, on a machine under heavy load.
  Expect less on your machine. About 12 min of that is AprilTag detection on
  the four cameras; the solve stages take about 11 min, of which the 6
  iterations take about 7 min (`solve_s` in the report's Timing section).
- **Output:** in the folder of `output.path`:
  - `cam_imu.json`: a summary of the solve.
  - `cam_imu_calib.yaml`: the full calibration, the file to deploy.
  - `cam_imu_camchain-imucam.yaml`: the same rig in Kalibr's camchain format.
  - `cam_imu_board_shape.yaml`: the solved board shape (when `estimate.board_points` is on).
  - `cam_imu_report.html`: the report.
  - `cam_imu_views.json`: the data of the report's 3D views.

## 5. Read the result

Recording 01 gives the values below. The full table and a 3D view of the rig
are on the [example page](/benchmark/examples/virig-4imus-4cams/#solved-rig).

**The report.** Every run writes `cam_imu_report.html` next to `cam_imu.json`.
It has these sections:

- **Summary:** status, reprojection median and P95, χ²/dof of the camera and
  IMU terms with a grade each, and the base IMU's `t_ga`. χ²/dof near 1 means
  the residuals match the noise you set; well below 1 (0.13 here) means the
  noise settings are conservative.
- **3D Geometry:** the solved rig path around the board as a player, and the
  rig to scale.
- **Cameras:** per camera, intrinsics, distortion, lever arm and rotation
  (log(R), rad) with their σ, `td`, readout time, and the corner accounting
  (corners used, past the lens fold, dropped as outliers).
- **Board Shape:** the solved corner heights as a table and a map, with the
  median σ of a height. Shown because `estimate.board_points` is on.
- **Reprojection Analysis:** error by frame, by tag and over the board, per
  camera.
- **Motion & Excitation:** the angular velocity of the solved path, which shows
  how well the motion constrains the lever arms and `td`.
- **Base IMU, Aux IMUs, IMU Residuals:** biases and their drift, `Dw`, `Da`,
  `Tg` and `t_ga` of each IMU; each extra IMU's pose and `td` relative to the
  base IMU. The block is titled "IMU intrinsics (full)" whatever level you
  solve; with `dw_da`, as here, `Tg` stays at zero.
- **Timing, Result files, Configuration:** where the time went, the files the
  run wrote, and the config it read.

## 6. Leave `state_hz` alone

`solve.state_hz` sets how many pose, velocity and bias states per second the
solve keeps. It **defaults to 30 Hz**; our config does not set it. In our tests
on several rigs:

- **Fast motion:** at low rates the states undersample the motion. The
  reprojection error, lever arm and `td` drift, and settle around 30 Hz.
- **Slow, smooth motion:** the rate does not matter.
- **Not affected either way:** stereo baseline, IMU intrinsics and biases.

So:

- **Leave it at 30** unless your motion is slow and you want the speed.
- **Check it first** if `td` or the lever arm look wrong on a fast-moving rig.

## Batch: many recordings from one config

To calibrate many recordings at once, list them under `input.bags` instead of a
single `path`:

```yaml title="virig_4imus_4cams_cam_imu.yaml"
input:
  format: ros1
  bags:
    - 4imus_4cams_01.bag
    - 4imus_4cams_02.bag
    - 4imus_4cams_03.bag
```

- **Output:** each bag gets its own `bag<NN>/` folder in the folder of `output.path` (`results/sweep` when it has none). `<NN>` is the bag's trailing number (`4imus_4cams_01.bag` gives `bag01`).
- **Formats:** ROS1 bags, ROS2 bags and EuRoC `mav0` folders all work. Each inherits the config's format (`input.format`: `ros1`, `ros2` or `euroc`).
- **Use:** a repeatability sweep over many recordings.

The spread of extrinsics and intrinsics across recordings tells you far more
about accuracy than any single run's residual.

## Awkward IMUs

Two options help with accelerometers you cannot trust.

**Gyro-only:** calibrate an extra IMU from its gyro alone.

- **Solved:** extrinsic rotation, `td`, `Dw`, gyro bias.
- **Pinned:** lever arm, `Da`, `Tg`.

```yaml title="virig_4imus_4cams_cam_imu.yaml"
input:
  imus:
    - {}                     # base IMU: needs the accel
    - {gyro_only: true}      # extra IMU
```

- **Order:** entries match `imu_topics` by order.
- **Not on the base IMU:** the base needs its accelerometer for gravity and metric scale.

**Estimate `t_ga`:** solve each IMU's *internal* delay between gyroscope and
accelerometer. It is on in our config:

```yaml title="virig_4imus_4cams_cam_imu.yaml"
solve:
  estimate_t_ga: true
```

Both options target the same failure. A driver that resamples the
accelerometer to the gyro rate adds a timing offset. Without `t_ga`, that
offset leaks into `td`.

The T265 is the clearest case:

- **IMU:** Bosch BMI055, 62.5 Hz accel interpolated to 200 Hz.
- **Measured:** `t_ga` −6.74 ms on recording 01 with the Xsens as base, and −6.697 ms median over 15 recordings with the T265 IMU as base and the Kalibr IMU model (above).
- **Not a pure shift:** the delay changes with the motion, so one `t_ga` is a compromise.

Details in [IMU model](/concepts/imu-model/). Why it matters, with real numbers:
[Why calibrate t_ga](/why/tga/).
