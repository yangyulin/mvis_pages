Step 2 of the walkthrough. Here you record takes the solver can use.

- **You need:** the board from <span id="boards-we-used" class="cm-anchor"></span>[step 1](/guides/board-setup/),
  set up and measured.
- **You get:** one bag (recording file) per take. [Step 3](/guides/camera-calibration/) calibrates the cameras
  from it.
- **Check:** each take passes the rules in section 1, and the solve in steps 3 and 4 converges.

A calibration can only find what the motion shows it. The 60 s motion below turns the rig about every axis
and sweeps the board across the whole image.

We built it from a real hand-held recording with our
[Looper Insight 9](/devices/looper-insight9/). Then we checked it on a **digital twin**: a simulated copy of
that rig, with realistic noise, which we record, solve and compare with the known truth. A motion is good if
the twin still converges on every calibration parameter, that is, the solve settles on the true value within
its uncertainty.

## 1. What a good take has

- **About 60 s long.** 50 s also works.
- **Fill the frame.** Cover the image out to its corners. The lens distortion is only pinned down where the
  board was seen.
- **Vary distance and angle.** Move in and out, and turn the rig about every axis.
- **No motion blur.** Use enough light for a short exposure (about 2 ms).
- **Rest at both ends.** Hold still for about 3 s at the start and at the end.

## <span id="step-by-step" class="cm-anchor"></span>3. Step by step

Do the moves in order and blend each into the next. Keep a comfortable hand pace: at most about 0.4 m/s and
1.9 rad/s.

| # | Move | Time | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Rest | 0–3 s | Hold still in front of the board and count to 3. |
| 1 | Left–right + turn | 3–20 s | Swing left and right round the board, 2 times. At each end, turn the camera a little past facing the board, so the board slides to the left or right edge of the picture. Turn round sharply. |
| 2 | Up–down + tilt | 20–32 s | Move up and down, 2 times. Tilt the camera a little past the board, so the board reaches the top or bottom edge of the picture. |
| 3 | In–out + twist | 32–47 s | Move in to 0.22 m and out to 0.52 m, 3 times. Twist the camera about its view axis like a steering wheel: one way coming in, the other way going out. Hold the board off-centre, so the twist carries it into the corners. |
| 4 | Loop + twist | 47–57 s | Move round a loop parallel to the board, about 43 cm across, 2 times. Turn the camera a little against the loop and twist it a little, once per loop, so the board runs into every corner. |
| 5 | Rest | 57–60 s | Hold still and count to 3. |

- **Vary the speed** on every sweep: push through the middle, brake into the ends.
- **Keep most of the board in view.** An AprilGrid may run partly off the edge; a chessboard may not.
- **Do not add shake.** The natural wobble of a hand is all the motion needs.

## 4. The running example's recordings

The VI-Rig 4 IMUs + 4 cameras set has 15 takes, `4imus_4cams_01.bag` to `4imus_4cams_15.bag`. Each bag holds
these topics:

| Sensor | Topic | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| RealSense T265 left | `/t265/fisheye1/image_raw` | 30 Hz | 848 × 800, global shutter |
| RealSense T265 right | `/t265/fisheye2/image_raw` | 30 Hz | 848 × 800, global shutter |
| ELP left | `/elp/split_sync_image_node/left/image_raw` | about 20–22 Hz | 640 × 480, rolling shutter; 25 Hz as captured |
| ELP right | `/elp/split_sync_image_node/right/image_raw` | about 20–22 Hz | 640 × 480, rolling shutter; 25 Hz as captured |
| Xsens MTi-100 | `/imu/data` | 400 Hz | IMU |
| MicroStrain GX3-25 | `/gx3_25/data` | 500 Hz | IMU |
| MicroStrain GX3-35 | `/gx3_35/imu/data` | 100 Hz | IMU |
| RealSense T265 IMU | `/t265/imu` | 200 Hz | IMU |

- **Download:** [`calibench-vi/datasets` → `virig-4imus-4cams`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/calibench-vi/datasets/tree/main/virig-4imus-4cams)
  on the Hub. Access is gated. The licence is CC BY-NC 4.0; for commercial use, ask us.
- **Which file:** use `4imus_4cams_NN.bag`. In it, the ELP image stamps sit on the camera's own clock. The
  `4imus_4cams_NN_raw.bag` next to it is the recording as captured, with the ELP driver's stamps. Moving
  the stamps drops the ELP frames that cannot be matched to the camera clock, so the ELP rate in
  `4imus_4cams_NN.bag` is about 20–22 Hz.
- **Board config:** the AprilGrid target file `april.yaml` sits next to the bags.
- **More:** the [dataset page](/datasets/virig-4imus-4cams/).

:::caution[Bag 09 starts one frame early]
In `4imus_4cams_09.bag` the right T265 fisheye starts one frame before the left one. MVIS and Basalt pair
frames by the nearest stamp, so they handle it. Kalibr reads each camera on its own, so its pairs come out
one frame off. Give Kalibr a trimmed copy of the bag.
:::

## Motions that fail

Each of these leaves some parameters loose: the solve cannot pin them down.

- **Fast shaking from one spot, face-on:** the lens distortion stays loose.
- **One slow axis at a time, never tilted:** the lever arm (where the camera sits relative to the IMU), the
  time offsets and the IMU scales stay loose.
- **A missing rotation** (no tilt, no twist, or only turning left–right): the matching gyroscope and
  accelerometer scales, or the whole camera–IMU rotation, cannot be found.
- **The board always in the centre:** the image edges are never seen, so the distortion stays loose.

## How we judged it

The twin is a simulated Looper Insight 9 in front of the board, with realistic corner and IMU noise, motion
blur and dropped detections.

We solved each motion on 3 noise draws and once without noise. Each solve estimated t_ga (the time offset
between the gyroscope and the accelerometer) and left the camera intrinsics (focal length, image centre and
lens distortion) free.

A motion is **good** if, on all 22 parameters:

- every solve converges;
- every error stays within its uncertainty;
- every uncertainty is at most 3 × that of a well-excited 80 s reference motion.

The 22 parameters are the camera–IMU rotation and lever arm, td (the camera–IMU time offset), t_ga, the
gyroscope and accelerometer scales, and the camera intrinsics.

To run the same kind of check on your own calibration, see [step 7: digital-twin check](/guides/simulation/).

## Replay it yourself

Run our motions through your own simulator and calibration tool with the
[replay pack](/downloads/calib-motion-replay.zip) (zip, 3.2 MB). It holds:

- **the four motions** at 200 Hz: IMU pose, ideal gyroscope and accelerometer;
- **the two twin rigs:** camera, IMU, truth, noise and detection rules;
- **our twin results**, with the pass rule.

Its README gives the frames, units and replay steps. The files are plain CSV, YAML and JSON, so you do not
need MVIS to use them. We ran the twin with the MVIS simulator (`mvis.simulation`).

**Licence:** the pack's README names the licence of each file. The two real-example trajectories and their
results come from our Looper Insight 9 recordings and are CC BY-NC 4.0.
