A camera–IMU calibration can only find what the motion shows it. If the rig never pitches, the solver
cannot tell the gyro scale on that axis from noise. If the board never reaches the image corners, the
lens distortion stays loose. The motion below takes 60 seconds and needs nothing beyond an AprilGrid.

We built it from a real hand-held recording with our own Looper Insight 9 in front of a monitor
AprilGrid (the [AprilGrid example](#real-examples) below), slowed it to a pace a hand keeps up comfortably, and changed it only where a
**noisy digital twin** of the rig said it was weak. The twin simulates the recording, solves it, and
compares the result with the truth. A motion is good if the twin still converges on every calibration
parameter.

## Step by step

The motion keeps the four moves of the AprilGrid example, our best real take, in the same order: **left–right with yaw**,
**up–down with pitch**, **back–forth with roll** and a **loop parallel to the board**. Rests start and end
the recording. Four rules run through all of it:

- **Vary the speed. Never move at a steady pace.** Push through the middle of every sweep and brake into
  the ends (about 1.25× the average rate in the middle and 0.75× at the ends; on back–forth a gentler
  1.15× and 0.85×).
- **Turn past the board.** At the ends of left–right (yaw) and up–down (pitch), keep turning past the
  board so it slides to the edges of the picture. On left–right, turn round sharply at each end.
- **Roll with the board off-centre.** During back–forth, aim beside the board while you roll, so the
  roll carries it into the corners of the picture.
- **Reach the corners.** In the loop, turn the camera a little against the loop and roll it, so the board
  runs round the picture border into every corner.

An AprilGrid is detected even when some of its tags leave the picture, so the board may run off the edge,
but keep most of it in view: in the guide motion all 36 tags are visible in the median frame, at least
60 % of them in about 95 % of the frames, and never fewer than 4.

**Setup**

- AprilGrid (we used 6 × 6 tags of 43.47 mm on a monitor) upright at chest height, flat, well lit.
- Short exposure, about **2 ms** (bright room, auto exposure off), so the fast parts stay sharp.
- Start about **0.42 m** in front of the board, face-on, rig upright.
- Record **60 s**. Do not add any shake: the slow, natural wobble of a hand-held camera (a degree or two, two
  or three times a second) is all the motion needs.

Blend one move into the next; do not stop between steps. The motion stays inside what a hand does
comfortably: it peaks at about 0.38 m/s, 1.9 rad/s and 1.5 m/s² (speed and acceleration of the IMU), and
the camera never looks more than 68° away from the board. The board reaches every image cell it can reach, all four picture corners
included.

**50 s is enough.** Drop one in-out and about one loop (2 in-outs, 1.1 loops, the rest unchanged): the
twin still passes every parameter, with σ at most 1.88× the reference against 1.69× for 60 s (td both
times; lever arm x 1.85× against 1.66×), about what 985 instead of 1185 frames costs.

### What the twin says about the guide motion

<CalibMotionVerdicts set="v4" />

**Why the loop rolls and left–right turns round sharply.** Our first guide from the AprilGrid example lost
td, the camera time offset, when slowed to the hand limits: σ 3.14× the reference at 60 s and 3.30× at 50 s,
over the 3× limit, against 1.11× for the smoothed real-speed fit of the AprilGrid example. It barely rolled in the loop, turned only
about 15° past the board at the ends of left–right and had less jiggle, so less angular acceleration. A 25°
roll in the loop, 22° turns past the board with a sharp turnaround, and more jiggle brought td back to
2.10× (60 s) and 2.18× (50 s). That jiggle was a 4–6 Hz buzz, faster than a hand shakes. The guide now has
the slower wobble of a real hand, 1.5–3.5 times a second and a little larger, which gives as much angular
acceleration and passes better still: td 1.69× and lever arm x 1.66× at 60 s (*Guide with a fast wobble*
in the table: 2.10× and 2.27×).

## Real examples

Two hand-held recordings of our Looper Insight 9, one per board: the *AprilGrid example* is our 41 s
AprilGrid recording of 2026-11-08, the *Chessboard example* our 61 s chessboard recording of
2026-11-07. Each was solved for real with MVIS (camera intrinsics free, t_ga estimated) and replayed

Coverage is the share of the reachable 100 px image cells the board reached (the player's number at
the end of the take). An AprilGrid is detected even when part of it is outside the picture, so its
coverage counts partly visible boards; the chessboard counts only frames with the whole board in view.
Lever is the camera position in the IMU frame. σ is the formal 1σ of the solve. Each take is compared
with the 80 s reference motion on the twin of its own board.

What they show: a shorter take is fine when every axis turns well past the board and the board sweeps
the picture. The AprilGrid example gets there in 41 s by moving fast: it peaks at 0.75 m/s, 5.0 rad/s and 6.1 m/s² (at
the IMU), two to three times what the guide motion asks for. The chessboard example is slower (0.54 m/s,
3.7 rad/s, 4.0 m/s²) and keeps the board near the image centre, so it needs 61 s, and it is the closest
of the two to the 3× limit. At a comfortable pace, follow the 60 s guide motion.

## Chessboard version

A chessboard is only detected when every one of its corners is in the picture, so a chessboard motion
must keep the whole board in view and cannot swing as far. *Chessboard version* in the player is our
guide for that case. We built it from our 30 s chessboard take of 2026-11-08 (*Chessboard take 11-08 01* in
the table below): twice left–right, twice up–down, twice back–forth, with no instructions beyond the three
moves. The twin's uncertainties for that take match the real solve's (lever arm within 11 %, camera–IMU rotation within
35 %, the time offsets within about 40 %), so the twin can be trusted to compare motions. The take passes,
close to the limit (σ at most 2.93× the reference, accel scale y). It moves smoothly and slowly
(0.47–0.55 m/s² RMS of linear acceleration), keeps the board near the image centre (69 % of the reachable
cells) and changes distance little (0.31–0.59 m).

One change per miss turned it into the chessboard version: vary the speed (push and brake, plus a small
jiggle); turn past the board at the ends of left–right and up–down, and add a corner square, for the edges
and corners; and a longer, off-centre, rolled back–forth for distance and roll. Each change passed the
twin on its own. All four together were faster
than a hand moves comfortably, so we tamed the jiggle and the in-out and cut the motion from 80 s to 60 s
by dropping whole swings: 2 left–right, 2 up–down, 3 in-outs and a 9.6 s corner square. It peaks at about
0.40 m/s, 1.9 rad/s and 1.4 m/s², keeps the whole chessboard in view in 93 % of the frames, and passes
every parameter with σ at most 1.92× the reference (lever arm x). Its fine wobble is the same slow hand shake
as in the AprilGrid guide; with the earlier 4–6 Hz buzz it passed at 2.48× (td).

## Motions that fail

We also ran motions that each miss one of the rules, on the AprilGrid twin and against the same 80 s
reference. None of them passes:

- **Fast shaking from one spot, nearly face-on** (like the UZH-FPV calibration bags): speed does not replace
  range: the distortion terms k3 and k4 stay loose.
- **One slow axis at a time, with pauses, never pitched** (like the XQ20 vendor recordings): little angular
  acceleration and unexcited axes. The lever arm, both time offsets, gyro scale x and y, accel scale z and
  k4 all fail.
- **A missing rotation.** Translation only, or yaw only: the camera–IMU rotation cannot be found and every
  parameter fails. No pitch: gravity never tips onto the optical axis (gyro scale y, accel scale z fail). No
  roll: no turn about the optical axis (gyro scale z, accel scale y fail).
- **The board always centred**: the lens edge is never seen, so k2–k4 stay loose.
- **Smooth, slow orbits aimed at the board centre**, with no varied speed and no turn past the board: little
  angular acceleration and the board stays mid-image. The lever arm, both time offsets, gyro scale z and
  k1–k4 fail.

## How we judged it

The twin is a simulated **Looper Insight 9** (camera 0 and its IMU, with their calibrated values as the
truth) in front of the board, an AprilGrid or a chessboard. Each motion drives the simulated rig. Noise
matches the real sensor: corner noise, IMU white noise and bias drift, motion blur and dropped detections.
On the AprilGrid twin every tag in the picture is used; on the chessboard twin only frames with the whole
board in view, as with the real detectors. Each motion is solved with the normal camera–IMU setup (t_ga
estimated, camera intrinsics free) on 3 noise seeds plus 1 noise-free run.

A motion **passes** a parameter if:

1. every solve converges: the solver reports success, and its reprojection error is plausible, at most
   1.5× the reference motion's median on each noise seed (about 0.70 px here) and below 0.05 px on the
   noise-free run;
2. the noise-free error is at most 1σ of the reference motion (or a small floor), so the motion and not
   the noise sets the result;
3. σ is honest: the error is above 3σ on at most one of the 3 noise seeds, and above 4σ on none (one seed
   in three just over 3σ happens by chance);
4. σ is at most 3× the σ of the 80 s reference motion. The two are compared directly, whatever the length
   of the motion: a 20 or 30 s take is fine as long as it pins every parameter down this well.

A motion is **good** if every parameter passes. σ is the median of the formal σ over the 3 seeds. The 22
parameters: camera–IMU rotation (x, y, z), lever arm (x, y, z), time offsets td and t_ga, gyro and accel
scale per axis, and the camera intrinsics fx, fy, cx, cy, k1–k4. Rotation errors are reported as the log(R)
rotation vector in mrad, never as roll-pitch-yaw angles. The reference on each twin is a well-excited
simulated motion of 80 s run on that twin; it is not shown on this page.
