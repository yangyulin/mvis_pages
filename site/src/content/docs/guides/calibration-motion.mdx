A camera–IMU calibration can only find what the motion shows it. The 60 s motion below excites every
axis and sweeps the board across the whole image. We built it from a real hand-held recording with our
Looper Insight 9 and checked it with a **noisy digital twin**: the twin simulates the recording, solves
it and compares the result with the truth. A motion is good if the twin still converges on every
calibration parameter.

## Step by step

Four moves in order, blended into each other, with a short rest at each end:

| # | Move | Time | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Rest | 0–3 s | Hold still in front of the board, count 3. |
| 1 | Left–right + yaw | 3–20 s | Swing left and right around the board, 2 times (about 43° each side), turning about 22° past facing it at each end so the board slides to the picture edge. Turn round sharply. |
| 2 | Up–down + pitch | 20–32 s | Move up and down, 2 times (about 33° above and below), tipping about 34° past the board so it reaches the top or bottom edge. |
| 3 | Back–forth + roll | 32–47 s | Move in to 0.22 m and out to 0.52 m, 3 times, rolling like a steering wheel up to about 65°. Hold the board off-centre so the roll carries it into the corners. |
| 4 | Loop + roll | 47–57 s | Move round a loop parallel to the board, about 40 cm across, 2 times, turning a little against the loop and rolling about 25°, so the board runs into every corner. |
| 5 | Rest | 57–60 s | Hold still, count 3. |

- **Vary the speed** on every sweep: push through the middle, brake into the ends.
- **Keep most of the board in view.** An AprilGrid may run partly off the edge; a chessboard may not.
- **Don't add shake.** The natural wobble of a hand is all the motion needs.

**Setup:** AprilGrid upright and flat (we used 6 × 6 tags of 43.47 mm on a monitor), short exposure
(about 2 ms), start about 0.42 m away, face-on. The motion stays at a comfortable hand pace (about
0.4 m/s and 1.9 rad/s at most). 50 s also works: drop one in-out and one loop.

## Real examples

Two hand-held recordings of our Looper Insight 9, solved with MVIS and replayed through the twin of
their board. Both pass. Trajectory and image coverage only; no camera images.

## Chessboard version

A chessboard is only detected with every corner in the picture, so its motion swings less and adds a
short corner square instead of the loop. Same rules otherwise; it also passes the twin.

## Motions that fail

- **Fast shaking from one spot, face-on:** the lens distortion stays loose.
- **One slow axis at a time, never pitched:** lever arm, time offsets and IMU scales stay loose.
- **A missing rotation** (no pitch, no roll, or yaw only): the matching gyro and accelerometer scales,
  or the whole camera–IMU rotation, cannot be found.
- **The board always centred:** the image edges are never seen, so the distortion stays loose.

## How we judged it

The twin is a simulated Looper Insight 9 in front of the board, with realistic corner and IMU noise,
motion blur and dropped detections. Each motion is solved on 3 noise seeds plus a noise-free run (t_ga
estimated, camera intrinsics free). A motion is **good** if, on all 22 parameters (camera–IMU rotation,
lever arm, td, t_ga, gyro and accelerometer scales, and the camera intrinsics), every solve converges, the
error stays within its uncertainty, and the uncertainty is at most 3× that of a well-excited 80 s
reference motion.
