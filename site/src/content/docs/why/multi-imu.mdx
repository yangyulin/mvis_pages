This page shows why a rig with several IMUs needs them calibrated against each other. You need nothing to run
here. Most numbers come from our published runs on [VI-Rig](/benchmark/results/virig-4imus-4cams/); sections 3
and 5 add unpublished studies, marked where used.

**The answer first:**

- **No shared clock:** each IMU stamps its data on its own clock. On the running example, the T265 IMU is 7.2 ms
  off the Xsens.
- **No shared origin:** each IMU sits at its own place on the rig, with its own rotation.
- **What MVIS solves:** the **base IMU** is the one the trajectory and the reference clock hang on; every other
  IMU is an **aux IMU**. For each aux IMU, MVIS solves its lever (position), rotation, $t_d$ and $t_{ga}$
  against the base IMU.

How to turn it on: [Turn on the aux spatial and temporal terms](/guides/multi-sensor-rigs/#2-turn-on-the-aux-spatial-and-temporal-terms).

## 1. Each IMU has its own clock offset and pose

All four cameras and all four IMUs in one solve: [example run](/benchmark/examples/virig-4imus-4cams/).

- **Data:** [VI-Rig 4 IMUs + 4 cams](/datasets/virig-4imus-4cams/), recording 4imus_4cams_01.
- **Base IMU:** the Xsens MTi; the other three are solved against it.

| IMU | Rate | Lever (mm) | $\log\mathbf{R}$ (rad) | $t_d$ (ms) | $t_{ga}$ (ms) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Xsens MTi (base) | 400 Hz | 0 | 0 | 0 | −0.82 ± 0.02 |
| MicroStrain GX3-25 | 500 Hz | [−122.8, −45.3, −3.1] | [+2.2502, +2.1849, −0.0279] | −0.69 | +0.10 ± 0.02 |
| MicroStrain GX3-35 | 100 Hz | [−82.3, −46.2, −3.5] | [+2.2304, +2.2070, −0.0192] | −2.07 | +0.01 ± 0.02 |
| T265 IMU | 200 Hz | [+54.7, −107.4, −30.3] | [+1.5724, −0.0054, −0.0113] | −7.17 | −6.74 ± 0.02 |

- **Frames:** each lever and rotation is the aux IMU in the base IMU frame. Rotations are $\log\mathbf{R}$ in rad.
- **$t_d$ sign:** $t_I = t_{I_j} + t_d$, with $t_I$ on the base clock and $t_{I_j}$ the aux IMU's own stamp.
- **Time:** the aux IMU clocks are 0.69 to 7.17 ms off the base clock.
- **$t_{ga}$:** only the T265 IMU has a large gyro–accel offset; the other three read near 0. See
  [Why calibrate t_ga](/why/tga/).

## 2. MVIS levers repeat several times tighter than Kalibr's

Every recording of each dataset, from the results pages.

- **Spread:** how much each value moves over recordings, as the standard deviation (sd). A small spread means the
  value repeats; it does not prove it is right.
- **MVIS column:** for the 4 IMUs + 4 cams set we use "MVIS (K-IMU)", MVIS run with Kalibr's IMU convention, so
  the IMU terms mean the same in both tools.
- **No Kalibr run:** the 5 IMUs + 4 cams set has none, so its numbers are plain MVIS.

**4 IMUs + 4 cams, mixed T265 fisheye 1 + ELP-L run, [T7](/benchmark/results/virig-4imus-4cams/#T7)** (T265
IMU base, Xsens aux):

| Xsens vs T265 IMU | MVIS (15 recordings) | Kalibr (14 recordings) |
|---|---|---|
| $t_d$ | 7.240 ms (sd 0.146) | 7.260 ms (sd 0.122) |
| distance to base $\lVert\mathbf{p}\rVert$ | 124.09 mm (sd 0.32) | 114.10 mm (sd 2.96) |

- **$t_d$ agrees:** the two tools are 0.02 ms apart.
- **Same offset, other side:** with the Xsens as base (section 1), the T265 IMU $t_d$ is −7.17 ms. With the T265
  IMU as base, the Xsens $t_d$ is +7.24 ms.
- **Lever differs:** the tools are 10 mm apart, and Kalibr's spread is about 9× larger.
- **Unequal counts:** Kalibr failed on one recording.
- **[T8](/benchmark/results/virig-4imus-4cams/#T8), a rough check:** on the ELP pair with the same two IMUs, MVIS gives 123.94 mm, sd 0.30 over
  15 recordings, and Kalibr 115.85 mm, sd 1.24 over 3 recordings from separate earlier runs.

More: [VI-Rig 4 IMUs + 4 cams results](/benchmark/results/virig-4imus-4cams/).

**4 IMUs + 3 cams, [T6](/benchmark/results/virig-4imus-3cams/#T6)** (Xsens base, three aux IMUs, 10 recordings each):

| Aux IMU | MVIS lever sd | Kalibr lever sd |
|---|---|---|
| GX3-25 | 0.20 mm | 0.88 mm |
| GX3-35 | 0.19 mm | 0.86 mm |
| T265 IMU | 0.19 mm | 1.42 mm |

More: [VI-Rig 4 IMUs + 3 cams results](/benchmark/results/virig-4imus-3cams/).

**5 IMUs + 4 cams** (Xsens base, two T265 units, 7 recordings, MVIS only):

| | $t_d$ (ms) | distance to base $\lVert\mathbf{p}\rVert$ (mm) |
|---|---|---|
| T265 A IMU | −7.170 (sd 0.115) | 180.77 (sd 0.40) |
| T265 B IMU | −7.259 (sd 0.120) | 188.60 (sd 0.41) |

- **Two units, same offset:** both T265 IMUs sit about 7.2 ms off the Xsens.
- **Nothing to compare:** this set has no Kalibr run. More:
  [VI-Rig 5 IMUs + 4 cams results](/benchmark/results/virig-5imus-4cams/).

## 3. The aux IMU lever does not depend on the cameras

- **Test:** T7 and T8 solve the same two IMUs (T265 IMU base, Xsens aux) on the same 15 recordings. Only the
  cameras differ: T7 uses T265 fisheye 1 + ELP-L, T8 the ELP pair.
- **Results medians:** the Xsens distance to base is 124.09 mm in T7 and 123.94 mm in T8.
- **Per-recording gap:** in an unpublished study, the gap between the two runs' Xsens levers was 0.11 mm.
- **What it means:** the aux IMU lever comes from the IMUs, not from which cameras are in the solve.

## 4. Several IMUs constrain each other

Several IMUs see the *same* motion. That redundancy constrains what one IMU leaves weak:

- **Time offsets:** every IMU must agree on when each motion happened.
- **IMU intrinsics:** scale, misalignment and similar sensor errors, $\mathbf{D}_w$ and $\mathbf{D}_a$.
- **A meaningful check:** a wrong calibration usually cannot satisfy every IMU at once. This makes the
  [round-trip check](/guides/roundtrip/) useful on these rigs.

## 5. Slow IMUs bias levers on short windows

- **Twin:** a noise-free simulated copy of the 4 IMUs + 3 cams rig with known truth and true $t_{ga}$ = 0.
  Unpublished.
- **Measured:** lever error at aux factor intervals 1 / 2 / 3.

| IMU rate | Interval 1 | Interval 2 | Interval 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 Hz | 2.94 mm | 1.47 mm | 0.63 mm |
| 200 Hz | 0.56 mm | 0.30 mm | 0.17 mm |
| 500 Hz | 0.11 mm | 0.05 mm | 0.05 mm |

- **Why:** at 100 Hz an aux factor at interval 1 holds only about 3 samples.
- **Default:** MVIS upsamples an IMU slower than 200 Hz before it integrates it
  (`solve.imu_min_rate_hz: 200`, `solve.imu_upsample_method: cubic`).
- **Upsampling helps:** in the same twin, cubic upsampling of the 100 Hz IMU gave 0.80 / 0.52 / 0.12 mm.
- **The knob:** `solve.aux_imu_stride` sets one aux IMU factor per this many base intervals. The default is 3.
- **Levers move with it:** keep the default unless you have a reason, and compare runs only at the same value.

**Conclusion:**

- **Calibrate every IMU:** each aux IMU has its own clock offset and pose, and MVIS solves both in one graph.
- **The levers repeat:** MVIS levers repeat more tightly than Kalibr's and do not depend on the cameras.
- **Mind slow IMUs:** keep the upsampling and stride defaults, and compare runs only at the same stride.

## Limits

<div class="not-content rp-honest">
  <ul>
    <li><b>Slow IMUs:</b> on short windows they bias the lever; the twin numbers above are unpublished.</li>
    <li><b>More IMUs do not help the readout:</b> the rolling-shutter readout comes from the cameras. In an older
      study, adding IMUs moved the ELP readout from 15.9 to 16.6 ms and made the fit worse, reprojection error
      3.04 → 3.49. See <a href="/why/rolling-shutter/">Why calibrate rolling shutter</a>.</li>
    <li><b>Pick the base with care:</b> the base IMU carries the trajectory and the reference clock. Its errors
      reach every other sensor.</li>
    <li><b>Repeatable is not right:</b> spread over recordings shows precision. There is no reference lever for
      these rigs.</li>
  </ul>
</div>
