Use this page to see what rolling shutter does to a real calibration. You need
no setup: every number comes from runs on the VI-Rig dataset
[virig-4imus-4cams](/reference/datasets/virig-4imus-4cams/).

**The answer first:**

- **The ELP is rolling shutter.** It reads its rows one after another, over about 16.6 ms.
- **Calling it global costs fit.** The median reprojection error grows by about 20–25 %.
- **td hides the error.** The camera time offset `td` moves by about half the readout to absorb it.
- **No warning.** The solve still converges, and the shifted `td` looks like a normal time offset.

A **readout time** is the time a camera takes to read all its rows. A
**reprojection error** is the distance between a detected board corner and
where the calibration predicts it, in pixels.

## 1. What the ELP does

The rig has two ELP cameras (ELP-L, ELP-R), 640×480 at 25 Hz, rolling shutter.
MVIS solves the readout with everything else in the camera–IMU stage.

- **Example run, bag 01:** readout 16.68 ms (ELP-L) and 16.73 ms (ELP-R). See the
  [worked example](/benchmark/examples/virig-4imus-4cams/).
- **All 15 bags (ELP RS stereo + two IMUs), median:** ELP-L 16.618 ms (sd 0.122),
  ELP-R 16.594 ms (sd 0.111).
- **Left and right agree** to within 0.05 ms.

So during one image, the top and bottom rows are taken about 16.6 ms apart.
If the rig moves, the rows see the board from slightly different poses.

## 2. Turn it off and see

We ran the same camera–IMU solve twice, once with rolling shutter and once with
the ELP called global shutter. Setup: ELP pair, T265 IMU as base, Xsens as the
second IMU, bag 01.

:::caution[Older code]
This A/B test ran on October 2026 code. That is before the ELP stamp
correction, [board-shape estimation](/why/board-shape/) and `vision_pose: imu`. So the absolute
pixel values (about 1 px) are much worse than today's 0.16–0.24 px on the ELP. Read the
change between the two columns, not the values.
:::

| Bag | Median reprojection, RS (px) | Median reprojection, called global (px) | ELP-L td shift (ms) | Readout in the RS run (ms) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | 1.043 | 1.303 | +9.5 | 16.2 |

What we see:

- **Fit:** the median error grows by 0.26 px.
- **td moves by about half the readout.** A global-shutter model stamps the whole
  image at one time. The solver then moves `td` so that this time sits near the
  middle row.
- **The IMU barely notices:** the base gyro scale moved by 0.5 % or less. So a
  wrong shutter model mainly corrupts the camera's timing, not the IMU.

That shifted `td` is the real risk. It looks like a normal time offset. But it
is only right for the middle row: rows at the top and bottom are off by up to
half the readout, about 8 ms.

## 3. Repeatability against Kalibr

The public results compare MVIS and Kalibr on all 15 bags. See
[VI-Rig: 4 IMUs + 4 cameras](/benchmark/results/virig-4imus-4cams/).

**Camera only (ELP RS stereo + readout):**

| Camera | MVIS readout (ms) | Kalibr readout (ms) |
|---|---|---|
| ELP-L | 14.600 (sd 0.409, n = 15) | 16.354 (sd 2.00, n = 15) |
| ELP-R | 14.598 (sd 0.408, n = 15) | 17.369 (sd 1.42, n = 13) |

- **MVIS repeats 3–5× tighter** across bags than Kalibr's rolling-shutter model.
- **Kalibr's rolling-shutter model runs one camera at a time**, so it has no ELP
  stereo baseline.
- **Lens models differ:** MVIS uses the equidistant model on the ELP, Kalibr radtan.
  So only the readout is compared here.
- **Kalibr was not re-run** after the ELP stamp correction.
- **With the IMU**, the MVIS readout spread drops to about 0.1 ms (section 1).

**Camera–IMU, mixed rig (T265 fisheye 1 + ELP-L + two IMUs):**

| Camera | MVIS median reprojection (px) | Kalibr median reprojection (px) |
|---|---|---|
| ELP-L | 0.181 (rolling shutter) | 0.795 (taken as global shutter) |
| T265 fisheye 1 | 0.200 | 0.367 |

- **Kalibr's camera–IMU solver is global shutter only.** It takes ELP-L as global,
  with readout fixed at 0.
- **Not a clean on/off test.** MVIS uses two corners per tag, non-base cameras
  at 4 Hz or lower and an [estimated board shape](/why/board-shape/). Kalibr uses none of these. The
  gap on the global-shutter T265 suggests part of it comes from those settings.

## 4. Limits

- **Camera only and camera–IMU disagree.** MVIS gets 14.6 ms from camera only and
  16.6 ms with the IMU, about 2 ms apart.
- **Readout needs help to be seen.** It needs a high enough frame rate or IMU
  support. On a low-rate camera with no IMU, the readout can follow the
  solver's starting value, not the data.
- **Check repeatability, not one run.** Calibrate several bags and compare the
  readout. A value that moves a lot between bags is not well observed.
- **A round trip cannot test it.** `mvis apply-calib` corrects frame stamps
  only. It does not unwarp image rows, so a
  [round-trip validation](/guides/roundtrip/) says nothing about the readout.

## How to turn it on

Set `shutter_type: rolling` on each rolling-shutter camera and estimate the
readout in the camera–IMU stage. Steps and config:
[Rolling-shutter calibration](/guides/rolling-shutter/).
