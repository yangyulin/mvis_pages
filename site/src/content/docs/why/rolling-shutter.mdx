Use this page to see what rolling shutter does to a real calibration. You need no setup.
Data: our runs on the VI-Rig dataset [virig-4imus-4cams](/reference/datasets/virig-4imus-4cams/).

**The answer first:**

- **The ELP is rolling shutter:** it reads its rows one after another, over about 16.6 ms.
- **Calling it global costs fit:** the median reprojection error grows by about 20–25 %.
- **$t_d$ hides the error:** the camera time offset $t_d$ moves by about half the readout to absorb it.
- **No warning:** the solve still converges, and the shifted $t_d$ looks like a normal time offset.

How to turn it on: [Rolling-shutter calibration](/guides/rolling-shutter/).

## The setup

- **Readout time:** the time $t_r$ a camera takes to read all its rows.
- **Reprojection error:** the distance between a detected board corner and where the calibration predicts it,
  in pixels.
- **Cameras:** the rig has two ELP cameras, ELP-L and ELP-R, 640×480 at 25 Hz, rolling shutter.
- **Solve:** MVIS solves the readout with everything else in the camera–IMU stage.

## 1. The ELP reads its rows over about 16.6 ms

- **Example run, recording 01:** readout 16.68 ms for ELP-L and 16.73 ms for ELP-R. See the
  [worked example](/benchmark/examples/virig-4imus-4cams/).
- **All 15 recordings, median:** with ELP RS stereo and two IMUs, ELP-L reads 16.618 ms (sd 0.122) and ELP-R
  16.594 ms (sd 0.111).
- **Left and right agree:** to within 0.05 ms.
- **What it means:** within one image, the top and bottom rows are taken about 16.6 ms apart. If the rig
  moves, the rows see the board from slightly different poses.

## 2. Called global, the fit gets worse and the time offset moves

We ran the same camera–IMU solve twice: once with rolling shutter, once with the ELP called global shutter.
Setup: ELP pair, T265 IMU as base, Xsens as the second IMU, recording 01.

:::caution[Older code]
This A/B test ran on October 2026 code. That is before the ELP stamp correction,
[board-shape estimation](/why/board-shape/) and `vision_pose: imu`. So the absolute pixel values, about 1 px,
are much worse than today's 0.16–0.24 px on the ELP. Read the change between the two columns, not the values.
:::

| Recording | Median reprojection, RS (px) | Median reprojection, called global (px) | ELP-L $t_d$ shift (ms) | Readout in the RS run (ms) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | 1.043 | 1.303 | +9.5 | 16.2 |

- **Fit:** the median error grows by 0.26 px.
- **$t_d$ moves by about half the readout:** a global-shutter model stamps the whole image at one time. The
  solver then moves $t_d$ so that this time sits near the middle row.
- **The IMU barely notices:** the base gyro scale moved by 0.5 % or less. A wrong shutter model mainly
  corrupts the camera's timing, not the IMU.
- **The real risk:** the shifted $t_d$ looks like a normal time offset. But it is right only for the middle
  row. Rows at the top and bottom are off by up to half the readout, about 8 ms.

## 3. MVIS repeats the readout tighter than Kalibr

The public results compare MVIS and Kalibr on all 15 recordings. See
[VI-Rig: 4 IMUs + 4 cameras](/benchmark/results/virig-4imus-4cams/).

**Camera only (ELP RS stereo + readout):**

| Camera | MVIS readout (ms) | Kalibr readout (ms) |
|---|---|---|
| ELP-L | 14.600 (sd 0.409, n = 15) | 16.354 (sd 2.00, n = 15) |
| ELP-R | 14.598 (sd 0.408, n = 15) | 17.369 (sd 1.42, n = 13) |

- **MVIS repeats 3–5× tighter:** across recordings, compared with Kalibr's rolling-shutter model.
- **One camera at a time:** Kalibr's rolling-shutter model has no ELP stereo baseline.
- **Lens models differ:** MVIS uses the equidistant model on the ELP, Kalibr radtan. So we compare only the
  readout here.
- **Kalibr was not re-run:** after the ELP stamp correction.
- **With the IMU:** the MVIS readout spread drops to about 0.1 ms (section 1).

**Camera–IMU, mixed rig (T265 fisheye 1 + ELP-L + two IMUs):**

| Camera | MVIS median reprojection (px) | Kalibr median reprojection (px) |
|---|---|---|
| ELP-L | 0.181 (rolling shutter) | 0.795 (taken as global shutter) |
| T265 fisheye 1 | 0.200 | 0.367 |

- **Kalibr's camera–IMU solver is global shutter only:** it takes ELP-L as global, with readout fixed at 0.
- **Not a clean on/off test:** MVIS uses two corners per tag, non-base cameras at 4 Hz or lower and an
  [estimated board shape](/why/board-shape/). Kalibr uses none of these.
- **Part of the gap is settings:** the gap on the global-shutter T265 suggests part of it comes from those
  settings.

**Conclusion:**

- **Model the shutter:** the ELP is rolling shutter, and calling it global makes the fit worse.
- **The error goes into $t_d$:** it moves by about half the readout, with no warning.
- **MVIS repeats the readout:** it is tighter than Kalibr across recordings, and tighter still with the IMU.

## Limits

<div class="not-content rp-honest">
  <ul>
    <li><b>Camera only and camera–IMU disagree:</b> MVIS gets 14.6 ms from camera only and 16.6 ms with the
      IMU, about 2 ms apart.</li>
    <li><b>Readout needs help to be seen:</b> it needs a high enough frame rate or IMU support. On a low-rate
      camera with no IMU, the readout can follow the solver's starting value, not the data.</li>
    <li><b>Check repeatability, not one run:</b> calibrate several recordings and compare the readout. A value
      that moves a lot between recordings is not well observed.</li>
    <li><b>A round trip cannot test it:</b> <code>mvis apply-calib</code> corrects frame stamps only. It does
      not unwarp image rows, so a <a href="/guides/roundtrip/">round-trip validation</a> says nothing about the
      readout.</li>
    <li><b>Older A/B code:</b> the on/off test in section 2 is one recording on October 2026 code.</li>
  </ul>
</div>
