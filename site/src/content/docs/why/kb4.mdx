This page shows why `kb4` is the MVIS fisheye model and `equidistant` is not. You need nothing to run here; the
numbers come from our runs on the Intel T265 ([VI-Rig 5 IMUs + 4 cams](/datasets/virig-5imus-4cams/)),
[TUM-VI](/benchmark/results/tum-vi/) and TUM-Fisheye.

**The answer first:**

- **One polynomial:** below 90° off axis the two formulas are identical. Fitted separately on the T265, they agree
  to about $2\times10^{-6}$ px.
- **Past 90°, only kb4 projects:** `equidistant` has no projection for rays at or past 90°; `kb4` has one.
- **Equidistant stalled on the one past-90° lens we tested:** on BF2M, up to 97° off axis, it stayed at its
  starting values, 15–24 px off TUM in $f_x$, even on a recording where it dropped no corner.
- **kb4 converged there:** from the same start, `kb4` came within 0.4 px of TUM in $f_x$. We have not found why
  equidistant stalls.
- **No cost below 90°:** `fisheye` and `kannala_brandt` mean `kb4`.
- **Kalibr export:** a camera–IMU result with `kb4` exports to Kalibr as `pinhole-equi`, exact for rays in front
  of the camera. On export MVIS warns when the fit used corners past 90°, which Kalibr cannot project.
- **Fold warning:** MVIS warns when the kb4 polynomial folds inside the image, as it does on BF2M: no ray lands
  past about 712–743 px from the centre.

How to choose it: [Choose a fisheye model (T265 example)](/guides/camera-calibration/#3-choose-a-fisheye-model-for-the-t265).

## The two models

Both models map the angle $\theta$ of a ray off the optical axis to a distance $r$ from the principal point, with
the same four coefficients:

$$
r = f\,\theta\,(1 + k_1\theta^2 + k_2\theta^4 + k_3\theta^6 + k_4\theta^8)
$$

They differ only in how they get $\theta$ from a 3D point $(x, y, z)$:

| Model | $\theta$ from | Rays at or past 90° ($z \le 0$) |
|---|---|---|
| `equidistant` (Kalibr `pinhole-equi`) | $\theta = \arctan(\sqrt{x^2+y^2}/z)$ | Cannot be projected |
| `kb4` (Kannala–Brandt, as in Basalt) | $\theta = \operatorname{atan2}(\sqrt{x^2+y^2},\, z)$ | Projected, up to the polynomial's first fold |

## 1. Below 90°, they are the same

Where few or no corners pass 90°, the two models give the same calibration:

| Data | Solves | kb4 − equidistant |
|---|---|---|
| Intel T265, VI-Rig, 7 recordings, camera-only | 28 pairs | mapping gap p95 ≤ $1.9\times10^{-6}$ px, intrinsics ≤ $7.8\times10^{-7}$ px |
| Intel T265, VI-Rig, 7 recordings, camera–IMU | 28 pairs | rotation ≤ $1.0\times10^{-11}$ rad, lever ≤ $1.9\times10^{-9}$ mm, $t_d$ ≤ $1.5\times10^{-10}$ ms |
| TUM-VI, imu1–4, camera–IMU | 8 pairs | rotation ≤ $2.2\times10^{-13}$ rad |
| TUM-Fisheye, 4 lenses, 12 recordings; largest corner 58–89° by TUM's calibration | 24 pairs, one and all corners | same $f_x$ to 0.01 px |

- **Older results:** published T265 results solved with `equidistant` hold for `kb4` too.
- **Code:** MVIS main `644f7a2` and `197549a`, not re-run on newer code.

## 2. On a lens past 90°, equidistant stalled

- **Lens:** the TUM-Fisheye BF2M2020S23 sees the board up to 94–97° off axis, by MVIS's kb4 fit.
- **Corners:** each recording has 13–19 thousand detected corners, 11.8–18.3 thousand of them in the kb4 solve.
- **Reference:** TUM's own calibrations of the same images. They are another tool's results, not ground truth.

| Recording | Largest angle (kb4) | equidistant $f_x$ − TUM | kb4 $f_x$ − TUM | Mean error, equidistant / kb4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 94.0° | **+23.88 px** | +0.06 px | 1.35 / 0.16 px |
| 2 | 97.0° | **+15.51 px** | +0.31 px | 0.95 / 0.20 px |
| 3 | 96.1° | **+22.07 px** | +0.33 px | 1.48 / 0.20 px |

All corners, camera-only, MVIS main `f902d39`, 2026-11-11.

- **Two corners per tag:** with the MVIS default, the equidistant intrinsics are the same, mean error within
  0.01 px. kb4 is +0.12 to +0.39 px off TUM in $f_x$.
- **Same start:** both models start from $f_x = f_y$ = 404.98 / 396.51 / 402.95 px, the principal point at the
  image centre (639.5, 511.5) and zero distortion.
- **What happens:** kb4 moves from there. Equidistant does not: the solver gives up at the first step with the
  error unchanged, so every intrinsic ends at its starting value. The 15–24 px $f_x$ offsets are that start.
- **Where it gives up:** every trial step raises the cost. On recording 1 the cost is 15,541 at the start, and
  85,047 down to 42,972 for the trial steps as the damping $\lambda$ rises from $10^{-5}$ to $10^{1}$.
- **The damping cap:** the solver stops at MVIS's cap, $\lambda = 10^{2}$; GTSAM's default is $10^{5}$. We have
  not tested whether a higher cap lets equidistant converge.
- **kb4's other intrinsics:** $c_x$ is 0.30–0.48 px low, $c_y$ within 0.1 px and $f_y$ within 0.37 px of TUM.
- **Corners past 90° are not the whole story:** equidistant's stalled fit drops only 0, 3 and 1 corners as past
  90°. On recording 1, at its starting values, it puts no corner past 90°, where kb4's fit puts its largest at
  94°. It still stalls the same way.
- **Cause unknown:** we have not tested whether the corners near or past 90° block the first step.
- **Past-90° warning:** MVIS warns when corners lie at or past 90° with `equidistant` and suggests kb4. That fires
  on recordings 2 and 3.
- **No stall warning:** nothing in MVIS flags the stall. On recording 1 the only signs are the solver library's
  "Levenberg-Marquardt giving up" line, an error that does not change (15541.4565 -> 15541.4565), and a mean
  error about 8× kb4's.

## 3. Earlier runs on BF2M

MVIS `197549a`, 2026-11-04, not repeated on the section 2 code.

- **Corners past 90°:** by TUM's calibration, 36, 37 and 45 corners lie past 90°. We counted them on the frames
  of the kb4 all-corner run, which has the same corner totals as the kb4 solve in section 2.
- **Same stall then:** the three all-corner equidistant solves gave the same values to 4 decimals.
- **One corner per tag converged:** with one corner per tag, equidistant converged on the same recordings:
  $f_x$ −0.00 / +0.91 / +2.72 px from TUM, kb4 −0.00 / +0.14 / +0.15.
- **The stall depends on the corner set:** on the other four TUM-Fisheye lenses equidistant converged with one
  and with all corners (section 1).
- **Kalibr did not do better:** its `pinhole-equi` failed to converge on all 3 BF2M recordings, even seeded with
  TUM's $f_x$, and on 1 of the 12 other recordings.
- **Kalibr DS and EUCM converged:** on all 3, after a patched initial guess on a second attempt.

## 4. Choosing kb4 costs nothing below 90°

- **Same pixels below 90°:** section 1 shows it, so nothing changes on a lens that never gets there.
- **Kalibr export:** a camera–IMU result with `kb4` is written to Kalibr's camchain-imucam as `pinhole-equi`,
  with the same $k_1$–$k_4$. It is exact for rays in front of the camera.
- **Camera-only:** camera-only results have no Kalibr export.
- **The fold is checked:** a KB polynomial can turn back, so $r$ stops growing with $\theta$. MVIS reports the
  fold angle of every fit and flags corners past it.
- **TUM-VI folds beyond every corner:** with all corners, 13 of 16 fits fold inside the image, 12 of 16 with two
  corners per tag. The smallest margin is 22.2°, so no corner lies past the fold, but MVIS warns that the fold is
  inside the image.
- **BF2M folds inside the image too:** every kb4 fit folds at 120–128°, 712–743 px from the principal point; the
  image corner is 822 px away. No corner lies past the fold (the largest is at 97°), but pixels beyond that
  radius have no ray, and MVIS warns about it in every run.

**Conclusion:**

- **Same where both work:** `kb4` is the same model as `equidistant` where both work.
- **kb4 keeps working past 90°:** on the one past-90° lens we tested, where equidistant stalled.
- **Default:** `fisheye` and `kannala_brandt` mean `kb4`. Use `equidistant` only when you want that name, on a
  lens whose corners stay under 90°.

## Limits

<div class="not-content rp-honest">
  <ul>
    <li><b>Past-90° evidence is thin:</b> one lens, BF2M, with 3 recordings and 36–45 corners past 90° each, plus
      TUM-VI.</li>
    <li><b>TUM-VI counts are not re-checked:</b> there, 96 corners past 90° are detected and about 40 reach the
      solve; Basalt detects 2,481. These counts come from a one-off survey with our own solver.</li>
    <li><b>Reference:</b> TUM's calibration, not ground truth.</li>
    <li><b>Why equidistant stalls:</b> not found. At its starting values, recording 1's fit puts no corner past
      90°.</li>
    <li><b>Another start:</b> not tested, such as starting equidistant from TUM's fx.</li>
    <li><b>A higher damping cap:</b> not tested. MVIS stops at lambda 10<sup>2</sup>; GTSAM's default is
      10<sup>5</sup>.</li>
    <li><b>Older runs:</b> the one-corner-per-tag runs, where equidistant converged, and the section 1 rows were
      not re-run on the section 2 code.</li>
    <li><b>The fold downstream:</b> how much the kb4 fold near the image corners matters downstream is not
      tested.</li>
    <li><b>Who ran it:</b> the MVIS authors ran every solve.</li>
  </ul>
</div>
