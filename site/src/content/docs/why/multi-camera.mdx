Use this page to see what you gain from putting several cameras into one solve. You need nothing to follow along.
Data: every number here comes from the [VI-Rig 4 IMUs + 4 cams](/reference/datasets/virig-4imus-4cams/) dataset.

**The answer first:**

- **Sub-mm extrinsics:** Solving all cameras in one graph gives camera-to-camera extrinsics that repeat to a
  fraction of a millimetre over 15 recordings.
- **One $t_d$ per camera:** Each camera gets its own camera–IMU time offset $t_d$. The cameras on this rig are
  about 116–118 ms apart.
- **Wide helps narrow:** A narrow camera alone pins its own lever arm poorly. Adding the other cameras tightens it.
- **Not every rig bundles:** A global-shutter camera and an unsynchronised rolling-shutter camera are better
  solved one by one in the camera-only step.

How to turn it on: [Solve the cameras in pairs](/guides/camera-calibration/#1-solve-the-cameras-in-pairs), then
join them in [Camera–IMU calibration](/guides/camera-imu-calibration/).

## The setup

The rig has two camera pairs:

- **T265 L / R:** global-shutter fisheye, 848×800, 30 Hz.
- **ELP L / R:** rolling-shutter, 640×480, 25 Hz.

## 1. Cameras do not share a clock

[Example run](/benchmark/examples/virig-4imus-4cams/) on recording 01: all 4 cameras and 4 IMUs in one camera–IMU solve.

| Camera | $t_d$ (ms) |
|---|---|
| T265 L (cam0) | −5.41 |
| T265 R (cam1) | −7.47 |
| ELP L (cam2) | −123.25 |
| ELP R (cam3) | −123.30 |

- **Pairs agree:** Inside each pair the offsets are close.
- **Pairs disagree:** The ELP pair sits about 116–118 ms behind the T265 pair.
- **One shared offset fails:** One shared $t_d$ for the rig would be wrong by over 100 ms for one pair. Give each
  camera its own $t_d$.

## 2. Extrinsics repeat to a fraction of a millimetre

Public results over 15 recordings, batch 2026-11-08a ([full tables](/benchmark/results/virig-4imus-4cams/)).

Median and standard deviation (sd) of the camera-to-camera distance:

| Camera pair | Family | MVIS | Kalibr | Basalt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T265 L – T265 R | T1, camera only | 63.663 mm (sd 0.034) | 63.468 mm (sd 0.099) | 63.555 mm (sd 0.135) |
| ELP L – ELP R | T8, camera–IMU | 59.207 mm (sd 0.084) | not run | — |
| T265 L – ELP L | T7, camera–IMU | 153.50 mm (sd 0.17), 14 of 15 recordings | — | — |

- **T265 pair:** The MVIS spread is about 3× tighter than Kalibr and 4× tighter than Basalt.
- **Mixed pair:** Even a fisheye and a rolling-shutter camera on different clocks repeat to 0.17 mm.
- **Kalibr in T7:** Kalibr's camera baselines there are inputs from an MVIS export, so we do not compare them here.

## 3. A wide camera fixes a narrow one

- **Narrow view:** The ELP sees about 92° across. A board seen by it alone constrains the lever arm weakly along
  some axes (section 3).
- **Wide view:** The T265 fisheyes see the board over a much wider range of angles. In one graph their poses pin
  the rig, and the ELP gains from it.
- **Time offsets across lenses:** On fisheye + narrow rigs, plain time-offset estimation often fails. MVIS then
  uses two independent estimators: PnP-based and homography cross-correlation. See
  [What the pipeline does](/guides/camera-calibration/#what-the-pipeline-does).

## 4. When not to bundle

Joint is not always better. Two cases:

- **Hybrid rigs:** We tested a global-shutter camera plus an unsynchronised rolling-shutter camera, T265 L + ELP L.
  On recordings 03–05 a joint camera-only bundle gave:
  - 2.4–2.5 px rms reprojection
  - readout scatter of 15–19 ms
  - lever z shifts of 5 mm

  One mono solve per camera gave 0.43 px and 0.1 ms readout scatter.
- **What we do:** We solve each such camera on its own in the camera-only step. Then we join them in the
  camera–IMU solve, which brings the extrinsics and the $t_d$ of each camera.
- **Synced pairs are fine:** Hardware-synced, same-shutter pairs such as ELP L + R are fine as a joint camera-only
  solve.
- **A bad camera can pull a solve:** In an older test, we added ELP L to a Kalibr T265 run as a global-shutter
  camera. It pulled the T265 gyro scale up about 3 % on two axes. We later traced the cause to the ELP frame
  stamps. Check each camera's residuals before you trust the joint result.

**Conclusion:**

- **Own offset:** Give each camera its own $t_d$.
- **Bundle what shares a clock and a shutter:** Solve hybrid cameras one by one in the camera-only step and join
  them in the camera–IMU solve.
- **Check residuals:** One bad camera can pull the joint result.
