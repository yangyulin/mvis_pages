Use this page to see what you gain from putting several cameras into one solve.
You need nothing to follow along. Every number here comes from the
[VI-Rig 4 IMUs + 4 cams](/reference/datasets/virig-4imus-4cams/) dataset.

**The short answer:**

- **Sub-mm extrinsics:** solving all cameras in one graph gives camera-to-camera
  extrinsics that repeat to a fraction of a millimetre over 15 recordings.
- **One td per camera:** each camera gets its own time offset td (camera–IMU
  time offset). The cameras on this rig are about 116–118 ms apart.
- **Wide helps narrow:** a narrow camera alone pins its own lever arm poorly.
  Adding the other cameras tightens it.

The rig has two camera pairs:

- **T265 L / R:** global-shutter fisheye, 848×800, 30 Hz
- **ELP L / R:** rolling-shutter, 640×480, 25 Hz

## 1. Cameras do not share a clock

From the [example run](/benchmark/examples/virig-4imus-4cams/) on bag 01, with
all 4 cameras and 4 IMUs in one cam-IMU solve:

| Camera | td (ms) |
|---|---|
| T265 L (cam0) | −5.41 |
| T265 R (cam1) | −7.47 |
| ELP L (cam2) | −123.25 |
| ELP R (cam3) | −123.30 |

- **Pairs agree:** inside each pair the offsets are close.
- **Pairs disagree:** the ELP pair sits about 116–118 ms behind the T265 pair.
- **So:** one shared td for the rig would be wrong by over 100 ms for one pair.
  Give each camera its own td.

## 2. Extrinsics repeat

Public results over 15 recordings, batch 2026-11-08a
([full tables](/benchmark/results/virig-4imus-4cams/)). Median and standard
deviation (sd) of the camera-to-camera distance:

| Camera pair | Family | MVIS | Kalibr | Basalt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T265 L – T265 R | T1, camera only | 63.663 mm (sd 0.034) | 63.468 mm (sd 0.099) | 63.555 mm (sd 0.135) |
| ELP L – ELP R | T8, cam-IMU | 59.207 mm (sd 0.084) | not run | — |
| T265 L – ELP L | T7, cam-IMU | 153.50 mm (sd 0.17), 14 of 15 bags | — | — |

- **T265 pair:** the MVIS spread is about 3× tighter than Kalibr and 4× tighter
  than Basalt.
- **Mixed pair:** even a fisheye and a rolling-shutter camera on different
  clocks repeat to 0.17 mm.
- **Note:** in T7, Kalibr's camera baselines are inputs from an MVIS export, so
  we do not compare them here.

## 3. A wide camera fixes a narrow one

- **Narrow view:** the ELP sees about 92° across. A board seen by it alone
  constrains the lever arm weakly along some axes (section 3).
- **Wide view:** the T265 fisheyes see the board over a much wider range of
  angles. In one graph their poses pin the rig, and the ELP gains from it.
- **Time offsets across lenses:** on fisheye + narrow rigs, plain time-offset
  estimation often fails. MVIS then uses two independent estimators, PnP-based
  and homography cross-correlation. See
  [What the pipeline does](/guides/camera-calibration/#what-the-pipeline-does).

## 4. When not to bundle

Joint is not always better. Two cases:

- **Hybrid rigs:** a global-shutter camera plus an unsynchronised
  rolling-shutter camera (T265 L + ELP L here). On bags 03–05 a joint
  camera-only bundle gave:
  - 2.4–2.5 px rms reprojection
  - readout scatter of 15–19 ms
  - lever z shifts of 5 mm

  One mono solve per camera gave 0.43 px and 0.1 ms readout scatter.
- **What we do:** solve each such camera on its own in the camera-only step.
  Then join them in the cam-IMU solve, which brings the extrinsics and the td of
  each camera. Hardware-synced, same-shutter pairs (ELP L + R) are fine as a
  joint camera-only solve.
- **A bad camera can pull a solve:** in an older test, adding ELP L (as a
  global-shutter camera) to a Kalibr T265 run pulled the T265 gyro scale up
  about 3 % on two axes. The cause was later traced to the ELP frame stamps.
  Check each camera's residuals before you trust the joint result.

## Set it up

- **Camera-only:** one config per synced pair. List its cameras under
  `input:` → `cameras:` and set `reference_camera`. See
  [Camera-only calibration](/guides/camera-calibration/).
- **Cam-IMU:** the joint solve with every camera and IMU. It gives the
  extrinsics and td between the pairs. See
  [Camera–IMU calibration](/guides/camera-imu-calibration/).
