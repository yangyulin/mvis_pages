This page shows why MVIS can solve the shape of the calibration board, and what a flat solve does on our board.
Data: our VI-Rig recordings on the Hub, [`calibench-vi/datasets`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/calibench-vi/datasets).

**The answer first:**

- **Our board is not flat:** our AprilGrid on a panel is a saddle 10–11 mm peak to peak on this VI-Rig mount.
- **A flat solve hides it:** camera-only $f_x$ comes out 0.4–1.0 % low on average. Camera–IMU puts the gyro
  scale $\mathbf{D}_w$ about 1.1 % high and a lever about 9 mm off. No check fails; only the camera-only
  `board_point_bias` diagnostic hints at it.
- **What MVIS solves:** `estimate.board_points: true` adds one height per corner, in camera-only and camera–IMU
  runs. Kalibr and Basalt treat the board as flat.
- **The cost:** 144 more unknowns. On these runs the solve took no longer, and $f_x$ repeated no worse.
- **Off by default:** we turn it on for VI-Rig.

How to turn it on: [A board is never flat](/guides/board-setup/#4-a-board-is-never-flat).

## The setup

- **Board:** an AprilGrid with 6 × 6 tags of 88 mm, 144 corners.
- **Recordings:** the [VI-Rig 4 IMUs + 4 cameras](/datasets/virig-4imus-4cams/) set, recordings 1–15, all on one
  mount of the board.
- **Code:** every solve in sections 1–4 ran on MVIS main `f902d39` (2026-11-11), unless a line says otherwise.
- **Lever:** the translation between a camera and the IMU.

## 1. The board is a saddle

We estimated the height of every corner four ways, all on recordings 3–5 with all corners:

- **ELP left camera**, rolling shutter, camera-only.
- **T265 fisheye 1**, global shutter, camera-only.
- **ELP stereo pair**, camera-only.
- **Camera–IMU:** the ELP stereo pair with the T265 and Xsens IMUs. This one also ran on recordings 1–15 with
  two corners per tag, for the recording-to-recording row.

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Out of plane, peak to peak (all-corner estimates) | 10.5–11.0 mm |
| Out of plane, rms (all-corner estimates) | 2.3–2.4 mm |
| Shape | A ridge across the middle (+3 to +4.5 mm); two opposite corners sag to −5 to −6 mm |
| Agreement between the four estimates | 0.04–0.32 mm rms, correlation 0.991–0.9999 |
| Recording to recording | 0.05–0.16 mm rms; recordings 1–2 sit about 0.4 mm from the 15-recording mean |

- **Two corners per tag:** on this code these solves report 1.6–2.7 mm rms and 9.1–11.7 mm peak to peak over
  all 144 heights. Half of those heights have no data (section 4), so the first two rows leave them out. Since
  2026-11-12 MVIS main writes the rms and peak to peak over the observed corners only, with a per-corner count
  `n_obs`, in the camera-only board shape output.
- **Agreement:** the three estimates that use the ELP left camera agree within 0.04–0.13 mm. The T265 estimate,
  from another camera and lens, is 0.23–0.32 mm from them. Each comparison removes the best-fit plane first.
- **Not low-order:** a biquadratic surface still leaves 0.9–1.0 mm rms, a biquartic 0.5 mm. A few bending terms
  cannot replace one height per corner.
- **Per mount:** the shape depends on the mount. On another mount, the 5 IMUs + 4 cameras recordings, an older
  run estimated 7.3 mm peak to peak. That shape correlates only 0.60 with the stored 15-recording shape of this
  mount.

## 2. Camera-only: a flat board pulls the focal length down

Recordings 3–5, equidistant model, all 144 corners. We solved each once with the shape and once with a rigid flat
board:

| Camera | Board | Reprojection rms (px) | $f_x$ (px), mean ± std over 3 recordings | $c_y$ (px) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ELP left (rolling shutter) | shape | **0.445** | 428.12 ± 0.88 | 267.32 ± 0.28 |
| | flat | 0.614 | 426.58 ± 1.17 | 270.57 ± 0.75 |
| T265 fisheye 1 (global shutter) | shape | **0.427** | 286.72 ± 0.44 | 404.63 ± 0.47 |
| | flat | 0.573 | 284.29 ± 0.63 | 406.63 ± 0.90 |

- **Residual:** the shape cuts it by 27.5 % on the ELP and 25.5 % on the T265. Across all camera-only arms,
  including two corners per tag and stereo, the cut is 25–29 %.
- **Focal length:** flat reads $f_x$ 0.36 % low on the ELP and 0.85 % low on the T265. It is low on every
  recording, by 0.25–0.48 % and 0.77–0.93 %.
- **Two corners per tag:** the $f_x$ gaps are 0.42 % and 0.99 %, up to 1.09 % on one recording.
- **Principal point:** flat also moves $c_y$, by +3.3 px on the ELP and +2.0 px on the T265.
- **Stereo:** with two corners per tag, the ELP stereo pair reads $f_x$ 0.48 % low flat. Flat also moves its
  baseline by 0.3 %, from 59.23 to 59.04 mm, the stereo rotation by 1.2–1.8 mrad as log(R), mostly about y, and
  the rolling-shutter readout by 0.16 ms.
- **With the IMU:** in the camera–IMU solves of section 3, on recordings 3–5, ELP left $f_x$ with the shape is
  427.9 px with two corners per tag and 428.2 px with all corners. Camera-only gives 428.1 px, so all are within
  0.2 px.
- **Smaller flat bias with the IMU:** there the flat-board $f_x$ bias is 0.1–0.2 %: +0.4 ± 0.6 px over 15
  recordings, with the opposite sign on 3 of them, and +0.8 px with all corners on recordings 3–5.

## 3. Camera–IMU: a flat board shows up as gyro scale and lever

15 recordings of the ELP stereo pair with the T265 and Xsens IMUs, two corners per tag. We solved each once with
the shape, starting from an earlier 7.7 mm estimate of the same board, and once with a rigid flat board. A good
gyroscope reads a scale $\mathbf{D}_w$ close to 1.

Mean ± std over the 15 recordings, range in brackets:

| Quantity | Flat board | With the shape |
|---|---|---|
| T265 gyro scale x | 1.0117 ± 0.0029 [1.0061–1.0161] | **1.0000 ± 0.0011** [0.9986–1.0021] |
| T265 gyro scale y | 1.0148 ± 0.0029 [1.0084–1.0188] | **1.0034 ± 0.0012** [1.0016–1.0058] |
| T265 gyro scale z | 1.0046 ± 0.0007 [1.0035–1.0066] | 1.0039 ± 0.0003 [1.0034–1.0044] |
| Xsens gyro scale x | 1.0115 ± 0.0032 [1.0050–1.0163] | **1.0008 ± 0.0012** [0.9991–1.0033] |
| ELP left lever z | −4.6 ± 3.3 mm [−8.5 to 0.9] | **4.6 ± 1.3 mm** [1.9 to 6.7] |
| Reprojection rms | 0.61 ± 0.03 px [0.57–0.65] | **0.33 ± 0.02 px** [0.30–0.39] |

- **Gyro scale:** with the shape, every axis of both IMUs is within 0.6 % of 1. The largest is T265 y, 1.0058,
  on recording 11; the Xsens stays within 0.33 %.
- **Per recording:** the shape lowers T265 x and y by 1.17 ± 0.28 % and 1.13 ± 0.23 %, and Xsens x by
  1.07 ± 0.30 %, with the same sign on all 15.
- **T265 z:** about 0.4 % high either way.
- **Accelerometer:** the T265 accel scale $\mathbf{D}_a$ moves by −0.31 ± 0.20 % in z; x and y barely move.
- **Lever:** the shape moves ELP left lever z by +9.3 ± 2.7 mm per recording. The lever also repeats better,
  std 1.3 mm against 3.3 mm flat.
- **Time offsets:** $t_{ga}$ moves by −0.18 ms and $t_d$ by under 0.1 ms.
- **Start value and corner set:** on recordings 3–5 we also solved all 144 corners from a flat start, instead of
  two per tag from the 7.7 mm estimate. The answer is nearly the same: gyro scale within about 0.1 % and lever z
  within 1 mm (on average 0.08 % and 0.9 mm), at about 1.5× the solve time. Both changed at once, so this run
  does not separate them.
- **No alarm:** the flat solves converged, and their 0.61 px residual failed no check. In the camera-only runs of
  section 2, the per-corner `board_point_bias` shows the twist: median 0.15–0.27 px flat against 0.06–0.13 px
  with the shape. Camera–IMU output has no such diagnostic.
- **Other mount:** an older run used 7 recordings of the 5 IMUs + 4 cameras rig, 4 cameras each, with the EUCM
  and kb4 lens models. Turning the shape off rotated the camera extrinsics by a median 6.8 mrad (log(R), after
  aligning the two solutions as a whole; 7.4–8.0 mrad as solved). It moved the lever by 1.3–2.2 mm and $c_y$ by
  1.8 px. That run compared extrinsics only, not gyro scale or $f_x$.

## 4. The cost, and when to turn it on

- **More unknowns:** 144 heights, each with a weak 3 mm prior (`board_height_sigma: 0.003`, in m).
- **Solve time:** not longer on these runs. Camera-only with all corners, means of 3 on 2 threads, shape against
  flat: ELP 21.7 against 22.7 min, T265 12.1 against 13.9 min, ELP stereo 34.0 against 33.9 min. Camera–IMU:
  +3 ± 35 s on 10–14 min solves.
- **$f_x$ repeatability:** no worse with the shape. Camera-only with all corners, the scatter with the shape is
  0.55–0.93× the flat scatter: ELP 0.88 against 1.17 px, T265 0.44 against 0.63 px. Three recordings are too few
  to rank. Camera–IMU over 15 recordings: 0.62 against 0.91 px.
- **Earlier study:** a study on development code found $f_x$ repeating 2–4× worse with the shape. Its flat runs
  let each corner move under a 1 mm prior. These runs do not show that.
- **Two corners per tag see half the heights:** with `corner_subsample: two_anti` a solve observes 72 of the 144
  corners. The other 72 heights have no data and stay near their start values.
- **Where two corners per tag is set:** it is the camera-only default, and our camera–IMU configs set it too. The
  camera–IMU default is `all`.
- **Default:** MVIS keeps `estimate.board_points` off. We turn it on for every VI-Rig solve, camera-only and
  camera–IMU; our other rigs keep it off.
- **When to turn it on:** when your board is measurably not flat. Signs: a smooth camera-only `board_point_bias`
  pattern across the board, or a gap under a straightedge. With the shape on, the MVIS report (main since
  2026-11-12) adds a Board Shape section with a map of the heights.
- **Start value:** a camera–IMU run can start from an earlier estimate with `board.heights_file`, for example the
  `<output>_board_shape.yaml` a previous run wrote. Camera-only runs do not read it.

**Conclusion:**

- **A flat solve is biased and silent:** on this mount it is off by 0.4–1.0 % in camera-only $f_x$, about 1.1 %
  in gyro scale and about 9 mm in lever, and no check fails.
- **The shape fits better:** the residual drops by about a quarter and the gyro scales sit within 0.6 % of 1, so
  we read the flat numbers as biased.
- **It is free here:** on these runs the shape cost no repeatability and no solve time.

## Limits

<div class="not-content rp-honest">
  <ul>
    <li><b>No measured reference shape:</b> the four estimates agree with each other, but none was checked
      against a metrology scan of the board.</li>
    <li><b>Our own runs:</b> the MVIS authors ran every solve.</li>
    <li><b>Gyro near 1 is a prior, not a measurement:</b> the same T265 reads its accel scale about 2 % high
      with or without the shape.</li>
    <li><b>One board, one mount:</b> sections 1–4 use one mount on one rig. The other mount appears only in the
      shape comparison of section 1 and the extrinsic shifts at the end of section 3, both from one older run.
      We did not test other mounts.</li>
    <li><b>Small camera-only sets:</b> section 2 has 3 recordings per camera, with the equidistant model only.</li>
    <li><b>Stereo settings:</b> the stereo runs used the regression settings (two corners per tag, 5 Hz with the
      reference camera at 10 Hz, 15 iterations), not the mono ones (10 Hz, 30 iterations).</li>
    <li><b>Not re-run with the shape on and off:</b> the camera–IMU setup that also puts the T265 camera in the
      solve.</li>
    <li><b>Untested cause:</b> why the flat-board fx bias is smaller in camera–IMU than in camera-only runs.</li>
    <li><b>Solve times:</b> from 2-thread runs on a shared, loaded machine.</li>
  </ul>
</div>
