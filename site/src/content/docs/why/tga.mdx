Use this page to see what a camera–IMU calibration does when the IMU's gyroscope and accelerometer are not in
step. Data: the Looper and the Intel T265 of [VI-Rig](/reference/datasets/virig-4imus-4cams/), with the
swept recordings on the Hub, [`calibench-vi/datasets`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/calibench-vi/datasets).

**The answer first:**

- **Real IMUs have a $t_{ga}$:** MVIS measures a few ms on the Looper and on the T265 IMU.
- **Tools without it move $t_d$:** Kalibr and Basalt treat the IMU as one clock, so their $t_d$ takes a steady
  share of $t_{ga}$.
- **No warning:** every run of every tool converged.
- **MVIS keeps $t_d$ flat:** it reports the delay as $t_{ga}$, and its IMU scales barely change.
- **A corrected dataset needs it:** without $t_{ga}$, a round trip leaves a camera time error.

How to turn it on: [Estimate `t_ga`](/guides/camera-imu-calibration/#awkward-imus). MVIS fits $t_{ga}$ by default
(`solve.estimate_t_ga: true`, with a weak 10 ms prior, `sigma_t_ga: 0.010`), as in these runs.

## The setup

- **$t_{ga}$:** the time offset between the gyroscope and the accelerometer inside one IMU.
- **$t_d$:** the time offset between the camera and the IMU.
- **Who fits what:** MVIS fits both. Kalibr and Basalt fit only $t_d$: they treat the IMU as one clock.
- **The question:** what happens to a camera–IMU calibration when the IMU has a $t_{ga}$?

## 1. With t_ga = 0, the three tools agree

- **Two datasets:** Looper Insight 9 (looper_1) and Intel T265 (VI-Rig april_01).
- **Prepared so $t_{ga}$ is 0:** MVIS calibrated each raw recording. We then resampled the camera and IMU streams
  with that result. So on the new recording, by MVIS's estimate, $t_d$ = 0, $t_{ga}$ = 0 and the IMU scales are 1.
- **Settings:** each tool's usual settings. T265: camera intrinsics pinned. Looper: intrinsics free.
- **Result:** all three tools read $t_d$ within 0.3 ms of 0. The IMU scales are within 0.4 % of 1, except Basalt
  on the T265.

<TgaTables which="zero" />

- **Basalt on the T265:** it reads gyro x and accel z about 1.2 % and 1.5 % from 1.
- **Not caused by $t_{ga}$:** that offset is already there with no delay.

## 2. Without a t_ga, td takes a steady share of the delay

- **Sweep:** we delayed only the accel by $\tau$ = −50, −20, −10, −5, −2, 0, 2, 5, 10, 20 and 50 ms. Everything
  else is unchanged. That is 11 recordings per device, and the true $t_{ga}$ in each is $\tau$.
- **Run:** all three tools on every recording, with the same settings as above.

<TgaSweepPlot />

- **MVIS:** it reports the delay as $t_{ga}$, with slope {L.slope.mvis_tga.toFixed(2)} on both devices. Its $t_d$
  stays flat, and its IMU scales change by under 0.01 %.
- **Kalibr and Basalt:** they have no $t_{ga}$, so their $t_d$ takes a steady share of $\tau$.
- **What a share means:** a share of {L.slope.kalibr.toFixed(2)} means $t_d$ moves {L.slope.kalibr.toFixed(2)} ms
  for every 1 ms of $t_{ga}$.
- **The shares:** {L.slope.kalibr.toFixed(2)} for Kalibr and {L.slope.basalt.toFixed(2)} for Basalt on Looper;
  {T.slope.kalibr.toFixed(2)} and {T.slope.basalt.toFixed(2)} on the T265.
- **Their IMU scales drift too:** mostly under 0.2 % within ±10 ms, and up to about 1.8 % at ±50 ms. Basalt on
  the T265 reaches accel 0.3 % at ±2 ms.
- **No warning:** every run of every tool converged.
- **Not a bug:** with one IMU clock, the closest fit puts $t_d$ somewhere between the gyro and the accel.
- **Where it lands:** that depends on the recording, likely on how much the gyro and the accel each drive the
  fit. We have not tested this.

## 3. Real devices have a t_ga

- **Measured:** MVIS reads $t_{ga}$ on the raw recordings as **{ms(L.raw.mvis_tga)} ms** on Looper and
  **{ms(T.raw.mvis_tga)} ms** on the T265.
- **The effect:** a tool without $t_{ga}$ moves $t_d$ by its share of that.

## 4. On the running example, only the T265 IMU has a large t_ga

The guides use the VI-Rig recording [virig-4imus-4cams](/reference/datasets/virig-4imus-4cams/). It has four IMUs on
one rig, so it shows $t_{ga}$ on four devices at once.

- **One run, recording 01:** MVIS solved all four cameras and all four IMUs together, with $t_{ga}$ on, as in
  [step 4](/guides/camera-imu-calibration/). The Xsens is the base IMU.
- **$t_{ga}$ per IMU:** ± 0.02 ms on each, from the run's result table.

- **Only the T265 IMU has a large offset.** The other three read within 1 ms of 0.
- **It repeats over 15 recordings:** in the [results](/benchmark/results/virig-4imus-4cams/), the T265 IMU reads
  {sgn(tga15('T7').med, 3)} ms, sd {tga15('T7').sd.toFixed(3)}, in T7 and {sgn(tga15('T8').med, 3)} ms,
  sd {tga15('T8').sd.toFixed(3)}, in T8. In those two runs the T265 IMU is the base.

## 5. Without t_ga, a round trip leaves a td error

Older MVIS code; the date is in Data.

- **Setup:** recording 01, T265 cameras and the T265 IMU, camera intrinsics pinned. We ran a
  [round trip](/guides/roundtrip/) twice: calibrate, correct the recording, calibrate again. The only change was
  $t_{ga}$ on or off.
- **Both runs pass:** the IMU scales and biases go back to 1 and 0 in both.
- **With $t_{ga}$:** the second calibration finds camera $t_d$ of −0.03 and −0.04 ms. $t_{ga}$ goes from −6.97 to
  +0.17 ms.
- **Without $t_{ga}$:** the second calibration finds −1.47 ms $t_d$ on both cameras. It does not go to 0.
- **Where it goes:** this is the gyro–accel offset that was not modelled, showing up as a camera time error.

**Conclusion:**

- **$t_{ga}$ matters for camera–IMU calibration:** on these two devices, a tool without it moves $t_d$ by about
  {Math.min(...moved).toFixed(1)}–{Math.max(...moved).toFixed(1)} ms.
- **It makes a corrected dataset consistent in time:** to use a tool without $t_{ga}$, remove the offset first
  with [corrected-dataset export](/guides/roundtrip/#export-a-corrected-dataset).

## Limits

<div class="not-content rp-honest">
  <ul>
    <li><b>The test favours MVIS:</b> The zero point is MVIS's own calibration, and the sweep injects exactly the
      shift MVIS models, a pure accel delay.</li>
    <li><b>Raw data:</b> Which tool's <code>td</code> is right on raw data is not tested.</li>
    <li><b>Not a pure shift:</b> On the T265 the real offset shrinks with frequency, from {band(0)} to
      {band(lastSure)}, less certain above {bands.uncertain_from_hz} Hz. So one <code>t_ga</code> is a
      compromise.</li>
    <li><b>One recording per device:</b> Each sweep starts from a single recording.</li>
    <li><b>Who ran it:</b> The MVIS authors ran all three tools.</li>
  </ul>
</div>
