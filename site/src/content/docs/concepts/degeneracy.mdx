Use this page when a calibration value looks wrong or varies between
recordings, and you suspect the motion.

A motion is **degenerate** for a value when it gives the data no way to measure
that value ([Observability](/concepts/observability/) explains the reasons). A
degenerate value does not make the solve fail. It converges, the residuals look
fine, and the value sits near its start or drifts with noise. So you have to look
for it.

Symbols follow [Notation & glossary](/concepts/notation/).

## Degenerate motions

| Motion | What is lost | How it shows |
|---|---|---|
| **Pure translation** (no rotation) | lever arm (all 3 axes); gyro scale $\mathbf{D}_w$; the extrinsic rotation is fixed only by the direction of gravity and acceleration, so it is weak; readout and time offset are weak | lever stays near its start with a wide 1σ; $\mathbf{D}_w$ diagonal stays near 1 |
| **Rotation about one axis only** | the lever-arm component along that axis; extrinsic rotation about that axis; $\mathbf{D}_w$ columns of the other two axes; readout, if the axis is the optical axis | one lever component with a much wider 1σ than the others; the rotation-excitation check shows one large singular value and two tiny ones |
| **Constant velocity** (steady turning and moving) | time offset $t_{d,i}$ (a time shift looks like a fixed transform); `t_ga` (no change in acceleration); $\mathbf{D}_a$ gets no signal beyond the gravity tilts | $t_{d,i}$ and the lever move together between recordings |
| **Planar motion** (rig slides in a plane and turns only about its normal, as on a cart or a table) | everything one-axis rotation loses; with a vertical normal, gravity stays on one IMU axis, so the other $\mathbf{D}_a$ columns and the accel bias along gravity trade off | as one-axis rotation, plus $\mathbf{D}_a$ diagonals with a wide 1σ |
| **Too slow** | time offset, readout and `t_ga` (all need fast change); weak $\mathbf{D}_w$ | wide 1σ on the timing values; readout far from the sensor's spec |
| **Too short** | fewer distinct orientations; biases and IMU scales separate poorly | values move a lot between recordings |
| **Board always centred, face-on** | lens distortion and focal length | distortion differs between recordings; residuals grow toward the image edges |

$\mathbf{T}_g$ is weak under almost any hand-held motion. That is why it is pinned
unless you set `estimate.imu.intrinsics: full`.

## How MVIS shows it

MVIS does not stop a run because the motion was weak. It reports, at four points.

### Before the solve

- **Recording length:** `mvis inspect` warns when a recording with an IMU is
  shorter than 30 s.
- **Window choice:** for bags longer than 80 s, the camera–IMU run keeps the 80 s
  with the most gyro and accelerometer excitation, scored per IMU as the mean
  squared angular rate plus the variance of the acceleration magnitude
  (`input.window_mode: motion`).

### In the start values

The start-value step runs simple fits before the joint solve and checks them:

- **Rotation excitation:** the singular values of the camera–gyro angular-velocity
  fit. One large and two tiny ones mean rotation about one axis.
- **Time-offset sharpness:** how much the fit error rises away from the best
  offset. A flat minimum means the offset is weak.
- **Lever arm:** if its 1σ in the linear fit is over 5 cm, it starts at 0.
- **Accelerometer bias:** it starts at 0. The start step estimates it only when
  asked (an advanced start option); it then falls back to 0 when the linear fit
  cannot separate it (smallest singular value of the scaled system under 10⁻³, or
  1σ over 0.05 m/s²).
- **Gravity and scale:** the fitted gravity should be close to 9.81 m/s² and the
  scale within 3 % of 1. A miss points at wrong intrinsics, units or stamps.
- **Auxiliary IMUs:** the gyro-to-gyro start needs a clear minimum (1.5 times
  sharper than the edges) and more than 10 s of overlap.

A value that starts at 0 is still solved jointly; the start only keeps a poor
guess out.

### In the joint solve

Freed IMU intrinsics carry a weak prior (0.1 on $\mathbf{D}_w$ and
$\mathbf{D}_a$). On a direction the motion does not excite, that prior wins: the
value stays near its start instead of wandering. Its 1σ then stays close to the
prior's 0.1: that is the sign the motion did not excite it.

### In the results

- **1σ for every value:** from the marginal covariance of the joint solve, in the
  result JSON and the report. Compare each with the others of its kind and across
  recordings.
- **Camera-only report, motion grades:** each camera gets a grade (good,
  marginal, poor) per value, from its board poses. The thresholds are empirical:

| Grade for | Measure | Good | Marginal |
|---|---|---|---|
| focal length | farthest over nearest board distance | over 2.0 | over 1.5 |
| distortion | image area covered by the board corners | over 60 % | over 30 % |
| rotation diversity | smallest over largest spread of the rotation axes | over 0.1 | over 0.03 |
| time offset | spread of the angular speed | over 0.5 rad/s | over 0.2 rad/s |
| readout | peak angular speed about the camera's x and y axes | over 1.0 rad/s | over 0.3 rad/s |

- **Camera–IMU report, motion and excitation:** the angular velocity of the
  solved trajectory over time. Gaps and one-axis stretches are easy to see.
- **`mvis verdict`:** grades a result green, yellow or red. It flags a χ² per
  degree of freedom (the weighted squared error divided by its degrees of
  freedom, about 1 when the noise model fits) over 1.5 (yellow) or 3.0 (red), a solve that hit its
  iteration cap, an IMU scale more than 5 % from 1, and a camera time offset over
  50 ms. A wrong value from weak motion can still pass these; the 1σ and a second
  recording are the stronger checks.

## Check it yourself

- **Calibrate two or more recordings.** A value that moves far more than its 1σ
  between them was not observed.
- **Run the digital twin.** MVIS replays your motion on a simulated copy of your
  rig with known truth, solves it, and shows which values come back
  ([step 7: digital-twin check](/guides/simulation/)).
- **Run the round trip.** Correct the data with the result and calibrate again;
  if the first result was right, the second finds nothing left to correct
  ([step 8: round-trip validation](/guides/roundtrip/)).

## What to do

| If you see | Add to the motion |
|---|---|
| lever arm weak, or one component weak | rotation about the other axes, with brisk changes of turning speed |
| $t_{d,i}$ moves between recordings | changes of speed and direction; avoid long steady sweeps |
| readout weak or off | faster tilts about the camera's x and y axes, board across all rows |
| `t_ga` weak | short, sharp reversals |
| $\mathbf{D}_a$ weak | tilt the rig so each axis points up and down at some point |
| $\mathbf{D}_w$ weak | turns about every axis, at several speeds |
| distortion differs | move the board into the image corners and edges |

The motion in [step 2: record the dataset](/guides/calibration-motion/) covers all
of these in about 60 s. Its [motions that fail](/guides/calibration-motion/#motions-that-fail)
list the same cases from the recording side.
