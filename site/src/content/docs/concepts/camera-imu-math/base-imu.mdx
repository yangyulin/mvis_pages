Use this page to see how MVIS turns the base IMU's raw readings into a factor
between two trajectory states, with the IMU's own errors solved inside it.

- **Base IMU:** the first IMU in the config. Its clock is the reference clock and
  its pose is the trajectory.
- **State:** the IMU pose, velocity and biases at one time $t_k$. States sit at
  `solve.state_hz` (30 Hz by default) on the first camera's frames.
- **Preintegration:** the IMU readings between two states, combined into one
  relative rotation, velocity change and position change.

Symbols follow [Notation & glossary](/concepts/notation/). The IMU conventions
(`calyx`, `kalibr`, `rpng`) are on [IMU model](/concepts/imu-model/).

## Corrected readings

The gyroscope gives $\boldsymbol{\omega}_m$ and the accelerometer
$\mathbf{a}_m$. MVIS corrects each reading before it integrates it:

$$
{}^{I}\mathbf{a} = {}^{I}_{a}\mathbf{R}\;\mathbf{D}_a\,\big(\mathbf{a}_m - \mathbf{b}_a\big)
$$

$$
{}^{I}\boldsymbol{\omega} = {}^{I}_{w}\mathbf{R}\;\mathbf{D}_w\,\big(\boldsymbol{\omega}_m - \mathbf{b}_g - \mathbf{T}_g\,{}^{I}\mathbf{a}\big)
$$

- **$\mathbf{D}_w$, $\mathbf{D}_a$:** correction matrices. Their diagonal fixes
  the scale of each axis; the rest fixes misalignment (axes not quite at right
  angles). They multiply the raw reading, so a diagonal of 0.998 means the raw
  axis read 0.2 % high.
- **$\mathbf{T}_g$:** g-sensitivity, the part of the gyro reading caused by
  linear acceleration.
- **$\mathbf{b}_g$, $\mathbf{b}_a$:** the biases, solved at every state.
- **${}^{I}_{w}\mathbf{R}$, ${}^{I}_{a}\mathbf{R}$:** the gyro and accelerometer
  triad rotations into the IMU frame. Each convention frees at most one of them:

| Convention | $\mathbf{D}_w$ | $\mathbf{D}_a$ | Free rotation | Intrinsic parameters |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `calyx` (default) | full 3×3 (9) | upper-triangular (6) | none; folded into $\mathbf{D}_w$ | 9 + 6 + 9 ($\mathbf{T}_g$) = 24 |
| `kalibr` | lower-triangular (6) | lower-triangular (6) | ${}^{I}_{w}\mathbf{R}$ (3) | 6 + 6 + 9 + 3 = 24 |
| `rpng` | upper-triangular (6) | upper-triangular (6) | ${}^{I}_{a}\mathbf{R}$ (3) | 6 + 6 + 9 + 3 = 24 |

All three give the solver one 24-value intrinsics variable $\mathbf{x}_{in}$.
It starts at identity (no correction).

An IMU slower than 200 Hz is first upsampled (cubic, the real samples kept)
before integration (`solve.imu_min_rate_hz`).

## Preintegration

Between two states at $t_k$ and $t_{k+1}$ ($\Delta t = t_{k+1} - t_k$), the
corrected readings give the motion in the frame of the first state:

$$
\Delta\mathbf{R}_{k} = {}^{I_k}_{I_{k+1}}\mathbf{R},
\qquad
\Delta\mathbf{v}_{k} = \int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}} {}^{I_k}_{I_\tau}\mathbf{R}\;{}^{I}\mathbf{a}_\tau\,d\tau,
\qquad
\Delta\mathbf{p}_{k} = \int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}}\!\!\int_{t_k}^{s} {}^{I_k}_{I_\tau}\mathbf{R}\;{}^{I}\mathbf{a}_\tau\,d\tau\,ds
$$

MVIS evaluates these with **ACI** (analytic combined IMU integration). Between two
samples it takes the midpoint of the two corrected readings as constant and
integrates the rotation and the rotated acceleration in closed form. The same
pass gives:

- the Jacobians of $\Delta\mathbf{R}$, $\Delta\mathbf{v}$, $\Delta\mathbf{p}$
  with respect to the biases, the 24 intrinsics and `t_ga`;
- the 15×15 covariance $\mathbf{P}_k$, from the noise densities (`solve.sigma_g`,
  `sigma_a`) and the bias random-walk densities (`sigma_bg`, `sigma_ba`).

The integration runs once, at the biases and intrinsics of the start point.
Small changes after that are applied through the Jacobians. A detection gap is
bridged by one factor over the whole gap.

## The gyro-to-accel offset `t_ga`

Many drivers publish gyro and accelerometer on one stamp, though the two were
sampled at different instants. MVIS takes the stamp as gyro time. The
accelerometer value that belongs to gyro time $t$ is the one stamped
$t + t_{ga}$. Over one integration step from sample 0 to sample 1, to first order:

$$
\bar{\mathbf{a}}_m = \tfrac12\big(\mathbf{a}_{m,0} + \mathbf{a}_{m,1}\big) + t_{ga}\,\dot{\mathbf{a}}_m,
\qquad
\dot{\mathbf{a}}_m = \frac{\mathbf{a}_{m,1} - \mathbf{a}_{m,0}}{\delta t}
$$

- **One more Jacobian column:** $\partial\bar{\mathbf{a}}_m/\partial t_{ga} = \dot{\mathbf{a}}_m$,
  carried through $\mathbf{D}_a$ and, through $\mathbf{T}_g$, into the gyro.
- **Re-integration:** after the first two iterations, when `t_ga` has moved more
  than 3 ms from the value the factors were integrated at, MVIS integrates that
  IMU again with its accelerometer resampled at $t + t_{ga}$
  (`solve.tga_relin_threshold_s`, default 0.003 s). The linear term then covers
  only what is left.
- **Prior:** a weak zero-mean prior, 10 ms (`solve.sigma_t_ga`).

`estimate_t_ga` is on by default in camera–IMU runs. What the offset is and when
it matters: [Why calibrate t_ga](/why/tga/).

## The residual

The factor links state $k$ (pose ${}^{G}_{I_k}\mathbf{T}$, velocity
${}^{G}\mathbf{v}_{I_k}$, biases) to state $k+1$. It predicts the measured
preintegration from the two states and compares.

Write $\delta\boldsymbol{\eta}$ for the change since integration:
$\delta\mathbf{b}_g$, $\delta\mathbf{b}_a$, $\delta\mathbf{x}_{in}$ and
$\delta t_{ga}$. First-order corrections, built from the Jacobians above:

$$
\delta\boldsymbol\theta = \mathbf{J}_{R}\,\delta\boldsymbol\eta,
\qquad
\delta\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{J}_{v}\,\delta\boldsymbol\eta,
\qquad
\delta\mathbf{p} = \mathbf{J}_{p}\,\delta\boldsymbol\eta
$$

The 15 residual rows are:

$$
\mathbf{r}_\theta = \mathrm{Log}\!\Big(\Delta\mathbf{R}_k^\top\;{}^{G}_{I_k}\mathbf{R}^\top\,{}^{G}_{I_{k+1}}\mathbf{R}\;\mathrm{Exp}(\delta\boldsymbol\theta)^\top\Big)
$$

$$
\mathbf{r}_p = {}^{G}_{I_k}\mathbf{R}^\top\Big({}^{G}\mathbf{p}_{I_{k+1}} - {}^{G}\mathbf{p}_{I_k} - {}^{G}\mathbf{v}_{I_k}\,\Delta t - \tfrac12\,{}^{G}\mathbf{g}\,\Delta t^2\Big) - \delta\mathbf{p} - \Delta\mathbf{p}_k
$$

$$
\mathbf{r}_v = {}^{G}_{I_k}\mathbf{R}^\top\Big({}^{G}\mathbf{v}_{I_{k+1}} - {}^{G}\mathbf{v}_{I_k} - {}^{G}\mathbf{g}\,\Delta t\Big) - \delta\mathbf{v} - \Delta\mathbf{v}_k
$$

$$
\mathbf{r}_{b} = \begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{b}_{g,k+1} - \mathbf{b}_{g,k}\\ \mathbf{b}_{a,k+1} - \mathbf{b}_{a,k}\end{bmatrix}
$$

The cost is $\|\mathbf{r}\|^2_{\mathbf{P}_k}$ with
$\mathbf{r} = [\mathbf{r}_\theta;\ \mathbf{r}_p;\ \mathbf{r}_v;\ \mathbf{r}_b]$.

- **Bias random walk:** $\mathbf{r}_b$ is the random-walk term. Biases may drift
  between states, by as much as the random-walk densities allow. There is no
  separate bias factor.
- **No robust loss:** the IMU residual is plain least squares. Only the
  reprojection factor uses Huber.
- **The covariance follows the correction:** $\mathbf{P}_k$ is rotated by the
  current $\delta\boldsymbol\theta$ before it weights the residual.

## Jacobians

Every block is analytic and checked against finite differences in the test
suite.

| Variable | Rows it touches | Comes from |
|---|---|---|
| pose $k$ | $\mathbf{r}_\theta$, $\mathbf{r}_p$, $\mathbf{r}_v$ | the predicted relative motion, rotated into frame $k$ |
| pose $k+1$ | $\mathbf{r}_\theta$, $\mathbf{r}_p$ | the same, at the end point |
| velocity $k$, $k+1$ | $\mathbf{r}_p$, $\mathbf{r}_v$ | ${}^{G}_{I_k}\mathbf{R}^\top$, times $\Delta t$ in $\mathbf{r}_p$ |
| biases $k$ | all | the bias Jacobians of the integration; identity in $\mathbf{r}_b$ |
| biases $k+1$ | $\mathbf{r}_b$ | identity |
| intrinsics $\mathbf{x}_{in}$ | $\mathbf{r}_\theta$, $\mathbf{r}_p$, $\mathbf{r}_v$ | the intrinsic Jacobians of the integration |
| `t_ga` | $\mathbf{r}_\theta$, $\mathbf{r}_p$, $\mathbf{r}_v$ | the `t_ga` column of the integration |

## Which intrinsics are solved

`estimate.imu.intrinsics` frees blocks of $\mathbf{x}_{in}$. A pinned block gets a
very tight prior at its start value; a freed block gets a weak one, so a direction
the motion does not excite stays near its start instead of drifting.

| Setting | Free | Weak prior on the free terms |
|---|---|---|
| `pinned` | nothing | — |
| `dw_da` (default) | $\mathbf{D}_w$, $\mathbf{D}_a$ | 0.1 (`solve.sigma_dw_da`) |
| `dw_da_r` | as `dw_da`, plus the free rotation (`kalibr`, `rpng`; same as `dw_da` under `calyx`, which has no separate rotation) | 0.1 (`solve.sigma_imu_R`) |
| `full` | as `dw_da_r`, plus $\mathbf{T}_g$ | 0.05 (`solve.sigma_tg`) |

## The same model inside the reprojection factor

The reprojection factor integrates the same corrected readings from an anchor
state to each corner's capture time
([Camera–IMU calibration math](/concepts/camera-imu-math/#where-was-the-imu-at-that-time)).
So $\mathbf{D}_w$, $\mathbf{D}_a$, the biases and `t_ga` are fitted by both
factors, with one consistent model.

## What this means for your recording

- **Rotate about all three axes.** Each column of $\mathbf{D}_w$ needs rotation
  about its axis.
- **Point every axis at gravity at some time.** Gravity is the largest specific
  force you have. Tilting the rig through many orientations separates
  $\mathbf{D}_a$ from $\mathbf{b}_a$.
- **Change the acceleration briskly.** `t_ga` appears only through
  $\dot{\mathbf{a}}_m$; smooth, steady motion leaves it loose.
- **Leave $\mathbf{T}_g$ pinned** unless the motion has strong linear acceleration
  while it rotates. Hand-held motion rarely does.
