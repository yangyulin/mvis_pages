Use this page to see how MVIS calibrates every IMU after the first one.

An **auxiliary IMU** is any IMU after the first in `input.imu_topics`. It is
rigidly mounted, so it feels the same motion as the base IMU, seen from another
place, along other axes and on another clock. MVIS solves, for each one:

- **Extrinsic** ${}^{I}_{I_{s_j}}\mathbf{T}$: its rotation and position in the
  base-IMU frame. The position part is its **lever arm**.
- **Time offset** $t_{d,j}$: an auxiliary sample stamped $t$ is the motion at
  base-IMU time $t + t_{d,j}$.
- **Its own intrinsics:** $\mathbf{D}_w$, $\mathbf{D}_a$, $\mathbf{T}_g$, in the
  same convention as the base IMU.
- **Its own `t_ga`** and its own biases.

Below we write $I_s$ for $I_{s_j}$. Symbols follow
[Notation & glossary](/concepts/notation/). The base factor this builds on is
[Base IMU factor](/concepts/camera-imu-math/base-imu/).

## Variables

| Variable | Size | Where |
|---|---|---|
| calibration: ${}^{I}_{I_s}\mathbf{T}$, $t_{d,j}$, 24 intrinsics | 6 + 1 + 24 = 31 | one per auxiliary IMU |
| velocity ${}^{G}\mathbf{v}_{I_s}$, biases $\mathbf{b}_{g,s}$, $\mathbf{b}_{a,s}$ | 9 | at the auxiliary knots |
| `t_ga` of this IMU | 1 | one per auxiliary IMU |

The auxiliary IMU has no pose states of its own. Its poses come from the base
trajectory, so every auxiliary factor pulls on the base states too.

**Knots:** one auxiliary factor spans 3 base intervals by default
(`solve.aux_imu_stride: 3`). Fewer factors make the solve faster. The stride is not
accuracy-neutral: the lever arms shift with it. When the lever arm matters, solve
again with `solve.aux_imu_stride: 1` (one factor per interval, slower) and compare.

## From the base pose to the auxiliary pose

At each knot $k$ MVIS builds the auxiliary IMU's pose from the base state, the
extrinsic and the time offset:

$$
{}^{G}_{I_s}\mathbf{R}_k = {}^{G}_{I_k}\mathbf{R}\;\mathrm{Exp}\!\big({}^{I}_{I_s}\mathbf{R}\;{}^{I_s}\boldsymbol{\omega}_k\;\delta t_{d}\big)\;{}^{I}_{I_s}\mathbf{R}
$$

$$
{}^{G}\mathbf{p}_{I_s,k} = {}^{G}\mathbf{p}_{I_k} + {}^{G}\mathbf{v}_{I_k}\,\delta t_{d}
+ {}^{G}_{I_k}\mathbf{R}\;\mathrm{Exp}\!\big({}^{I}_{I_s}\mathbf{R}\;{}^{I_s}\boldsymbol{\omega}_k\;\delta t_{d}\big)\;{}^{I}\mathbf{p}_{I_s}
$$

- **${}^{I_s}\boldsymbol{\omega}_k$:** the auxiliary IMU's corrected angular
  velocity at the knot.
- **$\delta t_d = t_{d,j} - \bar t_{d,j}$:** only the part of the time offset not
  yet absorbed. MVIS cuts the auxiliary samples for interval $[t_k, t_{k+1}]$ at
  stamps $[t_k - \bar t_{d,j},\ t_{k+1} - \bar t_{d,j}]$, with $\bar t_{d,j}$ the
  starting value. The pose then needs only a small shift, which keeps the
  first-order propagation accurate even for offsets of tens of ms.

## The residual

The auxiliary IMU's own readings are preintegrated over the knot interval,
exactly as for the base IMU: corrected by its own biases, intrinsics and `t_ga`.
The residual is the same 15-row residual as the
[base factor](/concepts/camera-imu-math/base-imu/#the-residual), evaluated on the
auxiliary poses above and the auxiliary velocity and biases:

$$
\mathbf{r}_{\text{aux}}^{j,k} =
\mathbf{r}_{\text{imu}}\Big(
{}^{G}_{I_s}\mathbf{T}_{k},\ {}^{G}_{I_s}\mathbf{T}_{k'},\
{}^{G}\mathbf{v}_{I_s,k},\ {}^{G}\mathbf{v}_{I_s,k'},\
\mathbf{b}_{s,k},\ \mathbf{b}_{s,k'};\
\mathbf{x}_{in,s},\ t_{ga,s}\Big)
$$

with $k'$ the next knot. The Jacobians chain the base-factor Jacobians through
the pose map above, so they reach the base poses, the base velocities, the
extrinsic and $t_{d,j}$. All are analytic and checked against finite differences.

### Why the lever arm needs rotation

A point at ${}^{I}\mathbf{p}_{I_s}$ on a rigid body feels the base acceleration
plus two rotation terms:

$$
{}^{I_s}\mathbf{a} = {}^{I}_{I_s}\mathbf{R}^\top\Big({}^{I}\mathbf{a}
+ \big[{}^{I}\dot{\boldsymbol\omega}\big]_\times{}^{I}\mathbf{p}_{I_s}
+ \big[{}^{I}\boldsymbol\omega\big]_\times^2\,{}^{I}\mathbf{p}_{I_s}\Big)
$$

$[\cdot]_\times$ is the cross-product matrix. With no rotation both terms vanish
and the lever arm leaves no trace. The rotation of the extrinsic and $t_{d,j}$
come from the gyro alone: two gyros on one body read the same angular velocity,
rotated and shifted in time.

## Starting values

Before the joint solve, MVIS initialises each auxiliary IMU from its gyro and the
base gyro alone. Both are direct angular-rate measurements at 100 Hz or more, so
this is sharper than going through a camera.

1. **Time offset:** scan $t_{d,j}$ and keep the value where the two gyro signals,
   aligned by the best rotation, agree best. A parabola refines the minimum.
2. **Rotation:** the best rotation between the two gyro signals at that offset
   (the Kabsch method, a closed-form least-squares rotation fit).
3. **Gyro bias** from the means of the two signals.
4. **Lever arm:** 0. Only the joint solve can see it.

The start is accepted when the minimum is sharp (the error at
$\pm$ half the search window is more than 1.5 times the minimum) and the two
streams overlap by more than 10 s.

## Priors

- **First knot:** velocity and biases, as for the base IMU (0.5 m/s, 0.01 rad/s,
  0.1 m/s² by default).
- **Extrinsic and time offset:** free. `estimate.imu.extrinsics: false` or
  `estimate.imu.td: false` holds them at their start.
- **Intrinsics:** staged as for the base IMU (`estimate.imu.intrinsics`).
- **`t_ga`:** the same weak 10 ms prior as the base IMU.

## Gyro-only mode

Some accelerometers are noisy or interpolated by their driver. Mark such an IMU
`gyro_only: true` and MVIS calibrates it from its gyro alone:

```yaml title="cam_imu.yaml"
input:
  imus:
    - {}                    # base IMU: always full
    - {gyro_only: true}     # second IMU: gyro only
```

The factor keeps only the rotation and gyro-bias rows:

$$
\mathbf{r}_{\text{gyro}} =
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathrm{Log}\!\Big(\Delta\mathbf{R}^\top\;{}^{G}_{I_s}\mathbf{R}_k^\top\,{}^{G}_{I_s}\mathbf{R}_{k'}\;\mathrm{Exp}(\delta\boldsymbol\theta)^\top\Big)\\[2pt]
\mathbf{b}_{g,s,k'} - \mathbf{b}_{g,s,k}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

| | Gyro-only IMU |
|---|---|
| **Solved** | extrinsic rotation, $t_{d,j}$, gyro bias, and $\mathbf{D}_w$ unless `estimate.imu.intrinsics: pinned` |
| **Held** | lever arm, $\mathbf{D}_a$, $\mathbf{T}_g$, `t_ga` (none of them reach the gyro) |
| **States** | the gyro bias at each knot, no velocity |

The base IMU cannot be gyro-only: it needs its accelerometer for gravity and
metric scale.

## What this means for your recording

- **Rotate about at least two axes**, briskly. The auxiliary rotation and time
  offset come from angular velocity; the lever arm from angular velocity and
  angular acceleration.
- **Record long enough for the gyro start:** the two IMUs must overlap by more
  than 10 s.
- **Mount the IMUs rigidly.** The model assumes one rigid body.
