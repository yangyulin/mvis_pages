An ideal IMU reports angular velocity and specific force in its own orthogonal
frame. A real one reports them through a scale error, a non-orthogonal axis
triad, and — for the gyroscope — a sensitivity to linear acceleration. Open MVIS
carries those as state so they are solved rather than assumed.

Symbols follow [Notation & glossary](/concepts/notation/).

## Measurement model

$$
\boldsymbol{\omega}_m = \mathbf{D}_w\, \boldsymbol{\omega} + \mathbf{T}_g\, \mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}_g + \mathbf{n}_g
$$

$$
\mathbf{a}_m = \mathbf{D}_a\, \mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}_a + \mathbf{n}_a
$$

- $\mathbf{D}_w,\ \mathbf{D}_a$ — scale and misalignment, lower- or
  upper-triangular depending on convention (see below)
- $\mathbf{T}_g$ — g-sensitivity: how linear acceleration leaks into the
  gyroscope reading
- $\mathbf{b}_g,\ \mathbf{b}_a$ — slowly-varying biases, random-walk driven
- $\mathbf{n}_g,\ \mathbf{n}_a$ — white noise, densities from the datasheet

Only six of the nine entries of each $\mathbf{D}$ are identifiable: the frame
has to be pinned to something. Which three are fixed is exactly what separates
the two conventions.

## Two conventions

```xml title="launch/<your>.launch"
<param name="imu_model" type="int" value="1" />
<!-- 0: Kalibr, 1: RPNG -->
```

| | Kalibr (`0`) | RPNG (`1`) |
|---|---|---|
| Frame pinned to | accelerometer triad | gyroscope triad |
| $\mathbf{D}_w$ | lower-triangular | upper-triangular |
| $\mathbf{D}_a$ | upper-triangular | lower-triangular |
| Reported alongside | `Tw`, `Ta` (inverses) | `Tw`, `Ta` (inverses) |

They describe the same physical device. A calibration produced under one
convention is **not** numerically comparable with the other — the matrices
differ even when the sensor does not. When comparing against an external
calibration, check which convention produced it before concluding anything from
the numbers.

:::caution[The default is not the same as Kalibr's]
`imu_model` defaults to the RPNG parameterisation in the shipped launch files.
If you are cross-checking against Kalibr output, set it to `0` or convert.
:::

## Gyro-only auxiliaries

An auxiliary IMU can contribute only its gyroscope
([Multi-camera / multi-IMU rigs](/guides/multi-sensor-rigs/)). Then
$\mathbf{D}_a$, $\mathbf{b}_a$, and the accelerometer noise terms drop out of
its block, and its translation relative to the base IMU is constrained only
indirectly. The result file writes identity in place of the unused matrices —
identity there means *not estimated*, not *estimated as identity*.

## What is actually observable

Scale and misalignment need excitation on every axis: a bag that rotates about
one axis constrains one row of $\mathbf{D}_w$ and leaves the rest at their
priors. G-sensitivity is weaker still — it requires simultaneous rotation and
high linear acceleration, which most hand-held calibration motions do not
produce. When $\mathbf{T}_g$ comes back near its initial value with a wide
covariance, it was not observed.

[Observability](/concepts/observability/) treats this properly; the practical
consequence is in [Degeneracy](/concepts/degeneracy/).
