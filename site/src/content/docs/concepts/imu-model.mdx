Use this page to see how MVIS models the IMU and which IMU options to pick.

MVIS solves the IMU intrinsics rather than assuming a datasheet. IMU
intrinsics are the sensor's own error terms:

- **Scale and misalignment** for the gyroscope and the accelerometer
- **G-sensitivity** (optional)
- **Biases**

Symbols follow [Notation & glossary](/concepts/notation/).

## Measurement model

$$
\boldsymbol{\omega}_m = \mathbf{D}_w\, \boldsymbol{\omega} + \mathbf{T}_g\, \mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}_g + \mathbf{n}_g
$$

$$
\mathbf{a}_m = \mathbf{D}_a\, \mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}_a + \mathbf{n}_a
$$

- `Dw` / `Da` carry scale and misalignment.
- `Tg` is the g-sensitivity: how linear acceleration leaks into the gyro reading.
- `b_g` / `b_a` are the biases.

## Three conventions

```yaml title="cam_imu.yaml"
solve:
  imu_intrinsics_model: kalibr    # kalibr | rpng | calyx
```

The nine entries of each `D` are not all identifiable. The frame must be
pinned to something. *What* it is pinned to is the convention:

| Model | Pins the frame to | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `kalibr` | accelerometer triad | Matches Kalibr output |
| `rpng` | gyroscope triad | RPNG lineage |
| `calyx` | — | Gyro correction as a full 3×3, decomposed post-hoc into rotation × upper-triangular |

:::note[The convention is not a quality lever]
On real data — TUM-VI and MIC, with camera intrinsics pinned or freed — **all
three recover identical extrinsics and intrinsics**. Choose the one that matches
whatever you are comparing against. Do not expect accuracy from the choice.
:::

The matrices themselves are *not* numerically comparable across conventions.
Before concluding that two calibrations disagree, check they used the same one.

## `t_ga` — the gyro↔accel offset

`t_ga` is the time offset between the gyroscope and accelerometer channels
inside one IMU.

Many IMU drivers publish gyro and accelerometer on one timestamp. Yet the two
channels are sampled at different rates and instants. `t_ga` is that
*internal* offset:

- The combined stamp is taken as the gyro clock.
- The accel stored at `t` is modelled as physically sampled at `t − t_ga`.

```yaml title="cam_imu.yaml"
solve:
  estimate_t_ga: true
  sigma_t_ga: 0.001
```

It is estimated **inside the ACI factor**. The factor gains two things:

- One extra Jacobian column in the preintegration (`∂a/∂t_ga = ȧ` from
  per-step finite differences, coupled into the gyro row through `Tg`)
- An optional sixth factor key with a staged prior

With the flag off, the five-key factor is untouched.

**In simulation**, injected offsets of −2, +1, and +5 ms recover with |z| ≤ 1.3
at σ = 0.024 ms. There is no `td` / `Dw` / `Da` cross-contamination.

**On real data**, the T265's Bosch BMI055 reads `t_ga` about −5.7 to −8.1 ms
across recordings and code versions (april_01 now −5.69 ± 0.43 ms). The offset
changes with frequency, from about −6.8 ms at 2–4 Hz to −1.2 ms at 30–40 Hz.
So one `t_ga` is a compromise for the motion, not a constant of the driver; see
the [limits](/benchmark/tga/#limits).

MVIS corrects `t_ga` to first order. The ½·t_ga²·ä term is ignored, which
matters at large offsets.

Where `t_ga` shows up:

- **Reported** in the result JSON, the calibration YAML, and the text report
- **Corrected** by [`apply-calib`](/guides/corrected-datasets/), which
  re-interpolates the accel channel at `t + t_ga`
- **Checked** by [`roundtrip`](/guides/roundtrip/), which tests that it
  collapses toward zero

**Why it matters:** tools without `t_ga` put about 0.3–0.6 of a pure-shift
offset into the camera `td`. See [Why calibrate t_ga](/benchmark/tga/).

## Gyro-only auxiliaries

The other answer to an untrustworthy accelerometer is to skip it:

```yaml title="cam_imu.yaml"
input:
  imus:
    - {}                    # base IMU
    - {gyro_only: true}     # aux
```

What a gyro-only IMU solves and pins:

- **Solved:** extrinsic rotation, `td` (here the IMU-to-IMU time offset), `Dw`,
  and gyro bias
- **Pinned:** lever arm (the translation between the two IMUs), `Da`, and `Tg`

It is **not permitted on the base IMU**. The base IMU needs the accelerometer
for gravity and metric scale. The calibration self-documents with a
`gyro_only: true` flag per IMU.

On the T265 this makes the `td` collapse in a correct→recalibrate round-trip
(+8.6 → −9.0 ms becomes +0.9 → +0.4 ms). It also halves the residual lever
drift.

## Which to reach for

Both address the same defect:

| Option | What it does | Prefer it for |
|---|---|---|
| `estimate_t_ga` | **Measures** the offset causing it | The base IMU, where you have no choice but to use the accel — and where the measured value is itself worth having |
| `gyro_only` | **Avoids** the bad accelerometer | An auxiliary whose accelerometer adds nothing you need |

## What is actually observable

Each term needs the right motion to be observed:

- **Scale and misalignment** need excitation on every axis.
- **G-sensitivity** needs simultaneous rotation and strong linear acceleration.
  Most hand-held calibration motions do not produce that.

When `Tg` returns near its initial value with a wide covariance, it was not
observed. See [Degeneracy](/concepts/degeneracy/).
