Use this page to see how MVIS models an IMU, which convention to pick, and how
to correct raw IMU readings with a calibration result.

MVIS solves the IMU intrinsics rather than taking them from a datasheet. IMU
intrinsics are the sensor's own error terms:

- **Scale and misalignment** of the gyroscope and the accelerometer
  ($\mathbf{D}_w$, $\mathbf{D}_a$)
- **The rotation between the two triads** (the gyro and accel axes are not
  exactly parallel)
- **G-sensitivity** $\mathbf{T}_g$: specific force leaking into the gyro (optional)
- **Biases** $\mathbf{b}_g$, $\mathbf{b}_a$
- **The gyro–accel time offset** $t_{ga}$ inside one IMU

Symbols follow [Notation & glossary](/concepts/notation/). The code is in
[`src/mvis/imu/`](https://github.com/openmvis/openmvis/tree/main/src/mvis/imu):
`imu_model.py` holds the shared equations, `kalibr.py`, `rpng.py` and
`calyx.py` the three layouts.

## Measurement model

$\mathbf{D}_w$ and $\mathbf{D}_a$ are **correction** matrices: they turn a raw
reading into a true value. The raw readings are therefore their inverses applied
to the truth. With ${}^{I}\boldsymbol{\omega}$ the true angular velocity and
${}^{I}\mathbf{a}$ the true specific force, both in the IMU frame $\{I\}$:

$$
\mathbf{a}_m(t) = \mathbf{D}_a^{-1}\, {}^{I}_{a}\mathbf{R}^{\top}\, {}^{I}\mathbf{a}(t - t_{ga}) + \mathbf{b}_a + \mathbf{n}_a
$$

$$
\boldsymbol{\omega}_m(t) = \mathbf{D}_w^{-1}\, {}^{I}_{w}\mathbf{R}^{\top}\, {}^{I}\boldsymbol{\omega}(t) + \mathbf{T}_g\, {}^{I}\mathbf{a}(t) + \mathbf{b}_g + \mathbf{n}_g
$$

- ${}^{I}_{w}\mathbf{R}$ and ${}^{I}_{a}\mathbf{R}$ rotate the gyro triad
  $\{w\}$ and the accel triad $\{a\}$ into $\{I\}$. Each convention fixes one of
  them to the identity.
- $t_{ga}$ is the [gyro–accel offset](#t_ga-the-gyroaccel-offset) of the
  accel against the gyro clock.
- $\mathbf{n}_g$, $\mathbf{n}_a$ are white noise. The biases follow a random
  walk, $\dot{\mathbf{b}}_g = \mathbf{n}_{bg}$, $\dot{\mathbf{b}}_a = \mathbf{n}_{ba}$.
  Their densities are the config's `sigma_g`, `sigma_a`, `sigma_bg`, `sigma_ba`.

## Inverse model (correction)

The correction is the exact inverse. The accel comes first, because the gyro's
g-sensitivity term needs the corrected specific force:

$$
{}^{I}\mathbf{a}(t) = {}^{I}_{a}\mathbf{R}\, \mathbf{D}_a \big(\mathbf{a}_m(t + t_{ga}) - \mathbf{b}_a\big)
$$

$$
{}^{I}\boldsymbol{\omega}(t) = {}^{I}_{w}\mathbf{R}\, \mathbf{D}_w \big(\boldsymbol{\omega}_m(t) - \mathbf{b}_g - \mathbf{T}_g\, {}^{I}\mathbf{a}(t)\big)
$$

This is the correction the IMU factor applies to every sample inside the solve
([`src/mvis/calibration/factors/aci.py`](https://github.com/openmvis/openmvis/blob/main/src/mvis/calibration/factors/aci.py)).
The simulator uses the forward model above to make raw readings, so the two are
exact inverses without noise.

## Three conventions

```yaml title="cam_imu.yaml"
estimate:
  imu:
    model: calyx        # kalibr | rpng | calyx  (default calyx)
```

`solve.imu_intrinsics_model` is the same setting under its low-level name.

The two triads and $\{I\}$ have 3 + 3 + 3 rotational degrees of freedom, but
the data only sees the rotation between the triads. So one rotation must be
pinned. *What* is pinned, and the shape of each matrix, is the convention:

| Convention | $\mathbf{D}_w$ | $\mathbf{D}_a$ | Free rotation | $\{I\}$ is | Parameters |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `kalibr` | lower-triangular (6) | lower-triangular (6) | ${}^{I}_{w}\mathbf{R}$ (3) | the accel triad | 6 + 6 + 3 + 9 ($\mathbf{T}_g$) = 24 |
| `rpng` | upper-triangular (6) | upper-triangular (6) | ${}^{I}_{a}\mathbf{R}$ (3) | the gyro triad | 6 + 6 + 3 + 9 = 24 |
| `calyx` (default) | full $3\times3$ (9) | upper-triangular (6) | none: the rotation is inside $\mathbf{D}_w$ | the accel triad | 9 + 6 + 9 = 24 |

On top of those 24, every convention has the two biases (3 + 3) and $t_{ga}$ (1).

### `kalibr`

$$
\mathbf{D}_w = \begin{bmatrix} d_{w1} & 0 & 0 \\ d_{w2} & d_{w4} & 0 \\ d_{w3} & d_{w5} & d_{w6} \end{bmatrix},
\quad
\mathbf{D}_a = \begin{bmatrix} d_{a1} & 0 & 0 \\ d_{a2} & d_{a4} & 0 \\ d_{a3} & d_{a5} & d_{a6} \end{bmatrix},
\quad
{}^{I}_{a}\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{I}
$$

$$
{}^{I}\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{D}_a(\mathbf{a}_m - \mathbf{b}_a), \qquad
{}^{I}\boldsymbol{\omega} = {}^{I}_{w}\mathbf{R}\,\mathbf{D}_w(\boldsymbol{\omega}_m - \mathbf{b}_g - \mathbf{T}_g\,{}^{I}\mathbf{a})
$$

This is the structure of **Kalibr's scale-misalignment IMU model**. Kalibr's
`imu.yaml` maps onto it as $\mathbf{D}_w = \mathbf{M}_g^{-1}$,
$\mathbf{D}_a = \mathbf{M}_a^{-1}$, ${}^{I}_{w}\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{C}_{gyro\_i}^{\top}$
and $\mathbf{T}_g = \mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{C}_{gyro\_i}$
([`src/mvis/io/kalibr.py`](https://github.com/openmvis/openmvis/blob/main/src/mvis/io/kalibr.py)).
Pick it when you compare against Kalibr.

### `rpng`

$$
\mathbf{D}_w = \begin{bmatrix} d_{w1} & d_{w2} & d_{w4} \\ 0 & d_{w3} & d_{w5} \\ 0 & 0 & d_{w6} \end{bmatrix},
\quad
\mathbf{D}_a = \begin{bmatrix} d_{a1} & d_{a2} & d_{a4} \\ 0 & d_{a3} & d_{a5} \\ 0 & 0 & d_{a6} \end{bmatrix},
\quad
{}^{I}_{w}\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{I}
$$

$$
{}^{I}\mathbf{a} = {}^{I}_{a}\mathbf{R}\,\mathbf{D}_a(\mathbf{a}_m - \mathbf{b}_a), \qquad
{}^{I}\boldsymbol{\omega} = \mathbf{D}_w(\boldsymbol{\omega}_m - \mathbf{b}_g - \mathbf{T}_g\,{}^{I}\mathbf{a})
$$

The convention of the RPNG group's VINS work (the
[OpenVINS](https://docs.openvins.com/) lineage). Here $\{I\}$ is the gyro
triad, so the camera–IMU extrinsics are relative to the gyro axes.

### `calyx`

$$
\mathbf{D}_w = \begin{bmatrix} d_{w1} & d_{w2} & d_{w3} \\ d_{w4} & d_{w5} & d_{w6} \\ d_{w7} & d_{w8} & d_{w9} \end{bmatrix},
\quad
\mathbf{D}_a \text{ upper-triangular as in } \texttt{rpng},
\quad
{}^{I}_{w}\mathbf{R} = {}^{I}_{a}\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{I}
$$

$$
{}^{I}\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{D}_a(\mathbf{a}_m - \mathbf{b}_a), \qquad
{}^{I}\boldsymbol{\omega} = \mathbf{D}_w(\boldsymbol{\omega}_m - \mathbf{b}_g - \mathbf{T}_g\,{}^{I}\mathbf{a})
$$

The full $\mathbf{D}_w$ is linear in its nine entries, so the solve needs no
rotation variable. After the solve, MVIS splits it into a rotation and an
upper-triangular matrix with a positive diagonal (a QR decomposition):

$$
\mathbf{D}_w = {}^{I}_{w}\mathbf{R}\; \mathbf{U}_w
$$

The result reports both: `R_wtoI`, `U_w`, and `Dw_decomposed` with the
per-axis gyro scale (`Dw_scale`, the diagonal of $\mathbf{U}_w$), the
cross-coupling (`Dw_shear`, its upper off-diagonals) and
`gyro_misalign_rotvec` $= \mathrm{Log}({}^{I}_{w}\mathbf{R})$ in rad.
Read the scale from `Dw_scale`, not from `Dw_diag`: in `calyx` the diagonal of
$\mathbf{D}_w$ mixes scale with rotation.

### How they compare

- **Same physics, different bookkeeping.** All three describe the same
  sensor. They differ in which frame $\{I\}$ is and where the triad rotation sits.
- **Not a quality lever.** Before the 0.1.0 release we checked this on TUM-VI
  and on the Monado SLAM calibration set MIC (Valve Index, see
  [Datasets](/datasets/)), with camera intrinsics pinned or freed. All three
  recovered the same extrinsics and camera intrinsics. Choose the one that matches what you compare against.
- **Not comparable entry by entry.** A `kalibr` $\mathbf{D}_w$ and an `rpng`
  $\mathbf{D}_w$ of the same IMU differ. Before concluding that two calibrations
  disagree, check they used the same convention.
- **Default staging differs.** The default `estimate.imu.intrinsics: dw_da`
  frees $\mathbf{D}_w$ and $\mathbf{D}_a$ only. In `calyx` that already
  includes the triad rotation (it lives in $\mathbf{D}_w$). In `kalibr` and
  `rpng` the rotation stays at the identity until you choose `dw_da_r`.

| `estimate.imu.intrinsics` | Frees |
|---|---|
| `pinned` (or `false`) | nothing |
| `dw_da` (default) | $\mathbf{D}_w$, $\mathbf{D}_a$ |
| `dw_da_r` | $\mathbf{D}_w$, $\mathbf{D}_a$, the triad rotation (`kalibr`, `rpng`; same as `dw_da` in `calyx`) |
| `full` (or `true`) | the above plus $\mathbf{T}_g$ |

Freed blocks carry weak priors around their start values: the identity for
$\mathbf{D}_w$, $\mathbf{D}_a$ and the rotation, zero for $\mathbf{T}_g$
(`sigma_dw_da` 0.1, `sigma_imu_R` 0.1 rad, `sigma_tg` 0.05).

## Correct readings with a result

`mvis cam-imu` writes a calibration YAML next to its JSON summary (for
`cam_imu.json`, the file is `cam_imu_calib.yaml`). Each entry of its `imus:`
list has:

| Key | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `name`, `role`, `topic` | IMU name, `base` or `aux`, and its topic when known |
| `model` | `kalibr`, `rpng` or `calyx` |
| `Dw`, `Da` | the correction matrices, $3\times3$ |
| `imu_intr_xi` | the 24 intrinsics as an offset from the identity, in the convention's order; it also carries $\mathbf{T}_g$ and the triad rotation |
| `b_g`, `b_a` | biases, rad/s and m/s² |
| `t_ga`, `t_ga_sigma` | gyro–accel offset and its 1-sigma, s |
| `Dw_diag`, `Da_diag` | the diagonals, for a quick look only; in `calyx` `Dw_diag` is not a scale |
| `Dw_decomposed` | `calyx` only (base and aux): scale, shear and misalignment rotation |
| `R_wtoI`, `U_w` | `calyx`, base IMU only: the split above |
| `td` | aux only: IMU–IMU time offset, s |
| `p_IsinI_m` | aux only: lever arm to the base IMU, m |
| `gyro_only` | aux only, present when the IMU was calibrated gyro-only |

The layout of `imu_intr_xi`:

| Convention | $\mathbf{D}_w$ | $\mathbf{D}_a$ | $\mathbf{T}_g$ (column-major) | rotation vector |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `kalibr` | `[0:6]` | `[6:12]` | `[12:21]` | `[21:24]`, ${}^{I}_{w}\mathbf{R}$ |
| `rpng` | `[0:6]` | `[6:12]` | `[12:21]` | `[21:24]`, ${}^{I}_{a}\mathbf{R}$ |
| `calyx` | `[0:9]` | `[9:15]` | `[15:24]` | none |

### With MVIS

[`mvis.recalib.load_rig_calib`](/reference/python-api/) reads any convention
and applies the inverse model above:

```python
from mvis.recalib import load_rig_calib

imu = load_rig_calib("cam_imu_calib.yaml").base_imu
w, a = imu.intr.correct(w_m, a_m, imu.b_g, imu.b_a)   # true omega, true specific force in {I}
```

### With numpy only

Read the YAML with `yaml.safe_load` and pass one `imus:` entry. The `Dw` and
`Da` matrices come straight from the file; $\mathbf{T}_g$ and the rotation come
from `imu_intr_xi`. `w_m` and `a_m` are length-3 arrays of one sample, with the
accel already shifted by $t_{ga}$ ([below](#shifting-the-accel-by-t_ga)).

```python title="kalibr"
import numpy as np
from scipy.spatial.transform import Rotation

def correct_kalibr(w_m, a_m, imu):
    xi = np.asarray(imu["imu_intr_xi"])
    Dw, Da = np.asarray(imu["Dw"]), np.asarray(imu["Da"])   # lower-triangular
    Tg = xi[12:21].reshape(3, 3, order="F")
    R_wtoI = Rotation.from_rotvec(xi[21:24]).as_matrix()
    a = Da @ (a_m - imu["b_a"])                     # accel triad = {I}
    w = R_wtoI @ Dw @ (w_m - imu["b_g"] - Tg @ a)
    return w, a
```

```python title="rpng"
import numpy as np
from scipy.spatial.transform import Rotation

def correct_rpng(w_m, a_m, imu):
    xi = np.asarray(imu["imu_intr_xi"])
    Dw, Da = np.asarray(imu["Dw"]), np.asarray(imu["Da"])   # upper-triangular
    Tg = xi[12:21].reshape(3, 3, order="F")
    R_atoI = Rotation.from_rotvec(xi[21:24]).as_matrix()
    a = R_atoI @ Da @ (a_m - imu["b_a"])
    w = Dw @ (w_m - imu["b_g"] - Tg @ a)            # gyro triad = {I}
    return w, a
```

```python title="calyx"
import numpy as np

def correct_calyx(w_m, a_m, imu):
    xi = np.asarray(imu["imu_intr_xi"])
    Dw, Da = np.asarray(imu["Dw"]), np.asarray(imu["Da"])   # full, upper-triangular
    Tg = xi[15:24].reshape(3, 3, order="F")
    a = Da @ (a_m - imu["b_a"])                     # accel triad = {I}
    w = Dw @ (w_m - imu["b_g"] - Tg @ a)            # Dw already holds the triad rotation
    return w, a
```

We checked all three against `ImuModel.correct` in MVIS on random
intrinsics; they agree to machine precision.

### Shifting the accel by t_ga

Do this first, on the whole recording: re-sample the accel channel at each
stamp plus $t_{ga}$, so that gyro and accel describe the same instant.

```python
import numpy as np

def shift_accel(t, acc, t_ga):
    """t: (N,) stamps in s, sorted; acc: (N, 3) raw accel. Returns acc at t + t_ga."""
    return np.stack([np.interp(t + t_ga, t, acc[:, k]) for k in range(3)], axis=1)
```

### What `mvis apply-calib` does

[`mvis apply-calib`](/guides/roundtrip/) writes a corrected copy of a ROS1 bag
([`src/mvis/recalib/apply.py`](https://github.com/openmvis/openmvis/blob/main/src/mvis/recalib/apply.py)).
On each IMU message, in this order:

1. **t_ga:** the accel is linearly re-interpolated at header stamp + $t_{ga}$
   (`--no-t-ga` skips it).
2. **Intrinsics and bias:** the inverse model above subtracts the saved
   $\mathbf{b}_g$, $\mathbf{b}_a$ and removes $\mathbf{D}_w$, $\mathbf{D}_a$,
   $\mathbf{T}_g$ and the triad rotation. `--keep-bias` leaves the biases in;
   `--no-imu-intrinsics` removes only the biases.
3. **td:** the header stamp becomes stamp + `td` (`--no-td` keeps the original).

An auxiliary IMU entry without `imu_intr_xi` is read with identity intrinsics,
so only its saved bias (if any) and its stamps change.

## t_ga: the gyro–accel offset

$t_{ga}$ is the time offset between the gyroscope and accelerometer channels
inside one IMU.

Many drivers publish gyro and accel together under one stamp, yet the two
channels are sampled at different rates and instants. MVIS takes the shared
stamp as the gyro clock and models the accel stored at $t$ as sampled at
$t - t_{ga}$.

```yaml title="cam_imu.yaml"
solve:
  estimate_t_ga: true    # default in camera–IMU runs
  sigma_t_ga: 0.010      # weak prior, s
```

- **In the solve:** the IMU factor corrects to first order,
  $\mathbf{a}_m(t + t_{ga}) \approx \mathbf{a}_m(t) + t_{ga}\,\dot{\mathbf{a}}_m(t)$,
  with $\dot{\mathbf{a}}_m$ from finite differences of the raw accel. That adds
  one Jacobian column; through $\mathbf{T}_g$ it also reaches the gyro row. When
  $t_{ga}$ moves more than 3 ms from where the factors were integrated, they are
  integrated again (`tga_relin_threshold_s`).
- **Reported** in the calibration YAML, the `cam_imu.json` summary
  (`t_ga_ms`) and the report.
- **Corrected** by `mvis apply-calib`, as above.
- **Checked** by [`mvis roundtrip`](/guides/roundtrip/): after correction a
  re-calibration should find $t_{ga}$ near zero.

**Why it matters:** a tool without $t_{ga}$ puts part of that delay into the
camera $t_d$. On real devices a single $t_{ga}$ can also depend on the motion.
Both are shown in [Why calibrate t_ga](/why/tga/).

## Gyro-only auxiliaries

The other answer to an untrustworthy accelerometer is to skip it:

```yaml title="cam_imu.yaml"
input:
  imus:
    - {}                    # base IMU
    - {gyro_only: true}     # auxiliary IMU
```

What a gyro-only IMU solves and pins:

- **Solved:** extrinsic rotation, `td` (here the IMU-to-IMU time offset),
  $\mathbf{D}_w$ and the gyro bias.
- **Pinned:** the lever arm (the translation between the two IMUs),
  $\mathbf{D}_a$ and $\mathbf{T}_g$.

It is **not allowed on the base IMU**: the base IMU needs its accelerometer for
gravity and metric scale. The result marks each such IMU with `gyro_only: true`.

We built it for the Intel T265 (Bosch BMI055), whose 62.5 Hz accel the driver
interpolates to 200 Hz. We calibrated, corrected the bag and calibrated again.
Without gyro-only, the T265's `td` moved from +8.6 ms to −9.0 ms between the two
runs. With gyro-only it moved from +0.9 ms to +0.4 ms.

## Which to reach for

Both address an accelerometer that is out of step with its gyro:

| Option | What it does | Prefer it for |
|---|---|---|
| `estimate_t_ga` | **Measures** the offset | The base IMU, which must use its accel; the value itself is worth having |
| `gyro_only` | **Avoids** the accelerometer | An auxiliary IMU whose accel adds nothing you need |

## What is observable

Each term needs the right motion:

- **Scale and misalignment** need rotation and acceleration on every axis.
- **The triad rotation** needs rotation about several axes while the
  accelerometer sees gravity from several directions.
- **G-sensitivity** $\mathbf{T}_g$ needs rotation and strong linear
  acceleration at the same time. Most hand-held motions do not produce that,
  which is why the default leaves it out.
- **$t_{ga}$** needs the specific force to change quickly, so that a time shift
  changes the reading.

When a freed term returns near its start value with a wide covariance, it was
not observed. See [Observability](/concepts/observability/) and
[Degeneracy](/concepts/degeneracy/).
