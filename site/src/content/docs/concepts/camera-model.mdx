Use this page to pick the camera model for each camera in your rig, and to see
the exact equations MVIS uses.

A camera model $\Pi$ maps a 3D point $\mathbf{P} = [X\ Y\ Z]^\top$ in the camera
frame $\{C_i\}$ to a pixel $[u\ v]^\top$: a projection plus a lens distortion.
Symbols follow [Notation & glossary](/concepts/notation/).

Each model lives in one class under
[`src/mvis/camera/`](https://github.com/openmvis/openmvis/tree/main/src/mvis/camera).
The class holds the projection, its Jacobians (the derivatives the optimiser
uses) and the unprojection. So every model works:

- in **camera-only and camera–IMU** calibration;
- with a calibration board of known corner positions, and in **feature mode**,
  where the corners' 3D positions are not given and are solved as well
  (example: [VI-Rig 4 IMUs + 3 cams, ArUco](/datasets/virig-4imus-3cams-aruco/));
- **mixed across cameras** in one rig.

## The eight models

| Model | Config value | Origin | Distortion coefficients, in file order | Suits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pinhole + radial-tangential | `radtan` | Brown–Conrady | `k1 k2 p1 p2` | Normal lenses |
| Kannala–Brandt KB4 | `kb4` (also `fisheye`, `kannala_brandt`) | Kannala–Brandt, $\theta$ from atan2 | `k1 k2 k3 k4` | Fisheye, including corners past 90° |
| Equidistant | `equidistant` | Kannala–Brandt on $X/Z, Y/Z$ | `k1 k2 k3 k4` | Fisheye with every corner under 90° |
| FOV | `fov` | Devernay–Faugeras | `omega` | Wide-angle, one parameter |
| Double Sphere | `double_sphere` | Usenko et al. (Basalt) | `xi alpha` | Very wide field of view |
| EUCM | `eucm` | Khomutenko et al. | `alpha beta` | Very wide field of view |
| UCM | `ucm` | Unified model (Mei), $\alpha$ form | `alpha` | Very wide field of view; Kalibr `omni` / `none` |
| Omni + radtan | `omni_radtan` | Mei, **experimental** | `xi k1 k2 p1 p2` | Catadioptric, ultra-wide |

Every model shares the four intrinsics $f_u, f_v, c_u, c_v$ (`fx fy cx cy`).
The distortion coefficients follow them.

```yaml title="config.yaml"
cameras:
  - camera_id: 0
    distortion_model: kb4
```

## Projection equations

Each model ends with the same affine step from a normalised point
$[m_x\ m_y]^\top$ to pixels:

$$
u = f_u\, m_x + c_u, \qquad v = f_v\, m_y + c_v
$$

What differs is how $[m_x\ m_y]$ comes from $\mathbf{P}$.
A point outside a model's valid domain has no pixel. That corner is left out
of the error statistics and, in camera–IMU, out of the cost. Left uncounted,
it would make the error look smaller than it is. So the result counts dropped
corners:

- `past_fold`: a Kannala–Brandt corner past the fold;
- `other_invalid`: anything else, such as $Z \le 0$ under `equidistant`.

### radtan

Source: [`src/mvis/camera/pinhole_radtan.py`](https://github.com/openmvis/openmvis/blob/main/src/mvis/camera/pinhole_radtan.py) (GTSAM `Cal3DS2`).

$$
x = \frac{X}{Z},\quad y = \frac{Y}{Z},\quad r^2 = x^2 + y^2
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_x &= x\,(1 + k_1 r^2 + k_2 r^4) + 2 p_1 x y + p_2\,(r^2 + 2x^2) \\
m_y &= y\,(1 + k_1 r^2 + k_2 r^4) + p_1\,(r^2 + 2y^2) + 2 p_2 x y
\end{aligned}
$$

**Valid:** $Z > 0$.

### kb4 and equidistant

Both use the Kannala–Brandt odd polynomial in the incidence angle $\theta$
(the angle of the ray off the optical axis):

$$
\theta_d = \theta\,(1 + k_1\theta^2 + k_2\theta^4 + k_3\theta^6 + k_4\theta^8)
$$

$$
m_x = \frac{\theta_d}{\rho}\, X, \qquad m_y = \frac{\theta_d}{\rho}\, Y, \qquad \rho = \sqrt{X^2 + Y^2}
$$

The two differ only in how they get $\theta$:

| Model | $\theta$ | Valid domain |
|---|---|---|
| `kb4` | $\theta = \operatorname{atan2}(\rho, Z)$ | $0 \le \theta < \pi$ and $\theta$ below the first fold $\theta_f$ |
| `equidistant` | $\theta = \arctan(\rho / Z)$, after dividing by $Z$ | $Z > 0$, so $\theta < 90°$ |

The **first fold** $\theta_f$ is the smallest $\theta > 0$ where
$\mathrm{d}\theta_d / \mathrm{d}\theta = 1 + 3k_1\theta^2 + 5k_2\theta^4 + 7k_3\theta^6 + 9k_4\theta^8 = 0$
($\pi$ when there is none). Past it, $\theta_d$ falls again and two rays share
one pixel, so the inverse is no longer unique.

- **Below 90°** the two models give the same pixels for the same coefficients.
- **At or past 90°** only `kb4` has a pixel. This is why `fisheye` means `kb4`
  ([Why kb4 for fisheye](/why/kb4/)).
- **After a solve** MVIS logs the fold angle and the radius in pixels where the
  valid domain ends. It warns when that radius lies inside the image.

Source: [`src/mvis/camera/kb4.py`](https://github.com/openmvis/openmvis/blob/main/src/mvis/camera/kb4.py),
[`src/mvis/camera/kb_domain.py`](https://github.com/openmvis/openmvis/blob/main/src/mvis/camera/kb_domain.py),
[`src/mvis/camera/pinhole_equidistant.py`](https://github.com/openmvis/openmvis/blob/main/src/mvis/camera/pinhole_equidistant.py) (GTSAM `Cal3Fisheye`).

### fov

Source: [`src/mvis/camera/fov_model.py`](https://github.com/openmvis/openmvis/blob/main/src/mvis/camera/fov_model.py).

$$
x = \frac{X}{Z},\quad y = \frac{Y}{Z},\quad r_u = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2},\quad
r_d = \frac{1}{\omega}\arctan\!\Big(2\, r_u \tan\frac{\omega}{2}\Big)
$$

$$
m_x = \frac{r_d}{r_u}\, x, \qquad m_y = \frac{r_d}{r_u}\, y
$$

On the optical axis the scale $r_d / r_u$ is its limit $2\tan(\omega/2)/\omega$.
As $\omega \to 0$ the model becomes a plain pinhole.
**Valid:** $Z > 0$.

### double_sphere

Source: [`src/mvis/camera/double_sphere.py`](https://github.com/openmvis/openmvis/blob/main/src/mvis/camera/double_sphere.py).

$$
d_1 = \lVert \mathbf{P} \rVert, \qquad
d_2 = \sqrt{X^2 + Y^2 + (\xi d_1 + Z)^2}
$$

$$
w = \alpha\, d_2 + (1 - \alpha)(\xi d_1 + Z), \qquad
m_x = \frac{X}{w}, \quad m_y = \frac{Y}{w}
$$

**Valid:** $w > 0$. Expected ranges: $\xi \in [-1, 1]$, $\alpha \in [0, 1]$.

### eucm

Source: [`src/mvis/camera/eucm.py`](https://github.com/openmvis/openmvis/blob/main/src/mvis/camera/eucm.py).

$$
d = \sqrt{\beta\,(X^2 + Y^2) + Z^2}, \qquad
w = \alpha\, d + (1 - \alpha) Z, \qquad
m_x = \frac{X}{w}, \quad m_y = \frac{Y}{w}
$$

**Valid:** $w > 0$. Expected ranges: $\alpha \in [0, 1]$, $\beta > 0$.

### ucm

Source: [`src/mvis/camera/ucm.py`](https://github.com/openmvis/openmvis/blob/main/src/mvis/camera/ucm.py).
UCM is EUCM with $\beta = 1$:

$$
d = \lVert \mathbf{P} \rVert, \qquad
w = \alpha\, d + (1 - \alpha) Z, \qquad
m_x = \frac{X}{w}, \quad m_y = \frac{Y}{w}
$$

**Valid:** $Z > -w_f\, d$, with
$w_f = (1-\alpha)/\alpha$ for $\alpha > 0.5$, else $w_f = \alpha/(1-\alpha)$.
Past that bound the image folds back. Expected range: $\alpha \in [0, 1)$.

Kalibr's `omni` camera with distortion `none` is the same model in $\xi$ form.
MVIS converts on import and export only:

$$
\xi = \frac{\alpha}{1 - \alpha}, \qquad \gamma = \frac{f}{1 - \alpha}
$$

### omni_radtan (experimental)

Source: [`src/mvis/camera/omni_radtan.py`](https://github.com/openmvis/openmvis/blob/main/src/mvis/camera/omni_radtan.py).
A projection onto the unit sphere with mirror parameter $\xi$, then radtan
distortion on the normalised plane:

$$
d = \lVert \mathbf{P} \rVert, \qquad
x = \frac{X}{Z + \xi d}, \quad y = \frac{Y}{Z + \xi d}
$$

then $m_x, m_y$ from $x, y$ exactly as in [radtan](#radtan).
**Valid:** $Z + \xi d > 0$. Expected range: $\xi \in [0, 5]$.
This is Kalibr's `omni` / `radtan`.

All projection kernels outside GTSAM are in
[`src/mvis/calibration/factors/custom_projections.py`](https://github.com/openmvis/openmvis/blob/main/src/mvis/calibration/factors/custom_projections.py),
with analytical Jacobians.

## Choosing one

More parameters are not better. A model richer than the lens needs fits
noise. On wide field-of-view data it can slide into a basin (a wrong local
minimum) that looks converged and is not.

Choose by the largest incidence angle of the board corners, not by the lens's
advertised field of view. Every result reports this angle
(`max_theta_deg`, and `max_theta_used_deg` for the corners the fit kept), and
counts any corners past the model's valid domain.

- **Normal lens:** `radtan`.
- **Fisheye:** `kb4`, which is what `fisheye` and `kannala_brandt` select. It
  stays valid past 90° up to its first fold ([why](/why/kb4/)).
- **Every corner under 90°, and you want equidistant by name:** `equidistant`.
  It gives the same pixels as `kb4` there.
- **Very wide field of view, a different shape:** `eucm`, `ucm` or `double_sphere`.
- **Catadioptric** (lens plus mirror): `omni_radtan`. It is experimental.

### Kalibr export

The camchain written for Kalibr uses these names:

| MVIS | Kalibr `camera_model` / `distortion_model` |
|---|---|
| `radtan` | `pinhole` / `radtan` |
| `equidistant` | `pinhole` / `equidistant` |
| `kb4` | `pinhole` / `equidistant`: exact for $Z > 0$; MVIS warns when the fit used corners at or past 90°, which Kalibr cannot project |
| `fov` | `pinhole` / `fov` |
| `double_sphere` | `ds` / `none` |
| `eucm` | `eucm` / `none` |
| `ucm` | `omni` / `none`, converted to $\xi$ form |
| `omni_radtan` | `omni` / `radtan` |

:::caution[Wide-FoV models can diverge on stereo]
In our TUM-VI runs, stereo `double_sphere` and `eucm` fits can diverge into a
basin where the focal length and $\xi$ or $\alpha$ trade off, while the
**single-camera** fit is stable. The working recipe has two steps:

1. Fit each camera alone.
2. Pin those intrinsics for the joint solve
   ([`examples/configs/tumvi_cam_imu/`](https://github.com/openmvis/openmvis/tree/main/examples/configs/tumvi_cam_imu)).

You recognise a diverged fit by an implausible focal length or very large
distortion coefficients, not by a large residual.
:::

Camera-only calibration runs two passes: a first solve, then a second one
after outlier rejection. A **guardrail** keeps the first-pass result if the
second pass lands on any of these
([`src/mvis/calibration/pipeline_cam.py`](https://github.com/openmvis/openmvis/blob/main/src/mvis/calibration/pipeline_cam.py)):

- a focal length of 1 px or less;
- $f_u$ and $f_v$ more than 30 % apart;
- a distortion coefficient above 2 in magnitude (for `ucm`: $\alpha$ outside
  $[0, 1)$; for `omni_radtan`: $\xi$ outside $[0, 5]$);
- a 95th-percentile reprojection error more than 1.5 times the first pass's,
  on a camera whose $f_u$ moved less than 5 %;
- a mean RMS reprojection error over all cameras more than 1.5 times the first
  pass's.

It is a backstop, not a substitute for picking the right model.

## Rolling shutter

A rolling-shutter camera exposes its rows one after another, so one image
spans a range of times. The readout $t_r$ is the time from the first row to
the last.

A corner observed in row $v$ of an image of height $H$ was captured at

$$
t_I = t_C + t_d + \eta(v)\, t_r
$$

The row factor $\eta(v)$ depends on which row the driver stamps
(`rolling_mode`, default 0):

| `rolling_mode` | Frame stamp at | $\eta(v)$ |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | top row | $v / H$ |
| 1 | middle row | $v / H - 0.5$ |
| 2 | bottom row | $(v - H) / H$ |

Source: [`src/mvis/core/shutter.py`](https://github.com/openmvis/openmvis/blob/main/src/mvis/core/shutter.py).
For a global-shutter camera $t_r = 0$, so every corner of a frame shares one time.

The camera–IMU vision factor uses that per-corner time directly. It takes the
pose of $\{I\}$ at $t_I$ from the base IMU, composes the extrinsics, and then
projects:

$$
\mathbf{z}_{i,k} = \Pi\big({}^{C_i}\mathbf{p}_{k}(t_I)\big) + \mathbf{n}
$$

where ${}^{C_i}\mathbf{p}_k(t_I)$ is board corner $k$ in the camera frame at
the corner's capture time. Projection, time offset, readout and extrinsics sit
in one factor, so $t_d$ and $t_r$ get Jacobians and are solved with everything
else, not corrected afterwards. The HTML report uses the same projection code,
so the residuals it shows are the ones the optimiser minimised.

Turn it on per camera:

```yaml title="config.yaml"
cameras:
  - camera_id: 0
    shutter_type: rolling
```

Or through the `estimate:` block, with `estimate.camera.tr: true`.
Practical guidance: [Rolling shutter](/guides/rolling-shutter/).
