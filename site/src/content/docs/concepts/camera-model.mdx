Use this page to pick the camera model for each camera in your rig.
A camera model maps a 3D point to a pixel: a projection plus a lens distortion.

MVIS implements eight distortion models. Each lives in one place:
`camera/CameraModel` holds its projection, Jacobians (the derivatives the
optimiser uses) and unprojection. So the same models are available:

- in **camera-only and camera–IMU** calibration;
- in board and feature mode;
- **mixed across cameras** in a single rig.

Models outside GTSAM's built-ins carry custom analytical Jacobians.

Symbols follow [Notation & glossary](/concepts/notation/).

## The eight models

| Model | Config value | Origin | Suits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pinhole + radial-tangential | `radtan` | Brown–Conrady | Normal lenses |
| Kannala–Brandt KB4 | `kb4` (also `fisheye`, `kannala_brandt`) | Kannala–Brandt, θ from atan2 | Fisheye, including corners past 90° |
| Equidistant | `equidistant` | Kannala–Brandt on x/z | Fisheye with every corner under 90° (the same pixels as `kb4` there) |
| FOV | `fov` | Devernay–Faugeras | Wide-angle, single parameter |
| Double Sphere | `double_sphere` | Usenko / Basalt | Very wide FoV |
| EUCM | `eucm` | Khomutenko | Very wide FoV |
| UCM | `ucm` | Unified (Mei), α form | Very wide FoV; Kalibr `omni-none` |
| Omni + radtan | `omni_radtan` | Mei — **experimental** | Catadioptric, ultra-wide |

```yaml title="config.yaml"
cameras:
  - camera_id: 0
    distortion_model: kb4
```

## Choosing one

More parameters is not better. A model richer than the lens needs will fit
noise. On wide-FoV (field of view) data it can slide into a basin (a wrong local
minimum) that looks converged and is not.

Choose by the largest incidence angle of the board corners, that is, their angle
off the optical axis. Do not choose by the lens's advertised field of view.
Every result reports this angle, with any corners past the model's valid domain.

- **Normal lens** → `radtan`.
- **Fisheye** → `kb4`, which is what `fisheye` and `kannala_brandt` select. It
  stays valid past 90° up to its first fold ([why](/why/kb4/)). It exports to Kalibr as
  pinhole-equi (exact below 90°; MVIS warns when the fit used corners past it).
- **Every corner under 90°, equidistant asked for** → `equidistant`. It gives the
  same pixels as `kb4` there; name it explicitly.
- **Very wide FoV, a different shape** → `eucm`, `ucm` or `double_sphere`.
- **Catadioptric** (lens plus mirror) → `omni_radtan`, with the caveat that it is
  experimental.

:::caution[Wide-FoV models can diverge on stereo]
On TUM-VI, stereo `double_sphere` and `eucm` fits diverge into an fx–xi /
alpha basin, while the **single-camera** fit is stable. The working recipe is
two-step:

1. Fit each camera alone.
2. Pin those intrinsics for the joint solve (`examples/configs/tumvi_cam_imu/`).

You recognise a diverged fit by an implausible focal length or blown-out
distortion, not by a large residual.
:::

MVIS guards against this with the two-pass **pathology guardrail**. It reverts
to pass 1 if the optimiser lands on any of these:

- a non-positive focal;
- blown-out distortion;
- a P95 regression (the 95th-percentile value gets worse).

It is a backstop, not a substitute for picking the right model.

## Rolling shutter

A rolling-shutter camera exposes rows one after another, so one image spans a
range of times. Set `shutter_type: rolling` and the per-row readout is solved
with everything else:

```yaml title="config.yaml"
cameras:
  - camera_id: 0
    shutter_type: rolling
```

These all live in **one unified factor** rather than being applied in sequence:

- projection;
- time offset;
- rolling shutter;
- extrinsic composition;
- SLERP pose interpolation (smooth blending between two poses).

That is what lets readout be estimated jointly rather than corrected
afterwards. The same projection code backs the HTML report. So reported
residuals are the ones the optimiser actually minimised.

Practical guidance: [Rolling shutter](/guides/rolling-shutter/).

## Validation

We compared the models over a 52-run benchmark across MVIS T265 and TUM-VI
1024 (`doc/investigation_ds_convergence.md`).

- **Camera–IMU path:** on TUM-VI raw IMU bags, `omni_radtan` and `equidistant`
  reproduce the Kalibr cam-IMU extrinsics to **≤2 mm lever, ≤0.15 mm stereo
  baseline, ≤1 ms time offset**.
- **`kb4` vs `equidistant`:** on TUM-VI and T265, where few or no corners pass 90°,
  the fits agree to about 2e-6 px and their camera–IMU extrinsics to 1e-11 rad.
  Past 90°, only `kb4` holds: [Why kb4 for fisheye](/why/kb4/).

Model choice does not change the geometry a good fit recovers. It changes
whether you get a good fit at all.
