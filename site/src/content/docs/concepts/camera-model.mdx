MVIS implements eight distortion models. Each lives in one place —
`camera/CameraModel` holds its projection, Jacobians, and unprojection — so the
same models are available in **camera-only and camera–IMU** calibration, in
board and feature mode, and can be **mixed across cameras** in a single rig.
Models outside GTSAM's built-ins carry custom analytical Jacobians.

Symbols follow [Notation & glossary](/concepts/notation/).

## The eight models

| Model | Config value | Origin | Suits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pinhole + radial-tangential | `radtan` | Brown–Conrady | Normal lenses |
| Kannala–Brandt KB4 | `kb4` (also `fisheye`, `kannala_brandt`) | Kannala–Brandt, θ from atan2 | Fisheye, including corners past 90° |
| Equidistant | `equidistant` | Kannala–Brandt on x/z | Fisheye with every corner under 90° (the same pixels as `kb4` there) |
| FOV | `fov` | Devernay–Faugeras | Wide-angle, single parameter |
| Double Sphere | `double_sphere` | Usenko / Basalt | Very wide FoV |
| EUCM | `eucm` | Khomutenko | Very wide FoV |
| UCM | `ucm` | Unified (Mei), α form | Very wide FoV; Kalibr `omni-none` |
| Omni + radtan | `omni_radtan` | Mei — **experimental** | Catadioptric, ultra-wide |

```yaml title="config.yaml"
cameras:
  - camera_id: 0
    distortion_model: kb4
```

## Choosing one

More parameters is not better. A model richer than the lens needs will fit
noise, and on wide-FoV data it can slide into a basin that looks converged and
is not.

Choose by the largest incidence angle of the board corners off the optical axis,
not by the lens's advertised field of view. Every result reports it, with any
corners past the model's valid domain.

- **Normal lens** → `radtan`.
- **Fisheye** → `kb4`, which is what `fisheye` and `kannala_brandt` select. It
  stays valid past 90° up to its first fold, and exports to Kalibr as
  pinhole-equi (exact below 90°; MVIS warns when the fit used corners past it).
- **Every corner under 90°, equidistant asked for** → `equidistant`. It gives the
  same pixels as `kb4` there; name it explicitly.
- **Very wide FoV, a different shape** → `eucm`, `ucm` or `double_sphere`.
- **Catadioptric** → `omni_radtan`, with the caveat that it is experimental.

:::caution[Wide-FoV models can diverge on stereo]
On TUM-VI, stereo `double_sphere` and `eucm` fits diverge into an fx–xi /
alpha basin, while the **single-camera** fit is stable. The working recipe is
two-step: fit each camera alone, then pin those intrinsics for the joint solve
(`examples/configs/tumvi_cam_imu/`). A diverged fit is recognisable by an
implausible focal length or blown-out distortion, not by a large residual.
:::

MVIS guards against this: the two-pass **pathology guardrail** reverts to
pass 1 if the optimiser lands on a non-positive focal, blown-out distortion, or
a P95 regression. It is a backstop, not a substitute for picking the right
model.

## Rolling shutter

A rolling-shutter camera exposes rows sequentially, so one image spans a range
of times. Set `shutter_type: rolling` and the per-row readout is solved with
everything else:

```yaml title="config.yaml"
cameras:
  - camera_id: 0
    shutter_type: rolling
```

Projection, time offset, rolling shutter, extrinsic composition, and SLERP pose
interpolation all live in **one unified factor** rather than being applied in
sequence. That is what lets readout be estimated jointly rather than corrected
afterwards — and the same projection code backs the HTML report, so reported
residuals are the ones the optimiser actually minimised.

Practical guidance: [Rolling shutter](/guides/rolling-shutter/).

## Validation

The models were compared over a 52-run benchmark across MVIS T265 and TUM-VI
1024 (`doc/investigation_ds_convergence.md`). On the camera–IMU path, TUM-VI raw
IMU bags show `omni_radtan` and `equidistant` reproducing the Kalibr cam-IMU
extrinsics to **≤2 mm lever, ≤0.15 mm stereo baseline, ≤1 ms time offset**.
On TUM-VI and T265, where no corner passes 90°, `kb4` and `equidistant` fits
agree to 1e-6 px and their camera–IMU extrinsics to 1e-11 rad.

Model choice does not change the geometry a good fit recovers — it changes
whether you get a good fit at all.
