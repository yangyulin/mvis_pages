Use this page to see what the camera–IMU solve minimises and how a board corner
ties the cameras to the IMU trajectory. The two IMU factors have their own pages:

- **[Base IMU factor](/concepts/camera-imu-math/base-imu/):** links consecutive
  states of the base IMU. Its intrinsics, biases and `t_ga` sit inside it.
- **[Auxiliary IMU factor](/concepts/camera-imu-math/aux-imu/):** each extra IMU
  sees the same motion through its own extrinsic, time offset and intrinsics.

A **factor** is one term of the cost. A **residual** is the error one factor
measures. The **base IMU** is the first IMU in the config; its clock is the
reference clock, and the trajectory is its pose over time. Symbols follow
[Notation & glossary](/concepts/notation/).

## The cost

The solve finds the one set of values that makes every measurement fit best,
given the priors. It minimises:

$$
\min_{\mathbf{x}}\;
\sum_{i,f}\rho\!\left(\big\|\mathbf{r}_{\text{cam}}^{i,f}\big\|^2_{\sigma_{px}}\right)
+\sum_{k}\big\|\mathbf{r}_{\text{imu}}^{k}\big\|^2_{\mathbf{P}_k}
+\sum_{j,k}\big\|\mathbf{r}_{\text{aux}}^{j,k}\big\|^2_{\mathbf{P}_{j,k}}
+\sum_{p}\big\|\mathbf{r}_{\text{prior}}^{p}\big\|^2_{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_p}
$$

- **$\mathbf{x}$:** everything solved: the IMU states, the board pose, and every
  calibration value. The full list is on [Calibration graph](/concepts/calibration-graph/).
- **$i, f$:** camera, frame. **$m$:** a board corner. [Notation](/concepts/notation/)
  writes the corner index as $k$; here $k$ is taken by the IMU states, so corners
  use $m$ ($h_m$ and $\mathbf{z}_{i,m}$ are Notation's $h_k$ and $\mathbf{z}_{i,k}$).
- **$\mathbf{r}_{\text{cam}}^{i,f}$:** the residuals of all corners $m$ of frame $f$
  in camera $i$, stacked into one vector (2 rows per corner).
- **$k$:** an interval between two IMU states. **$j$:** an auxiliary IMU.
- **$\rho$:** the Huber loss. It acts on a frame's stacked residual, not on single
  corners: past a whitened norm of 2 (2 px at $\sigma_{px}$ = 1 px) the error grows
  linearly instead of quadratically, so a frame with a large total error is
  down-weighted as a whole (`solve.huber_px`, default 2.0).
- **$\|\mathbf{r}\|^2_{\boldsymbol\Sigma} = \mathbf{r}^\top\boldsymbol\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf{r}$:**
  the squared error weighted by its covariance. Pixels use $\sigma_{px}$ = 1 px
  (`solve.sigma_px`). The IMU terms use $\mathbf{P}$, the covariance the
  integration propagates from the IMU noise densities.

The solver is Levenberg–Marquardt (a damped Gauss–Newton method) with analytic
Jacobians (the derivatives of each residual with respect to each variable).

## The reprojection factor

The reprojection factor asks: given the current estimate, where should board
corner $m$ appear in the image, and how far is that from where it was detected?

### When was the corner captured?

A frame carries one stamp $t_C$ on the camera clock. Two things move the real
capture time of each corner:

- **Time offset $t_{d,i}$:** the camera clock runs offset from the base-IMU clock.
- **Rolling shutter:** a rolling-shutter camera exposes its rows one after
  another. The whole frame takes the readout time $t_{r,i}$. A corner on row
  $v_m$ of an image $H$ rows high is captured later than the first row.

$$
t_m = t_C + t_{d,i} + \eta(v_m)\, t_{r,i},
\qquad
\eta(v_m) = \frac{v_m}{H}
$$

$\eta(v)$ is the row factor of [Notation](/concepts/notation/). $\eta(v_m) = v_m/H$
is for a stamp at the top row, the default. With `solve.rolling_mode: 1` (stamp at
the middle row) $\eta(v_m) = v_m/H - 0.5$; with `solve.rolling_mode: 2` (stamp at
the bottom row) $\eta(v_m) = v_m/H - 1$. The readout $t_{r,i}$ is solved only for a
rolling-shutter camera with `estimate.camera.tr: true` (off by default). Otherwise
the camera is treated as global shutter and $t_{r,i}$ is held at 0.

### Where was the IMU at that time?

The IMU states sit at discrete times $t_k$ (30 Hz by default, `solve.state_hz`).
A corner's capture time $t_m$ almost never lands on one. MVIS gets the pose at
$t_m$ from the IMU itself. This is `solve.vision_pose: imu`, the default.

Take the state $k$ at or just before the frame (the **anchor**). Integrate the
raw base-IMU samples from $t_k$ over the signed interval $\Delta_m = t_m - t_k$:

$$
{}^{G}_{I}\mathbf{R}(t_m) = {}^{G}_{I_k}\mathbf{R}\;\Delta\mathbf{R}_m
$$

$$
{}^{G}\mathbf{p}_{I}(t_m) = {}^{G}\mathbf{p}_{I_k} + {}^{G}\mathbf{v}_{I_k}\,\Delta_m
+ \tfrac12\,{}^{G}\mathbf{g}\,\Delta_m^2 + {}^{G}_{I_k}\mathbf{R}\;\Delta\mathbf{p}_m
$$

- **$\Delta\mathbf{R}_m$, $\Delta\mathbf{p}_m$:** the rotation and position change
  integrated from the IMU readings over $\Delta_m$, in the anchor's frame. They
  use the **corrected** readings: current biases, $\mathbf{D}_w$, $\mathbf{D}_a$,
  $\mathbf{T}_g$ and `t_ga` (see [Base IMU factor](/concepts/camera-imu-math/base-imu/#corrected-readings)).
- **${}^{G}\mathbf{g} = [0,\ 0,\ -9.81]^\top$ m/s²:** gravity in the global frame,
  which is gravity-aligned.
- **A negative $\Delta_m$** is integrated backwards in time. The window follows
  the current $t_{d,i}$ and $t_{r,i}$, so it moves as they change.

The corner pose therefore follows the measured motion between states (for
example 200 Hz IMU samples between 30 Hz states). There is no interpolation
error for the calibration values to absorb. The IMU velocity and intrinsics enter
the reprojection factor too, so the gyro scale is fitted by the camera and the
IMU terms together.

To save time the factor integrates once and corrects the result to first order
as the estimate moves (`solve.vision_imu_linearized`, default on). It integrates
again when a capture time has moved more than 1 ms.

:::note[The older model: interpolation]
`solve.vision_pose: interp` places the corner on the straight path between the
two neighbouring states. With $\lambda = (t_m - t_k)/(t_{k+1} - t_k)$:

$$
{}^{G}_{I}\mathbf{R}(t_m) = {}^{G}_{I_k}\mathbf{R}\,\mathrm{Exp}\!\big(\lambda\,\mathrm{Log}({}^{G}_{I_k}\mathbf{R}^\top\,{}^{G}_{I_{k+1}}\mathbf{R})\big),
\qquad
{}^{G}\mathbf{p}_{I}(t_m) = (1-\lambda)\,{}^{G}\mathbf{p}_{I_k} + \lambda\,{}^{G}\mathbf{p}_{I_{k+1}}
$$

Its error grows with angular acceleration times the state spacing squared. On
fast motion the time offsets, gyro scale and lever arm absorb it.
:::

### Project and compare

With the IMU pose at $t_m$, chain the transforms from the board to the camera:

$$
{}^{C_i}\mathbf{p}_m =
\big({}^{G}_{I}\mathbf{T}(t_m)\;{}^{I}_{C_i}\mathbf{T}\big)^{-1}\;
{}^{G}_{B}\mathbf{T}\;\big({}^{B}\mathbf{p}_m + h_m\,\mathbf{e}_z\big)
$$

$$
\mathbf{r}_{\text{cam}}^{i,f,m} =
\Pi\big({}^{C_i}\mathbf{p}_m;\ \mathbf{f}_i,\ \mathbf{d}_i\big) - \mathbf{z}_{i,m}
$$

- **${}^{B}\mathbf{p}_m$:** the corner's position on the board, from the board
  config.
- **$h_m$:** the corner's height off the flat board, along the board normal
  $\mathbf{e}_z$. It is 0 unless `estimate.board_points: true`
  ([Why calibrate the board shape](/why/board-shape/)).
- **${}^{G}_{B}\mathbf{T}$:** the board pose. The board stays still, so it is one
  pose for the whole recording.
- **$\Pi$:** the camera model, with intrinsics $\mathbf{f}_i$ and
  distortion $\mathbf{d}_i$ ([Camera model](/concepts/camera-model/)).
- **$\mathbf{z}_{i,m}$:** the detected pixel.

One factor holds all corners of one frame. It touches the anchor pose, the
anchor velocity and biases, the board pose, the camera's calibration
(${}^{I}_{C_i}\mathbf{T}$, $t_{d,i}$, $t_{r,i}$, $\mathbf{f}_i$, $\mathbf{d}_i$),
the IMU intrinsics, `t_ga`, and the board heights when they are solved.

### How the time offsets get their gradient

The time offsets have a derivative because the pose depends on the capture time:

$$
\frac{\partial\,{}^{G}_{I}\mathbf{T}(t_m)}{\partial t_{d,i}} \;\propto\;
\begin{bmatrix} {}^{I}\boldsymbol{\omega}(t_m) \\ {}^{G}\mathbf{v}_{I}(t_m) \end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\frac{\partial\,\mathbf{r}_{\text{cam}}}{\partial t_{r,i}} = \eta(v_m)\,\frac{\partial\,\mathbf{r}_{\text{cam}}}{\partial t_{d,i}}
$$

So $t_{d,i}$ is seen only while the rig turns or moves, and $t_{r,i}$ only when
corners on different rows move differently. [Observability](/concepts/observability/)
works this through.

## Why calibration sits inside the factors

One could integrate the IMU with fixed values and correct the result later. The
solver could not move those values then: the correction has no derivative with
respect to them.

MVIS puts every calibration value inside the residual instead:

- the IMU intrinsics $\mathbf{D}_w$, $\mathbf{D}_a$, $\mathbf{T}_g$ and the biases;
- the extrinsics ${}^{I}_{C_i}\mathbf{T}$ and ${}^{I}_{I_{s_j}}\mathbf{T}$;
- the time offsets $t_{d,i}$, $t_{d,j}$, `t_ga` and the readout $t_{r,i}$.

Each then has a Jacobian, and the optimiser can move it. The price: the IMU
integrals depend on the values being solved. The factors carry first-order
corrections for small changes and integrate again when a value moves far.

## What each factor constrains

| Factor | Constrains directly | Constrains through the others |
|---|---|---|
| Reprojection | camera intrinsics, ${}^{I}_{C_i}\mathbf{T}$, $t_{d,i}$, $t_{r,i}$, board pose and shape | the trajectory, and the gyro scale through the corner poses |
| Base IMU | biases, $\mathbf{D}_w$, $\mathbf{D}_a$, $\mathbf{T}_g$, `t_ga` | metric trajectory, gravity direction |
| Auxiliary IMU | ${}^{I}_{I_{s_j}}\mathbf{T}$, $t_{d,j}$, its own intrinsics and `t_ga` | the base trajectory, through the shared motion |

No single factor sees the whole state. Which values the data can fix depends on
the motion: see [Observability](/concepts/observability/) and
[Degeneracy](/concepts/degeneracy/).

## What this means for your recording and setup

- **Turn and move while the board is in view.** The time offsets and the readout
  get their signal only from motion.
- **Fill the image rows.** The readout is fitted from how the error changes from
  the top row to the bottom row.
- **Give the solve a good camera prior.** The camera intrinsics are refined here,
  but they start from the camera-only result
  ([step 3](/guides/camera-calibration/)).
- **Use the cleanest camera as the first camera.** Its frames set the state times.
  A global-shutter, low-latency camera is best
  ([step 4](/guides/camera-imu-calibration/)).
