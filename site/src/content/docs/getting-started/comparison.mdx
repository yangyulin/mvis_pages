Use this page to see where MVIS sits next to Kalibr and Basalt, the toolboxes
most people already use. Kalibr is mature and widely validated. MVIS is not
trying to displace it on the single-IMU case it does well.

An IMU (inertial measurement unit) is the sensor with a gyroscope and an
accelerometer.

Checked against MVIS 0.1.0 and the benchmark batch 2026-11-10a.

## What is actually different

- **No ROS.** MVIS is pure Python via `pip`, on Linux and macOS.
  Bags are read with `rosbags`. If you calibrate outside a ROS environment,
  this is the whole story.
- **More than one IMU, each with its own mode.** MVIS puts every IMU in one
  graph, and each IMU can run gyro-only when its accelerometer is unreliable.
  Kalibr also solves several IMUs in one run. Basalt takes one IMU per run, so
  a four-IMU rig is calibrated IMU by IMU and composed.
- **It fits the gyro–accel offset.** `t_ga` is the time offset between the
  gyroscope and accelerometer channels. It is on by default in camera–IMU runs.
  Kalibr and Basalt have one IMU clock.
  On the same bags their `td` differs from MVIS by about 2.9–3.2 ms on Looper
  Insight 9 and 1.9–2.0 ms on the T265 (first camera); an injection sweep predicts 3.0–3.2 and
  1.6–1.7 ms of that from the offset alone. Whose `td` is closer to the truth is
  not yet tested. [Details](/why/tga/).
- **It can solve the board shape.** With `estimate.board_points: true` MVIS
  solves one height per corner. Kalibr and Basalt take the board as flat. On
  our VI-Rig mount a flat solve shifts the focal length, gyro scale and lever.
  [Details](/why/board-shape/).
- **Rolling shutter in the camera–IMU solve.** MVIS solves the readout time
  together with the IMU. Kalibr's rolling-shutter tool is camera-only.
- **Validation is a command, not a habit.** `mvis roundtrip` gives a
  PASS/FAIL. A PASS requires IMU intrinsics to collapse *and* geometry to
  hold. Nothing comparable ships with the others.
- **Discrete-time.** MVIS places state nodes at `state_hz`. Kalibr and Basalt
  fit a B-spline (a smooth continuous-time curve). The trade-off is in
  [Continuous-time refinement](/concepts/continuous-time/).

## Where the others are still ahead

- **Maturity.** Kalibr has a decade of use across the field. MVIS's
  [benchmark](/benchmark/results/) runs all three tools on the same public
  recordings, but that is not the same thing.
- **Ecosystem.** Kalibr's target generators, tutorials, and community answers
  have no MVIS equivalent yet.
- **Continuous-time.** Are your sensors genuinely asynchronous at high rate?
  Then a B-spline handles it natively. MVIS does not yet offer it.
- **Licence.** Kalibr and Basalt are BSD. MVIS is GPL-3.0-only, with a
  commercial licence for closed-source products.

## Agreement with Kalibr

This is not a rival claim; it is a validation. On TUM-VI, in benchmark batch
2026-11-10a, MVIS and Kalibr agree to:

- **Camera stereo (raw, 8 recordings):** focal lengths within 0.2 px and the
  stereo baseline within 0.001 mm.
- **Camera + IMU (raw and 512, 4 recordings each):** lever (camera-to-IMU
  distance) within 1.8 mm.
- **Time offset:** MVIS's `td` is 0.9 ms above Kalibr's. MVIS splits the gyro
  and accel timelines with `t_ga`; Basalt, which has one IMU clock like Kalibr,
  is within 0.1 ms of Kalibr.

Every row, with its spread, is on the
[TUM-VI eval page](/benchmark/results/tum-vi/).

MVIS also reads Kalibr output directly (`mvis.io.kalibr`). So Kalibr is a
natural source of initial values. Refinement from a Kalibr camchain is a
supported workflow, not a migration.

## Which to use

- **One IMU, one or two cameras, ROS available** → Kalibr.
- **A gyro-only auxiliary IMU** → MVIS.
- **Several IMUs** → MVIS or Kalibr.
- **No ROS on the machine** → MVIS.
- **Rolling shutter with an IMU** → MVIS.
- **You need to prove the calibration is right** → MVIS's round-trip.

Results on shared datasets are on the
[leaderboard](/benchmark/results/). Read
[Methodology](/benchmark/methodology/) first. These datasets have no ground
truth, so differences show disagreement, not accuracy.
