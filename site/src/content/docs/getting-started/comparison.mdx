Use this page to see where MVIS sits next to Kalibr and Basalt, the two
open-source camera–IMU toolboxes most people use. Kalibr is mature and widely
validated, and MVIS does not aim to replace it where it works well.

- **Checked against:** MVIS 0.1.0 and our benchmark runs of 10 November 2026
  ([Methodology](/benchmark/methodology/)).
- **The numbers:** per-dataset results of all three tools are on the
  [Leaderboard](/benchmark/results/).

## What is different

- **No ROS, no C++ build.** MVIS installs with `pip` and reads ROS1 and ROS2
  bags itself. Kalibr needs a ROS workspace; Basalt needs a C++ build.
  [Requirements](/getting-started/requirements/).
- **More than one IMU, each with its own mode.** MVIS puts every IMU in one
  graph, and each auxiliary IMU (every IMU but the base) can run gyro-only
  when its accelerometer is unreliable.
  Kalibr also solves several IMUs in one run. Basalt takes one IMU per run, so
  a four-IMU rig is calibrated IMU by IMU and composed.
- **It fits the gyro–accel offset.** `t_ga` is the time offset between the
  gyroscope and accelerometer channels of one IMU. It is on by default in
  camera–IMU runs. Kalibr and Basalt give an IMU one clock.
  - **On two real IMUs** (the Looper Insight 9 and the T265) MVIS measures a
    `t_ga` of about −5 to −6 ms.
  - **There, Kalibr's and Basalt's camera time offset `td`** differs from MVIS
    by about 1.6–3.2 ms. A sweep in which we delay only the accel predicts most of
    that gap from `t_ga` alone.
  - **Not yet tested:** which `td` is closer to the truth. [Details](/why/tga/).
- **It can solve the board shape.** With `estimate.board_points: true` MVIS
  solves one height per corner. Kalibr and Basalt take the board as flat. On
  our VI-Rig mount a flat solve shifts the focal length, gyro scale and lever.
  [Details](/why/board-shape/).
- **Rolling shutter in the camera–IMU solve.** MVIS solves the readout time
  together with the IMU. Kalibr's rolling-shutter tool is camera-only.
- **Validation is a command.** `mvis roundtrip` applies the calibration to
  its own recording, calibrates again and prints PASS or FAIL. A PASS needs
  the IMU intrinsics to come back as identity *and* the geometry to stay put.
  Neither Kalibr nor Basalt ships a check like it.
  [Round-trip validation](/guides/roundtrip/).
- **Discrete-time.** MVIS places pose, velocity and bias states at a fixed
  rate (`state_hz`, 30 Hz by default). Kalibr and Basalt fit a B-spline, a
  smooth curve in continuous time.
