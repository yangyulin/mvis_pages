Use this page to decide whether to calibrate `t_ga` (the time offset between the gyroscope and the accelerometer
inside one IMU), and to see what happens to `td` (the camera–IMU time offset) when a tool cannot model it.

MVIS fits `t_ga`. Kalibr (one time shift per camera) and Basalt (one camera time offset for the whole rig) treat the
IMU as one clock. If the accelerometer is off that clock, the offset has to go somewhere else.

**On two real IMUs, MVIS measures `t_ga` of about −5 to −6 ms. On the same bags, Kalibr's and Basalt's `td` differ
from MVIS by about 3 ms (Looper Insight 9) and about 2 ms (Intel T265). The sweep below predicts 3.0–3.2 ms and
1.6–1.7 ms of that from the offset alone.**

## Short answer

- **Turn it on:** the default is off.

  ```yaml title="cam_imu.yaml"
  solve:
    estimate_t_ga: true
    sigma_t_ga: 0.010   # seconds: a weak 10 ms prior, as in the runs here
  ```

  See [Configuration](/reference/configuration/) and [Estimate `t_ga`](/guides/camera-imu-calibration/).
- **Sign:** the accel stored at stamp `t` is taken as sampled at `t − t_ga`. A negative `t_ga` means the accel is
  stamped early: it leads the gyro on the stamp timeline. Both devices here read negative.
- **If `t_ga` is near zero** (well under 1 ms, within 2σ): MVIS sees no offset under its pure-shift model. That does
  not rule out a frequency-dependent response like the T265's. A real-data control on such an IMU has not been run yet.
- **If `t_ga` is 1 ms or more in size:** for an offset that is a pure shift, as injected here, a one-clock tool moves `td` by
  0.28–0.64 × `t_ga`. Whether that is its error on raw data is not tested.

  | Size of `t_ga` | `td` moved, T265-like (× 0.28–0.30) | `td` moved, Looper-like (× 0.59–0.64) |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 ms | 0.3 ms | 0.6 ms |
  | **5 ms** | **1.4–1.5 ms** | **3.0–3.2 ms** |
  | 10 ms | 2.8–3.0 ms | 5.9–6.4 ms |
  | 20 ms | 5.6–6.0 ms | 11.8–12.8 ms |

  The share depends on the IMU and the motion. It is not a constant of the tool.
- **IMU scale moves very little:** under 0.06 % between τ = 0 and −5 ms. In this test the offset shows up in `td`,
  not in scale.
- **If you must use a one-clock tool:** expect `td` to carry part of the offset, or remove it first with
  [corrected-dataset export](/guides/corrected-datasets/). On the MVIS-corrected bags, Kalibr and Basalt read `td`
  within 0.3 ms of zero (T265 −0.16 / −0.20 and −0.28; Looper −0.03 / −0.04 and −0.01). That is a self-consistency
  read. Whether a pre-shift gives Kalibr or Basalt the true `td` is not tested.

been re-run on current code.

## The offset is real

- **What `t_ga` is:** see [IMU model](/concepts/imu-model/). A common cause is a driver that resamples or filters
  the accel. The T265 driver places a 62.5 Hz accel onto 200 Hz gyro stamps.
- **Looper Insight 9:** `t_ga` **−5.03 ± 0.02 ms** on looper_1.
- **Intel T265:** **−5.69 ± 0.43 ms** on VI-Rig april_01. Older runs on other april recordings read −5.88 to
  −8.07 ms. The [T265 sensor page](/sensors/intel-t265/) reads −6.59 ± 0.43 ms from one recording, in a different
  run set.
- **Without the solver:** an estimator-free correlation check on twelve older Looper recordings (10 hand-held,
  2 arm) read −5.8 to −7.1 ms, against −5.0 to −5.5 ms from an older solver run; the check reads about 1.2 ms more
  negative than the solver (bias −1.24 ± 0.42 ms). On ten T265 recordings it read −6.05 ± 0.14 ms at 2–25 Hz.
- **On the T265 the offset shrinks with frequency** (Figure 2). One `t_ga` is a compromise for the motion.

## The sweep: delay the accelerometer, watch `td`

- **Start from a fair zero:** the MVIS-corrected bag, with a common crop. On it all three tools read `td` near zero.
- **Delay only the accel** by τ = −50, −20, −10, −5, −2, 0, 2, 5, 10, 20 and 50 ms: a′(t) = a(t − τ). That is 11 bags
  per device. The 0 ms bag is the corrected bag with the same crop. In MVIS's sign, the true `t_ga` is τ.
- **Run all three tools** on every bag with their usual settings.
- **Read** `td` and `t_ga`.

| Result | Looper Insight 9 | Intel T265 |
|---|---|---|
| MVIS `t_ga` slope | **1.000** (largest residual 0.001 ms) | **0.998** (σ 0.42 ms per bag) |
| MVIS `td` change over the sweep | under 0.01 ms | under 0.06 ms |
| Kalibr `td` slope (τ within ±10 ms only) | **0.639** (0.623) | **0.302** (0.316) |
| Basalt `td` slope (τ within ±10 ms only) | **0.590** (0.576) | **0.282** (0.292) |
| Kalibr `td` at −50 / +50 ms | −32.1 / +32.1 | −14.4 / +15.5 |
| Basalt `td` at −50 / +50 ms | −30.4 / +28.9 | −13.8 / +14.2 |

All runs of all three tools converged (run note; Basalt reports `ok` on all 22 of its runs).

**Fair to Kalibr and Basalt:** with one IMU clock, the closest fit puts `td` between the gyro and the accel
timelines. Neither tool is broken; neither has a parameter for this. Why the T265 share is about half of Looper's
is open.

## Would you notice it?

- **No warning:** Kalibr and Basalt converge at every τ.
- **Reprojection barely moves:** near the real offset (τ 0 to −5 ms) it does not change. Even at ±50 ms it rises only
  10–16 %, which is easy to miss.
- **Kalibr gyro error grows, but slowly:** at τ = −5 ms it is × 2.4 on Looper and × 1.2 on the T265, against τ = 0.
  At −50 ms it is × 21 and × 5.3.
- **A self round trip will not catch it:** Kalibr's own round trip on Looper returns +0.0006 ms.
- **What does catch it:** a run with `t_ga` on, or an estimator-free correlation check.

## Limits

- **Which `td` is true is not tested:** the page explains the gap, not which value is right. A clock check through a
  third IMU, camera-`td` injection and a control on an IMU with `t_ga` near 0 are still open.
- **The sweep uses our model:** a pure accel shift with linear interpolation, which is exactly what MVIS fits. A slope
  of 1.000 / 0.998 is consistency, not accuracy. A real offset that is not a pure shift fits less well.
- **Round trips cannot order tools:** self residuals on the T265 are MVIS +0.19 / −0.06, Kalibr −0.019 / −0.019 and
  Basalt −0.00002 ms.
- **The T265 offset depends on frequency:** −6.76 ms at 2–4 Hz to −1.17 ms at 30–40 Hz. Coherence falls above about
  22 Hz, so the top two bands are less certain. So −5.69 ms is a compromise for this motion; other recordings read
  −5.88 to −8.07 ms.
- **First-order model:** MVIS corrects `t_ga` to first order; the ½·t_ga²·ä term is ignored, which matters at large
  offsets. A re-linearised version is being tested and is not in MVIS yet.
- **Open questions:**
  - Why the T265 share (0.30) is about half Looper's (0.64). Unchecked candidates: the 62.5 Hz accel interpolated onto
    the gyro stamps, different motion, part of the offset going into scale.
  - MVIS on the Kalibr-corrected Looper bag reads `t_ga` −2.65 ms (expected −5.03), RMS 0.47 px and gyro x scale
    1.0069. It is unexplained, and it does not repeat on the T265 (−5.44 ms).
  - On the T265 the sweep predicts the raw Kalibr gap 0.2–0.25 ms short.
- **One recording per device:** april_01 and looper_1. The other recordings have not been run.
- **Basalt has one offset for the rig:** on the T265 its gap is −2.02 ms against cam0 and +0.68 ms against cam1.
- **Settings differ:** Kalibr uses 10 Hz frames, Basalt every second frame with its own camera calibration, MVIS
  30 Hz. Intrinsics are pinned on the T265 and free on Looper.
- **The `t_ga`-off row is from older code** and has not been re-run.
- **The MVIS authors ran every tool.**

<details class="rp-fold">
<summary>Replay it yourself</summary>

1. Download one sweep folder from the Hub (you need to accept the gate first).
2. Run each tool on each bag with your usual settings: `mvis cam-imu -c cam_imu.yaml` with `estimate_t_ga: true`,
   Kalibr's `kalibr_calibrate_imu_camera`, and `basalt_calibrate_imu`.
3. Read `td` (and `t_ga` for MVIS) and fit each tool's `td` against τ. The true values in every bag are
   `t_ga` = τ, `td` = 0, identity IMU scale and zero bias.

To build a sweep from your own recording: correct it with `mvis apply-calib`, crop it, then delay only the accel
channel by τ with linear interpolation, as each folder's README describes.

</details>

This page uses april_01 only. The T265 sensor page's −6.59 ± 0.43 ms is from another run set.
