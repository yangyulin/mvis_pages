Camera model

Use this page to pick the camera model for each camera in your rig, and to see the exact equations MVIS uses.

A camera model Π\PiΠ maps a 3D point P=[X Y Z]⊤\mathbf{P} = [X\ Y\ Z]^\topP=[X Y Z]⊤ in the camera frame {Ci}\{C_i\}{Ci​} to a pixel [u v]⊤[u\ v]^\top[u v]⊤: a projection plus a lens distortion. Symbols follow Notation & glossary.

Each model lives in one class under src/mvis/camera/. The class holds the projection, its Jacobians (the derivatives the optimiser uses) and the unprojection. So every model works:

  • in camera-only and camera–IMU calibration;
  • with a calibration board of known corner positions, and in feature mode, where the corners’ 3D positions are not given and are solved as well (example: VI-Rig 4 IMUs + 3 cams, ArUco);
  • mixed across cameras in one rig.

Projection equations

Section titled “Projection equations”

Each model ends with the same affine step from a normalised point [mx my]⊤[m_x\ m_y]^\top[mx​ my​]⊤ to pixels:

u=fu mx+cu,v=fv my+cvu = f_u\, m_x + c_u, \qquad v = f_v\, m_y + c_vu=fu​mx​+cu​,v=fv​my​+cv​

What differs is how [mx my][m_x\ m_y][mx​ my​] comes from P\mathbf{P}P. A point outside a model’s valid domain has no pixel. That corner is left out of the error statistics and, in camera–IMU, out of the cost. Left uncounted, it would make the error look smaller than it is. So the result counts dropped corners:

  • past_fold: a Kannala–Brandt corner past the fold;
  • other_invalid: anything else, such as Z≤0Z \le 0Z≤0 under equidistant.

kb4 and equidistant

Section titled “kb4 and equidistant”

Choosing one

Section titled “Choosing one”

More parameters are not better. A model richer than the lens needs fits noise. On wide field-of-view data it can slide into a basin (a wrong local minimum) that looks converged and is not.

Choose by the largest incidence angle of the board corners, not by the lens’s advertised field of view. Every result reports this angle (max_theta_deg, and max_theta_used_deg for the corners the fit kept), and counts any corners past the model’s valid domain.

  • Normal lens: radtan.
  • Fisheye: kb4, which is what fisheye and kannala_brandt select. It stays valid past 90° up to its first fold (why).
  • Every corner under 90°, and you want equidistant by name: equidistant. It gives the same pixels as kb4 there.
  • Very wide field of view, a different shape: eucm, ucm or double_sphere.
  • Catadioptric (lens plus mirror): omni_radtan. It is experimental.

Wide-FoV models can diverge on stereo

In our TUM-VI runs, stereo double_sphere and eucm fits can diverge into a basin where the focal length and ξ\xiξ or α\alphaα trade off, while the single-camera fit is stable. The working recipe has two steps:

  1. Fit each camera alone.
  2. Pin those intrinsics for the joint solve (examples/configs/tumvi_cam_imu/).

You recognise a diverged fit by an implausible focal length or very large distortion coefficients, not by a large residual.

Camera-only calibration runs two passes: a first solve, then a second one after outlier rejection. A guardrail keeps the first-pass result if the second pass lands on any of these (src/mvis/calibration/pipeline_cam.py):

  • a focal length of 1 px or less;
  • fuf_ufu​ and fvf_vfv​ more than 30 % apart;
  • a distortion coefficient above 2 in magnitude (for ucm: α\alphaα outside [0,1)[0, 1)[0,1); for omni_radtan: ξ\xiξ outside [0,5][0, 5][0,5]);
  • a 95th-percentile reprojection error more than 1.5 times the first pass’s, on a camera whose fuf_ufu​ moved less than 5 %;
  • a mean RMS reprojection error over all cameras more than 1.5 times the first pass’s.

It is a backstop, not a substitute for picking the right model.

Rolling shutter

Section titled “Rolling shutter”

A rolling-shutter camera exposes its rows one after another, so one image spans a range of times. The readout trt_rtr​ is the time from the first row to the last.

A corner observed in row vvv of an image of height HHH was captured at

tI=tC+td+η(v) trt_I = t_C + t_d + \eta(v)\, t_rtI​=tC​+td​+η(v)tr​

The row factor η(v)\eta(v)η(v) depends on which row the driver stamps (rolling_mode, default 0):

rolling_modeFrame stamp atη(v)\eta(v)η(v)
0top rowv/Hv / Hv/H
1middle rowv/H−0.5v / H - 0.5v/H−0.5
2bottom row(v−H)/H(v - H) / H(v−H)/H

Source: src/mvis/core/shutter.py. For a global-shutter camera tr=0t_r = 0tr​=0, so every corner of a frame shares one time.

The camera–IMU vision factor uses that per-corner time directly. It takes the pose of {I}\{I\}{I} at tIt_ItI​ from the base IMU, composes the extrinsics, and then projects:

zi,k=Π(Cipk(tI))+n\mathbf{z}_{i,k} = \Pi\big({}^{C_i}\mathbf{p}_{k}(t_I)\big) + \mathbf{n}zi,k​=Π(Ci​pk​(tI​))+n

where Cipk(tI){}^{C_i}\mathbf{p}_k(t_I)Ci​pk​(tI​) is board corner kkk in the camera frame at the corner’s capture time. Projection, time offset, readout and extrinsics sit in one factor, so tdt_dtd​ and trt_rtr​ get Jacobians and are solved with everything else, not corrected afterwards. The HTML report uses the same projection code, so the residuals it shows are the ones the optimiser minimised.
