Base IMU factor

Use this page to see how MVIS turns the base IMU’s raw readings into a factor between two trajectory states, with the IMU’s own errors solved inside it.

  • Base IMU: the first IMU in the config. Its clock is the reference clock and its pose is the trajectory.
  • State: the IMU pose, velocity and biases at one time tkt_ktk​. States sit at solve.state_hz (30 Hz by default) on the first camera’s frames.
  • Preintegration: the IMU readings between two states, combined into one relative rotation, velocity change and position change.

Symbols follow Notation & glossary. The IMU conventions (calyx, kalibr, rpng) are on IMU model.

Corrected readings

Section titled “Corrected readings”

The gyroscope gives ωm\boldsymbol{\omega}_mωm​ and the accelerometer am\mathbf{a}_mam​. MVIS corrects each reading before it integrates it:

Ia=aIR  Da (am−ba){}^{I}\mathbf{a} = {}^{I}_{a}\mathbf{R}\;\mathbf{D}_a\,\big(\mathbf{a}_m - \mathbf{b}_a\big)Ia=aI​RDa​(am​−ba​) Iω=wIR  Dw (ωm−bg−Tg Ia){}^{I}\boldsymbol{\omega} = {}^{I}_{w}\mathbf{R}\;\mathbf{D}_w\,\big(\boldsymbol{\omega}_m - \mathbf{b}_g - \mathbf{T}_g\,{}^{I}\mathbf{a}\big)Iω=wI​RDw​(ωm​−bg​−Tg​Ia)
  • Dw\mathbf{D}_wDw​, Da\mathbf{D}_aDa​: correction matrices. Their diagonal fixes the scale of each axis; the rest fixes misalignment (axes not quite at right angles). They multiply the raw reading, so a diagonal of 0.998 means the raw axis read 0.2 % high.
  • Tg\mathbf{T}_gTg​: g-sensitivity, the part of the gyro reading caused by linear acceleration.
  • bg\mathbf{b}_gbg​, ba\mathbf{b}_aba​: the biases, solved at every state.
  • wIR{}^{I}_{w}\mathbf{R}wI​R, aIR{}^{I}_{a}\mathbf{R}aI​R: the gyro and accelerometer triad rotations into the IMU frame. Each convention frees at most one of them:
ConventionDw\mathbf{D}_wDw​Da\mathbf{D}_aDa​Free rotationIntrinsic parameters
calyx (default)full 3×3 (9)upper-triangular (6)none; folded into Dw\mathbf{D}_wDw​9 + 6 + 9 (Tg\mathbf{T}_gTg​) = 24
kalibrlower-triangular (6)lower-triangular (6)wIR{}^{I}_{w}\mathbf{R}wI​R (3)6 + 6 + 9 + 3 = 24
rpngupper-triangular (6)upper-triangular (6)aIR{}^{I}_{a}\mathbf{R}aI​R (3)6 + 6 + 9 + 3 = 24

All three give the solver one 24-value intrinsics variable xin\mathbf{x}_{in}xin​. It starts at identity (no correction).

An IMU slower than 200 Hz is first upsampled (cubic, the real samples kept) before integration (solve.imu_min_rate_hz).

Preintegration

Section titled “Preintegration”

Between two states at tkt_ktk​ and tk+1t_{k+1}tk+1​ (Δt=tk+1−tk\Delta t = t_{k+1} - t_kΔt=tk+1​−tk​), the corrected readings give the motion in the frame of the first state:

ΔRk=Ik+1IkR,Δvk=∫tktk+1IτIkR  Iaτ dτ,Δpk=∫tktk+1 ⁣ ⁣∫tksIτIkR  Iaτ dτ ds\Delta\mathbf{R}_{k} = {}^{I_k}_{I_{k+1}}\mathbf{R}, \qquad \Delta\mathbf{v}_{k} = \int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}} {}^{I_k}_{I_\tau}\mathbf{R}\;{}^{I}\mathbf{a}_\tau\,d\tau, \qquad \Delta\mathbf{p}_{k} = \int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}}\!\!\int_{t_k}^{s} {}^{I_k}_{I_\tau}\mathbf{R}\;{}^{I}\mathbf{a}_\tau\,d\tau\,dsΔRk​=Ik+1​Ik​​R,Δvk​=∫tk​tk+1​​Iτ​Ik​​RIaτ​dτ,Δpk​=∫tk​tk+1​​∫tk​s​Iτ​Ik​​RIaτ​dτds

MVIS evaluates these with ACI (analytic combined IMU integration). Between two samples it takes the midpoint of the two corrected readings as constant and integrates the rotation and the rotated acceleration in closed form. The same pass gives:

  • the Jacobians of ΔR\Delta\mathbf{R}ΔR, Δv\Delta\mathbf{v}Δv, Δp\Delta\mathbf{p}Δp with respect to the biases, the 24 intrinsics and t_ga;
  • the 15×15 covariance Pk\mathbf{P}_kPk​, from the noise densities (solve.sigma_g, sigma_a) and the bias random-walk densities (sigma_bg, sigma_ba).

The integration runs once, at the biases and intrinsics of the start point. Small changes after that are applied through the Jacobians. A detection gap is bridged by one factor over the whole gap.

The gyro-to-accel offset t_ga

Section titled “The gyro-to-accel offset t_ga”

Many drivers publish gyro and accelerometer on one stamp, though the two were sampled at different instants. MVIS takes the stamp as gyro time. The accelerometer value that belongs to gyro time ttt is the one stamped t+tgat + t_{ga}t+tga​. Over one integration step from sample 0 to sample 1, to first order:

aˉm=12(am,0+am,1)+tga a˙m,a˙m=am,1−am,0δt\bar{\mathbf{a}}_m = \tfrac12\big(\mathbf{a}_{m,0} + \mathbf{a}_{m,1}\big) + t_{ga}\,\dot{\mathbf{a}}_m, \qquad \dot{\mathbf{a}}_m = \frac{\mathbf{a}_{m,1} - \mathbf{a}_{m,0}}{\delta t}aˉm​=21​(am,0​+am,1​)+tga​a˙m​,a˙m​=δtam,1​−am,0​​
  • One more Jacobian column: ∂aˉm/∂tga=a˙m\partial\bar{\mathbf{a}}_m/\partial t_{ga} = \dot{\mathbf{a}}_m∂aˉm​/∂tga​=a˙m​, carried through Da\mathbf{D}_aDa​ and, through Tg\mathbf{T}_gTg​, into the gyro.
  • Re-integration: after the first two iterations, when t_ga has moved more than 3 ms from the value the factors were integrated at, MVIS integrates that IMU again with its accelerometer resampled at t+tgat + t_{ga}t+tga​ (solve.tga_relin_threshold_s, default 0.003 s). The linear term then covers only what is left.
  • Prior: a weak zero-mean prior, 10 ms (solve.sigma_t_ga).

estimate_t_ga is on by default in camera–IMU runs. What the offset is and when it matters: Why calibrate t_ga.

The residual

Section titled “The residual”

The factor links state kkk (pose IkGT{}^{G}_{I_k}\mathbf{T}Ik​G​T, velocity GvIk{}^{G}\mathbf{v}_{I_k}GvIk​​, biases) to state k+1k+1k+1. It predicts the measured preintegration from the two states and compares.

Write δη\delta\boldsymbol{\eta}δη for the change since integration: δbg\delta\mathbf{b}_gδbg​, δba\delta\mathbf{b}_aδba​, δxin\delta\mathbf{x}_{in}δxin​ and δtga\delta t_{ga}δtga​. First-order corrections, built from the Jacobians above:

δθ=JR δη,δv=Jv δη,δp=Jp δη\delta\boldsymbol\theta = \mathbf{J}_{R}\,\delta\boldsymbol\eta, \qquad \delta\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{J}_{v}\,\delta\boldsymbol\eta, \qquad \delta\mathbf{p} = \mathbf{J}_{p}\,\delta\boldsymbol\etaδθ=JR​δη,δv=Jv​δη,δp=Jp​δη

The 15 residual rows are:

The cost is ∥r∥Pk2\|\mathbf{r}\|^2_{\mathbf{P}_k}∥r∥Pk​2​ with r=[rθ; rp; rv; rb]\mathbf{r} = [\mathbf{r}_\theta;\ \mathbf{r}_p;\ \mathbf{r}_v;\ \mathbf{r}_b]r=[rθ​; rp​; rv​; rb​].

  • Bias random walk: rb\mathbf{r}_brb​ is the random-walk term. Biases may drift between states, by as much as the random-walk densities allow. There is no separate bias factor.
  • No robust loss: the IMU residual is plain least squares. Only the reprojection factor uses Huber.
  • The covariance follows the correction: Pk\mathbf{P}_kPk​ is rotated by the current δθ\delta\boldsymbol\thetaδθ before it weights the residual.

Which intrinsics are solved

Section titled “Which intrinsics are solved”

estimate.imu.intrinsics frees blocks of xin\mathbf{x}_{in}xin​. A pinned block gets a very tight prior at its start value; a freed block gets a weak one, so a direction the motion does not excite stays near its start instead of drifting.

SettingFreeWeak prior on the free terms
pinnednothing—
dw_da (default)Dw\mathbf{D}_wDw​, Da\mathbf{D}_aDa​0.1 (solve.sigma_dw_da)
dw_da_ras dw_da, plus the free rotation (kalibr, rpng; same as dw_da under calyx, which has no separate rotation)0.1 (solve.sigma_imu_R)
fullas dw_da_r, plus Tg\mathbf{T}_gTg​0.05 (solve.sigma_tg)

The same model inside the reprojection factor

Section titled “The same model inside the reprojection factor”

The reprojection factor integrates the same corrected readings from an anchor state to each corner’s capture time (Camera–IMU calibration math). So Dw\mathbf{D}_wDw​, Da\mathbf{D}_aDa​, the biases and t_ga are fitted by both factors, with one consistent model.

What this means for your recording

Section titled “What this means for your recording”
  • Rotate about all three axes. Each column of Dw\mathbf{D}_wDw​ needs rotation about its axis.
  • Point every axis at gravity at some time. Gravity is the largest specific force you have. Tilting the rig through many orientations separates Da\mathbf{D}_aDa​ from ba\mathbf{b}_aba​.
  • Change the acceleration briskly. t_ga appears only through a˙m\dot{\mathbf{a}}_ma˙m​; smooth, steady motion leaves it loose.
  • Leave Tg\mathbf{T}_gTg​ pinned unless the motion has strong linear acceleration while it rotates. Hand-held motion rarely does.
