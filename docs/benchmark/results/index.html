◆ counts are the rows where two tools' medians differ by more than the larger of their spreads, counted by the same code as the parameter tables. They mark disagreement, not who is right. On camera + IMU families the MVIS of these pairs is its run with Kalibr's IMU model (K), so the IMU terms mean the same thing in both tools.

How to read a cell
  • Each repeatability cell is one family and one group of parameters, compared on the recordings every compared tool ran. Its shape names the tool whose estimates scatter least; two shapes are a tie between them, both ahead of the third.
  • ≈ is a tie: the gap is within chance or under 20 %. The hatched box means too few shared recordings to decide (fewer than 6). ○ means a tool received the values as input, so there is nothing to compare; – means the tools’ models differ there.
  • Point at a cell for its sentence; click it for its numbers on the dataset eval page. Nothing is counted per tool across cells.
Run dates and data settings
  • 2026-11-08: MVIS and Kalibr on T6 and T11, and Basalt on T11, were re-run with IMU noise measured at rest (white-noise density × 2.5, the same values for every tool; bias walks at the defaults).
  • 2026-11-08: MVIS on T2 and T12 was re-run with the current MVIS code.
  • 2026-11-07: Kalibr camera + IMU on T6 and T11 was re-run from its own camera calibration.
  • 2026-11-07: on T3, every tool ran on the same view-filtered bags (≤ 85°).
  • 2026-11-04: Kalibr on T1 bag 09 was re-run.
  • 2026-11-02: Kalibr on T7 was re-run.
  • 2026-10-22 or earlier: all other Kalibr and Basalt runs.
  • T6c: the MVIS values come from camera-only runs.

About these results

Section titled “About these results”
How a cell is decided

For each family and group of parameters, we decide a verdict in five steps:

  1. We take only the recordings every compared tool ran.
  2. For each tool, we measure the scatter (standard deviation) of each parameter across those recordings.
  3. We compare two tools by the median ratio of their scatters.
  4. A tool is marked more repeatable only if both of these hold:
    • its scatter is at least 20 % smaller, and
    • a gap that large would rarely appear (under 5 %, Holm-adjusted within the cell) if we swapped the two tools’ results recording by recording.
  5. Otherwise it is a tie: either the gap is within chance, or it is under 20 %.

Notes on the test:

  • Too few recordings: with fewer than 6 shared recordings, no gap can pass this test (the smallest possible p is 2/2ⁿ). Those cells say “too few recordings”.
  • Not compared: values a tool received as input, and rows where the tools’ models differ.
  • Distortion coefficients trade off against each other, so their scatter can overstate differences in the lens mapping.
  • Many tests: each cell is a separate 5 % test. Across the 27 tested cells of this batch, of which 14 name a tool, one or two calls may be chance. The intervals are not adjusted for multiple tests.
◆ differences between tools

The ◆ columns count, per tool pair, the parameter rows where the tools’ medians differ. No tool is the reference. Each dataset eval page lists every row.

  • Disagreement: two tools disagree where their medians differ by more than either tool’s scatter.
  • ◆ marks disagreement, not an error in either tool.
  • ‡ on a time offset: MVIS splits the gyro (td) and accel (td + t_ga) timelines, so Basalt − Kalibr is the like-for-like check there. Here td is the camera–IMU time offset, and t_ga is the time offset between the gyroscope and accelerometer channels.
