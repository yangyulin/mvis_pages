VI-Rig: 4 IMUs + 3 cameras

3 global-shutter cameras4 IMUs, 100–500 HzAprilGrid 6×6, 88 mmROS1 bagCC BY-NC 4.0

What you'll see: One MVIS camera + IMU run on recording april_01: every camera and all four IMUs solved together.

All 10 recordings and three tools: Results → Downloads: VI-Rig 4 IMUs + 3 cams dataset page →

1. Data

Section titled “1. Data”
  • Recording april_01, one of the 10 recordings of this rig. MVIS picks the 80 s window with the most motion.
  • Cameras: a Blackfly S (pinhole with radial-tangential distortion) and the two T265 fisheye cameras (equidistant lens model).
  • IMUs: the Xsens MTi is the base IMU; the two MicroStrain units and the T265's own IMU are solved against it.
  • Board: AprilGrid 6×6, tag 88 mm, spacing 0.3, t36h11.
  • Solve window: 80.0 s.

Download: VI-Rig 4 IMUs + 3 cams dataset page → The recordings are listed there, not copied here.

2. Config

Section titled “2. Config”

The marked lines are the choices that matter for this rig:

  • input.imu_topics: Four IMU topics. The first is the base IMU; the other three are solved against it.
  • input.window_mode: MVIS picks the 80 s window with the most motion.
  • camera_input.cameras: The two T265 cameras are added to the graph at 4 Hz, which keeps the solve small.
  • solve.sigma_g: IMU noise from a still recording of this rig, measured density × 2.5. The other three sigmas are set the same way.
  • estimate.board_points: The printed board is not quite flat, so its shape is estimated too.
  • estimate.camera.intrinsics: Camera intrinsics stay free in the camera + IMU solve.

This is the config of the run on this page, with every path cut to its file name. Point the paths at your copy.

3. Run

Section titled “3. Run”

The solve took about 13 min on one desktop. It writes cam_imu.json, a calibration YAML and a report beside the config.

4. Watch MVIS calibrate

Section titled “4. Watch MVIS calibrate”

MVIS's own player, from this run: the detected board corners, then the rig's path as the solve refines it. Press Play or drag the bar; pick a camera card to look through that camera. We publish no camera images.
