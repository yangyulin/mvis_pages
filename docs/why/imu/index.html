Why calibrate the IMU

Use this page to see what an IMU calibration buys a tracker downstream. Data: 24 headset recordings from the Monado SLAM Datasets; the VI-Rig numbers are in the last section.

The answer first:

  • Mono gains most: correcting the IMU and timing with MVIS cuts the median OpenVINS mono SE3 ATE from 28.80 to 9.56 mm, by two thirds.
  • Stereo gains less: the median stereo SE3 ATE drops from 8.94 to 7.04 mm, and the 1 s drift improves on 24/24 recordings.
  • Online calibration does not replace it: on the as-published recordings, only stereo with online tdt_dtd​ ties the corrected recording; every other setting stays behind.
  • Online calibration on top adds nothing: on the corrected recordings, keep it off.
  • Same kind of error on VI-Rig: the T265 accel scale is off by 1.7–2.0 % on every axis.

How to turn it on: estimate the IMU intrinsics in Camera–IMU calibration, then write a corrected recording with mvis apply-calib.

The setup

Section titled “The setup”

We ran the OpenVINS visual-inertial tracker on each recording twice: once on the recording as published and once on the recording corrected by MVIS.

Recordings: 24 imucalib recordings from the Monado SLAM Datasets:

HeadsetRecordings
Valve Index8
HP Reverb G212
Samsung Odyssey+4

Two arms: the two runs of a recording differ only in the input recording.

  • As published: the recording as the dataset ships it.
  • Corrected: the same recording after mvis apply-calib with the MVIS calibration. It applies:
    • the IMU intrinsics, the gyro and accel scale and misalignment Dw\mathbf{D}_wDw​ and Da\mathbf{D}_aDa​;
    • removal of the static bias;
    • the camera–IMU time offset tdt_dtd​ of each camera, by shifting the image stamps;
    • the gyro–accel time offset tgat_{ga}tga​, by re-interpolating the accelerometer.
  • Images: unchanged.

Shared by both arms:

  • the same MVIS camera intrinsics (kb4) and camera–IMU extrinsics;
  • identical OpenVINS configs, byte for byte.

So the test measures one thing: applying the MVIS IMU and time correction, given the MVIS geometry.

Ground truth: the lighthouse poses that come with the dataset.

Metrics:

  • SE3 ATE (mm): position error after a rigid alignment to the ground truth. This is the primary metric.
  • RPE 1 s (mm): position drift over 1 s windows.
  • Scale error (%): ∣s−1∣\lvert s - 1 \rvert∣s−1∣, where sss is the Sim3 scale. It shows how far the trajectory size is off after a similarity alignment.
  • Medians and wins: each number below is a median over recordings. “Better” counts the recordings where the corrected recording wins.
  • p: Wilcoxon signed-rank p, paired by recording, not corrected for multiple tests.

2. Online calibration on the published recordings does not catch up

3. Online calibration on corrected recordings adds nothing

Section titled “3. Online calibration on corrected recordings adds nothing”

Turning online calibration on for the corrected recording does not help:

  • Stereo + tdt_dtd​: 7.04 → 7.33 mm. Online is better in only 7/24.
  • No setting lowers SE3 ATE significantly. So use the corrected recording with online calibration off.

The online values still check MVIS:

  • Independent start: the online values start at td=0t_d = 0td​=0, so the agreement does not lean on MVIS.

4. On VI-Rig, the T265 accel scale is off on every axis

Section titled “4. On VI-Rig, the T265 accel scale is off on every axis”

The VI-Rig dataset virig-4imus-4cams shows why this matters on a real rig. We take the 15-recording results of family T8:

  • Rig: ELP stereo, T265 IMU as base, Xsens as second IMU.
  • Model: MVIS with Kalibr’s IMU model (kalibr).

Accel scales, the diagonal of Da\mathbf{D}_aDa​:

IMUDa\mathbf{D}_aDa​ xDa\mathbf{D}_aDa​ yDa\mathbf{D}_aDa​ z
T265 IMU1.019891.019051.01668

  • T265 IMU: its accel scale is off by 1.7–2.0 % on every axis.
  • Xsens: its scale stays within 0.1 % of 1. It is a check on the T265 result.
  • Same kind of fix: the corrected Monado recordings apply the MVIS IMU intrinsics Da\mathbf{D}_aDa​ and Dw\mathbf{D}_wDw​ in the same way.

To do it on your rig:

  • Estimate the IMU intrinsics in step 4, Camera–IMU calibration.
  • Write a corrected recording with mvis apply-calib in step 8, Round-trip validation.

Conclusion:

  • Correct the IMU before the tracker: the MVIS IMU and time correction cuts mono SE3 ATE by two thirds and helps stereo too.
  • Online calibration is no substitute: it does not catch up on the as-published recordings, and adds nothing on the corrected ones.
  • Keep it off: run the corrected recording with online calibration off.

Limits

Section titled “Limits”
  • In-sample: MVIS was solved on the same recordings. A check on held-out recordings is still open.
  • Shared geometry: Both arms use the MVIS camera intrinsics and extrinsics. This is not MVIS against the best calibration for the as-published data.
  • ”As published” is not raw: The dataset already carries its own Basalt IMU correction.
  • Valve corrected twice: On Valve that correction was applied twice, which leaves an accel scale error of about 6 %. Part of the Valve mono gain likely undoes that.
  • One combined gain: The correction fixes IMU intrinsics, bias, td and t_ga together. Their effects were not separated.
  • Too-large trajectories: Reverb G2 and Odyssey+ trajectories come out about 1–2 % too large in both arms. The cause is not known.
  • One recording gets worse: Odyssey+ MOC11 stereo goes from 11.4 to 16.6 mm with the correction, though its RPE and mono improve.
  • Small group: Odyssey+ has only 4 pairs.
  • Priors not tuned: The OpenVINS online priors are fixed and were not tuned.
