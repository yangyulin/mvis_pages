Why calibrate IMUs together

This page shows why a rig with several IMUs needs them calibrated against each other. You need nothing to run here. Most numbers come from our published runs on VI-Rig; sections 3 and 5 add unpublished studies, marked where used.

The answer first:

  • No shared clock: each IMU stamps its data on its own clock. On the running example, the T265 IMU is 7.2 ms off the Xsens.
  • No shared origin: each IMU sits at its own place on the rig, with its own rotation.
  • What MVIS solves: the base IMU is the one the trajectory and the reference clock hang on; every other IMU is an aux IMU. For each aux IMU, MVIS solves its lever (position), rotation, tdt_dtd​ and tgat_{ga}tga​ against the base IMU.

How to turn it on: Turn on the aux spatial and temporal terms.

3. The aux IMU lever does not depend on the cameras

Section titled “3. The aux IMU lever does not depend on the cameras”
  • Test: T7 and T8 solve the same two IMUs (T265 IMU base, Xsens aux) on the same 15 recordings. Only the cameras differ: T7 uses T265 fisheye 1 + ELP-L, T8 the ELP pair.
  • Results medians: the Xsens distance to base is 124.09 mm in T7 and 123.94 mm in T8.
  • Per-recording gap: in an unpublished study, the gap between the two runs’ Xsens levers was 0.11 mm.
  • What it means: the aux IMU lever comes from the IMUs, not from which cameras are in the solve.

4. Several IMUs constrain each other

Section titled “4. Several IMUs constrain each other”

Several IMUs see the same motion. That redundancy constrains what one IMU leaves weak:

  • Time offsets: every IMU must agree on when each motion happened.
  • IMU intrinsics: scale, misalignment and similar sensor errors, Dw\mathbf{D}_wDw​ and Da\mathbf{D}_aDa​.
  • A meaningful check: a wrong calibration usually cannot satisfy every IMU at once. This makes the round-trip check useful on these rigs.

5. Slow IMUs bias levers on short windows

Section titled “5. Slow IMUs bias levers on short windows”
  • Twin: a noise-free simulated copy of the 4 IMUs + 3 cams rig with known truth and true tgat_{ga}tga​ = 0. Unpublished.
  • Measured: lever error at aux factor intervals 1 / 2 / 3.
IMU rateInterval 1Interval 2Interval 3
100 Hz2.94 mm1.47 mm0.63 mm
200 Hz0.56 mm0.30 mm0.17 mm
500 Hz0.11 mm0.05 mm0.05 mm
  • Why: at 100 Hz an aux factor at interval 1 holds only about 3 samples.
  • Default: MVIS upsamples an IMU slower than 200 Hz before it integrates it (solve.imu_min_rate_hz: 200, solve.imu_upsample_method: cubic).
  • Upsampling helps: in the same twin, cubic upsampling of the 100 Hz IMU gave 0.80 / 0.52 / 0.12 mm.
  • The knob: solve.aux_imu_stride sets one aux IMU factor per this many base intervals. The default is 3.
  • Levers move with it: keep the default unless you have a reason, and compare runs only at the same value.

Conclusion:

  • Calibrate every IMU: each aux IMU has its own clock offset and pose, and MVIS solves both in one graph.
  • The levers repeat: MVIS levers repeat more tightly than Kalibr’s and do not depend on the cameras.
  • Mind slow IMUs: keep the upsampling and stride defaults, and compare runs only at the same stride.

Limits

Section titled “Limits”
  • Slow IMUs: on short windows they bias the lever; the twin numbers above are unpublished.
  • More IMUs do not help the readout: the rolling-shutter readout comes from the cameras. In an older study, adding IMUs moved the ELP readout from 15.9 to 16.6 ms and made the fit worse, reprojection error 3.04 → 3.49. See Why calibrate rolling shutter.
  • Pick the base with care: the base IMU carries the trajectory and the reference clock. Its errors reach every other sensor.
  • Repeatable is not right: spread over recordings shows precision. There is no reference lever for these rigs.
