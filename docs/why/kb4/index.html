Why kb4 over equidistant

Use this page to see why kb4 is the MVIS fisheye model and equidistant is not. You need nothing to run here; the numbers come from our runs.

The answer first:

  • One polynomial: both models share it. Below 90° off axis the formulas are identical; fitted separately on the T265, they agree to about 2e-6 px.
  • Past 90°, only kb4 can project: equidistant has no projection for rays at or past 90°; kb4 has one.
  • On the one past-90° lens we tested, equidistant stalled: on BF2M (up to 97° off axis) the equidistant solve stayed at its starting values, 15–24 px off TUM in fx, even on a recording where it dropped no corner. We have not found why. kb4, from the same start, came within 0.4 px of TUM in fx.
  • No cost below 90°: a camera–IMU result with kb4 exports to Kalibr as pinhole-equi (exact for rays in front of the camera; MVIS warns when the fit used corners past 90° or folds inside the image), and fisheye / kannala_brandt mean kb4. On a lens past 90°, the fitted polynomial can fold inside the image (BF2M: no ray past about 712–743 px from the centre).

How to set it: Choose a fisheye model (T265 example).

The two models

Section titled “The two models”

Both models map the angle θ of a ray off the optical axis to a distance r from the principal point, with the same four coefficients:

r = f · θ (1 + k₁θ² + k₂θ⁴ + k₃θ⁶ + k₄θ⁸)

They differ only in how they get θ from a 3D point (x, y, z):

Modelθ fromRays at or past 90° (z ≤ 0)
equidistant (Kalibr pinhole-equi)atan(√(x² + y²) / z), after dividing by zCannot be projected
kb4 (Kannala–Brandt, as in Basalt)atan2(√(x² + y²), z)Projected, up to the polynomial’s first fold

1. Below 90°, they are the same

Section titled “1. Below 90°, they are the same”

Where few or no corners pass 90°, the two models give the same calibration:

2. On a lens past 90°, equidistant stalled

Section titled “2. On a lens past 90°, equidistant stalled”

The TUM-Fisheye BF2M2020S23 lens sees the board up to 94–97° off axis (by MVIS’s kb4 fit). Each recording has 13–19 thousand detected corners (11.8–18.3 thousand in the kb4 solve). TUM’s own calibrations of the same images are the reference; they are another tool’s results, not ground truth.

RecordingLargest angle (kb4)equidistant fx − TUMkb4 fx − TUMMean error, equidistant / kb4
194.0°+23.88 px+0.06 px1.35 / 0.16 px
297.0°+15.51 px+0.31 px0.95 / 0.20 px
396.1°+22.07 px+0.33 px1.48 / 0.20 px

All corners, camera-only, MVIS main f902d39 (2026-11-11). With two corners per tag, the MVIS default, the equidistant intrinsics are the same (mean error within 0.01 px) and kb4 is +0.12 to +0.39 px off TUM in fx.

  • What happens: both models start from the same guess: fx = fy = 404.98 / 396.51 / 402.95 px, the principal point at the image centre (639.5, 511.5) and zero distortion. kb4 moves from there. Equidistant does not: the solver gives up at the first step with the error unchanged, so every intrinsic ends at its starting value. The 15–24 px fx offsets are that starting guess.
  • Where it gives up: every trial step raises the cost (recording 1: 15,541 at the start, 85,047 down to 42,972 as the damping lambda rises from 1e-5 to 1e1), and the solver stops at MVIS’s damping cap (lambda 1e2; GTSAM’s default is 1e5). We have not tested whether a higher cap lets equidistant converge.
  • kb4’s other intrinsics: cx is 0.30–0.48 px low, cy within 0.1 px, and fy within 0.37 px of TUM.
  • The corners past 90° are not the whole story: equidistant’s stalled fit drops 0, 3 and 1 corners as past 90°. At its starting values, recording 1’s fit puts no corner past 90° (kb4’s fit puts its largest at 94°), yet it stalls the same way. Whether the corners near or past 90° block the first step is not tested. We have not found why the solve stalls.
  • Warnings: since PR #45 (commit a39f357, merged as 5d28769, 2026-11-05), MVIS warns when corners lie at or past 90° with equidistant and suggests kb4. That fires on recordings 2 and 3. Nothing in MVIS flags the stall: on recording 1 the only signs are the solver library’s “Levenberg-Marquardt giving up” line, an error that does not change (15541.4565 -> 15541.4565), and a mean error about 8× kb4’s.

Earlier runs, not repeated on f902d39:

  • Corners past 90°: by TUM’s calibration, 36, 37 and 45 corners lie past 90°, counted at MVIS 197549a on the frames of the kb4 all-corner run (the same corner totals as the kb4 solve above).
  • The old result was the same: at MVIS 197549a (2026-11-04) the three all-corner equidistant solves gave the same values to 4 decimals.
  • One corner per tag converged: at 197549a, with one corner per tag, equidistant did converge on the same bags (fx −0.00 / +0.91 / +2.72 px from TUM; kb4 −0.00 / +0.14 / +0.15), so the stall depends on the corner set. On the other four TUM-Fisheye lenses it converged with one and with all corners (section 1).
  • Kalibr did not do better: its pinhole-equi failed to converge on all 3 BF2M recordings, even seeded with TUM’s fx (it also failed on 1 of the 12 other recordings). Its DS and EUCM fits converged on all 3, after a patched initial guess on a second attempt.

3. Choosing kb4 costs nothing below 90°

Section titled “3. Choosing kb4 costs nothing below 90°”
  • Same pixels below 90° (section 1), so nothing changes on a lens that never gets there.
  • Kalibr export: a camera–IMU result with kb4 is written to Kalibr’s camchain-imucam as pinhole-equi, with the same k₁–k₄. It is exact for rays in front of the camera. MVIS warns when the fit used corners past 90°, or when the polynomial folds inside the image. Camera-only results have no Kalibr export.
  • The fold is checked: a KB polynomial can turn back (r stops growing with θ). MVIS reports the fold angle of every fit and flags corners past it. On TUM-VI (MVIS b03d456, all corners), 13 of 16 fits fold inside the image (12 of 16 with two corners per tag), but beyond every corner (smallest margin 22.2°): no corner lies past the fold, although MVIS warns that the fold is inside the image.
  • On BF2M the fold is inside the image too: every kb4 fit folds at 120–128°, 712–743 px from the principal point, while the image corner is 822 px away. No corner lies past the fold (the largest is at 97°), but pixels beyond that radius have no ray. MVIS warns about it in every run.

Conclusion: kb4 is the same model as equidistant where both work, and keeps working on the one past-90° lens we tested, where equidistant stalled. Since MVIS 29daf90, fisheye and kannala_brandt mean kb4. Use equidistant only when you want that name, on a lens whose corners stay under 90°.

Limits

Section titled “Limits”

Past-90° evidence is thin. It is one lens (BF2M, 3 recordings, 36–45 corners past 90° each) plus TUM-VI, where 96 corners past 90° are detected and about 40 reach the solve. Basalt detects 2,481 there; those TUM-VI counts are from a one-off survey with our own solver, not yet re-checked. The reference is TUM’s calibration, not ground truth.

Not tested: why equidistant stalls on BF2M (at its starting values, recording 1’s fit puts no corner past 90°); whether it converges from another start, such as TUM’s fx; and whether it converges with a higher damping cap (MVIS stops at lambda 1e2; GTSAM’s default is 1e5). The one-corner-per-tag runs, where equidistant converged, and the section 1 rows were not re-run on f902d39. Nor did we test how much the kb4 fold near the image corners matters downstream. The MVIS authors ran every solve.
