Camera–IMU calibration

Use this page to solve cameras and IMUs in one graph. You need the bag from step 2 and the camera YAML from step 3. You get cam_imu.json.

The running example is our VI-Rig with 4 IMUs and 4 cameras (dataset page):

  • Cameras: a T265 fisheye pair (global shutter) and an ELP stereo pair (rolling shutter).
  • IMUs: Xsens MTi-100, MicroStrain GX3-25 and GX3-35, and the T265 IMU.

The solve estimates, all at once:

  • Extrinsics: where each camera and IMU sits relative to the base IMU (lever arm and rotation).
  • Time offset td: the clock delay of each camera and each extra IMU to the base IMU.
  • IMU intrinsics: scale and misalignment of each gyroscope (Dw) and accelerometer (Da).
  • t_ga: the delay between an IMU’s own gyroscope and accelerometer.
  • Camera intrinsics: refined from the step 3 prior, not pinned.
  • Biases of every gyroscope and accelerometer.

1. Point at the camera prior

Section titled “1. Point at the camera prior”

Step 3 (Camera-only calibration) writes a camera YAML. Name it in the cam-IMU config:

  • What it gives: the starting intrinsics of each camera.
  • One file, in cam_topics order: join the T265 and ELP results of step 3. MVIS sorts the cameras by id and matches them to cam_topics by order. So here the T265 fisheyes are id 0 and 1, the ELP left and right 2 and 3.
  • What happens next: the cam-IMU solve refines the intrinsics (estimate.camera.intrinsics: true). It solves the extrinsics and time offsets itself.

2. Write the config

Section titled “2. Write the config”

This is the config of the VI-Rig 4 IMUs + 4 cameras example, bag 01. The highlighted lines matter for this step.

What the highlighted lines do:

  • input: a ROS1 bag. The first imu_topics entry (/imu/data, the Xsens) is the base IMU. Every other sensor is solved relative to it.
  • window_mode: motion: MVIS picks the segment_length seconds (here 80 s) with the most IMU motion.
  • board.heights_file + estimate.board_points: true: we solve the board shape too. Our board is not flat on this VI-Rig mount; a flat solve shifts gyro scale and the lever (why).
  • solve.corner_subsample: two_anti: uses two opposite corners of each tag (top-left and bottom-right). Our config sets it. The cam-IMU default is all (every corner).
  • solve.estimate_t_ga: true, sigma_t_ga: 0.01: solve t_ga for each IMU. Keep this on.
  • solve.vision_pose: imu: carries each corner’s pose to its capture time through the raw base-IMU samples. It is the default.
  • sigma_g, sigma_a, sigma_bg, sigma_ba: IMU noise and bias random walk.
  • estimate.camera: intrinsics free, td and extrinsics solved.
  • estimate.imu: IMU intrinsics dw_da with the calyx model (see section 3), plus extrinsics and td of the extra IMUs.

The other lines belong to later steps:

  • camera_input.cameras: the three cameras after the first go into the graph at 4 Hz (factor_hz), which keeps the solve small.
  • ELP readout lines (estimate_readout, tr): step 5, Rolling-shutter calibration.
  • The three extra IMUs: step 6, Multi-IMU spatial-temporal calibration.

For your own rig, change the topics, the board and the prior path. Keep the rest as a start.

3. Turn on IMU intrinsics

Section titled “3. Turn on IMU intrinsics”

estimate.imu.intrinsics sets how much of each IMU model we solve:

ValueWhat is solved
pinnednothing: Dw and Da stay at their start values
dw_dascale and misalignment of gyro (Dw) and accel (Da). Our setting, and the default
dw_da_ras dw_da, plus the gyro–accel rotation. Same as dw_da for calyx, which keeps that rotation inside Dw
fullas dw_da_r, plus Tg (how linear acceleration leaks into the gyro)
  • A bool also works: true means full, false means pinned.
  • estimate.imu.model picks the model family: calyx (default), kalibr or rpng.
  • Priors: freed terms get a weak prior. solve.sigma_dw_da defaults to 0.1, solve.sigma_tg to 0.05.

What the terms mean: IMU model. Why it pays off: Why calibrate the IMU.

4. Run

Section titled “4. Run”
  • Other bag: --bag <file> overrides the config’s source with that ROS1 bag.
  • Time: about 12 min on one desktop (solve 700.5 s, MVIS main, 2026-11-10).
  • Output: cam_imu.json, a calibration YAML and a report beside the config.

6. Leave state_hz alone

Section titled “6. Leave state_hz alone”

solve.state_hz sets the rate of the pose, velocity and bias states. It defaults to 30 Hz; our config does not set it. A study across MIC, TUM-VI raw and T265 found:

CaseEffect of state_hz
Dynamic-motion rigsReprojection error, lever arm and td all alias at low rates (fast motion is undersampled) and level off around 30 Hz
Gentle calibration sequencesFlat: the rate does not matter
Stereo baseline, IMU intrinsics and biasesRate-invariant either way
  • Leave it at 30 unless you are on a gentle sequence and want the speed.
  • Suspect it first if td or the lever arm look wrong on a dynamic rig.

Batch: a whole dataset family from one config

Section titled “Batch: a whole dataset family from one config”

To calibrate many recordings at once, list them under input.bags instead of a single path:

  • Output: each bag gets its own bag<NN>/cam_imu.json in the folder of output.path (results/sweep when it has none). <NN> is the bag’s trailing number (4imus_4cams_01.bag gives bag01).
  • Formats: ROS1 bags and EuRoC mav0 folders both work. Each inherits the config’s format.
  • Use: a repeatability sweep over many recordings.

The spread of extrinsics and intrinsics across recordings is a far better accuracy signal than any single run’s residual.

Awkward IMUs

Section titled “Awkward IMUs”

Two options help with accelerometers you cannot trust.

Gyro-only: calibrate an extra IMU from its gyro alone.

  • Solved: extrinsic rotation, td, Dw, gyro bias.
  • Pinned: lever arm, Da, Tg.
  • Order: entries match imu_topics by order.
  • Not on the base IMU: the base needs its accelerometer for gravity and metric scale.

Estimate t_ga: solve each IMU’s internal delay between gyroscope and accelerometer. It is on in our config:

Both options target the same failure. A driver that interpolates the accelerometer to the gyro rate adds a timing offset. Without t_ga, that offset leaks into td.

The T265 is the clearest case:

  • IMU: Bosch BMI055, 62.5 Hz accel interpolated to 200 Hz.
  • Measured: t_ga −6.74 ms on bag 01, −6.698 ms median over 15 bags (above).
  • Not a pure shift: the delay changes with the motion, so one t_ga is a compromise.

Details in IMU model. Why it matters, with real numbers: Why calibrate t_ga.
