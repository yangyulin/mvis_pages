Record the dataset

Step 2 of the walkthrough. Here you record takes the solver can use.

  • You need: the board from step 1, set up and measured.
  • You get: one bag (recording file) per take. Step 3 calibrates the cameras from it.
  • Check: each take passes the rules in section 1, and the solve in steps 3 and 4 converges.

A calibration can only find what the motion shows it. The 60 s motion below turns the rig about every axis and sweeps the board across the whole image.

We built it from a real hand-held recording with our Looper Insight 9. Then we checked it on a digital twin: a simulated copy of that rig, with realistic noise, which we record, solve and compare with the known truth. A motion is good if the twin still converges on every calibration parameter, that is, the solve settles on the true value within its uncertainty.

1. What a good take has

Section titled “1. What a good take has”
  • About 60 s long. 50 s also works.
  • Fill the frame. Cover the image out to its corners. The lens distortion is only pinned down where the board was seen.
  • Vary distance and angle. Move in and out, and turn the rig about every axis.
  • No motion blur. Use enough light for a short exposure (about 2 ms).
  • Rest at both ends. Hold still for about 3 s at the start and at the end.

3. Step by step

Section titled “3. Step by step”

Do the moves in order and blend each into the next. Keep a comfortable hand pace: at most about 0.4 m/s and 1.9 rad/s.

#MoveTimeWhat to do
0Rest0–3 sHold still in front of the board and count to 3.
1Left–right + turn3–20 sSwing left and right round the board, 2 times. At each end, turn the camera a little past facing the board, so the board slides to the left or right edge of the picture. Turn round sharply.
2Up–down + tilt20–32 sMove up and down, 2 times. Tilt the camera a little past the board, so the board reaches the top or bottom edge of the picture.
3In–out + twist32–47 sMove in to 0.22 m and out to 0.52 m, 3 times. Twist the camera about its view axis like a steering wheel: one way coming in, the other way going out. Hold the board off-centre, so the twist carries it into the corners.
4Loop + twist47–57 sMove round a loop parallel to the board, about 43 cm across, 2 times. Turn the camera a little against the loop and twist it a little, once per loop, so the board runs into every corner.
5Rest57–60 sHold still and count to 3.
  • Vary the speed on every sweep: push through the middle, brake into the ends.
  • Keep most of the board in view. An AprilGrid may run partly off the edge; a chessboard may not.
  • Do not add shake. The natural wobble of a hand is all the motion needs.

Bag 09 starts one frame early

In 4imus_4cams_09.bag the right T265 fisheye starts one frame before the left one. MVIS and Basalt pair frames by the nearest stamp, so they handle it. Kalibr reads each camera on its own, so its pairs come out one frame off. Give Kalibr a trimmed copy of the bag.

5. Record and check

Section titled “5. Record and check”
  1. Record the 60 s motion with your rig. For 50 s, drop one in–out and one loop.
  2. Calibrate the cameras with the board block from step 1: step 3: camera-only calibration.
  3. Calibrate cameras and IMUs together and read the result: step 4: camera–IMU calibration.

Motions that fail

Section titled “Motions that fail”

Each of these leaves some parameters loose: the solve cannot pin them down.

  • Fast shaking from one spot, face-on: the lens distortion stays loose.
  • One slow axis at a time, never tilted: the lever arm (where the camera sits relative to the IMU), the time offsets and the IMU scales stay loose.
  • A missing rotation (no tilt, no twist, or only turning left–right): the matching gyroscope and accelerometer scales, or the whole camera–IMU rotation, cannot be found.
  • The board always in the centre: the image edges are never seen, so the distortion stays loose.

How we judged it

Section titled “How we judged it”

The twin is a simulated Looper Insight 9 in front of the board, with realistic corner and IMU noise, motion blur and dropped detections.

We solved each motion on 3 noise draws and once without noise. Each solve estimated t_ga (the time offset between the gyroscope and the accelerometer) and left the camera intrinsics (focal length, image centre and lens distortion) free.

A motion is good if, on all 22 parameters:

  • every solve converges;
  • every error stays within its uncertainty;
  • every uncertainty is at most 3 × that of a well-excited 80 s reference motion.

The 22 parameters are the camera–IMU rotation and lever arm, td (the camera–IMU time offset), t_ga, the gyroscope and accelerometer scales, and the camera intrinsics.

To run the same kind of check on your own calibration, see step 7: digital-twin check.

Replay it yourself

Section titled “Replay it yourself”

Run our motions through your own simulator and calibration tool with the replay pack (zip, 3.2 MB). It holds:

  • the four motions at 200 Hz: IMU pose, ideal gyroscope and accelerometer;
  • the two twin rigs: camera, IMU, truth, noise and detection rules;
  • our twin results, with the pass rule.

Its README gives the frames, units and replay steps. The files are plain CSV, YAML and JSON, so you do not need MVIS to use them. We ran the twin with the MVIS simulator (mvis.simulation).
